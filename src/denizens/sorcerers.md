## Who They Are

Sorcery is not inherited and it is not innate. You go and get it, through a hazardous ritual that not everyone survives. What the ritual grants is perception rather than raw power: an intuitive grasp of the framework holding the mended cosmos together, and the methods to alter how it runs.

The Sundering did not leave the multiverse undamaged. Something patched it. Sorcerers call that something the **God Machine**, and they treat it as a vast and incomprehensible occult framework to be used rather than worshipped. Learning to use it is the hard part.

<div class="callout">
<strong>A word about what you say out loud.</strong> "The God Machine" is internal Sorcerer language and internal Sorcerer cosmology. What the Basilicas know is that sorcerers undergo a rite in dangerous places, that they come back marked, and that afterwards they can intuit the meaning of arcane formulas that are squiggles to anyone else. Public and guild language prefers <em>the Works</em>, <em>the Mechanism</em>, or simply <em>the Rite</em>. A Sorcerer who lectures a mortal about the God Machine is making a choice, and it is usually a bad one.
</div>

### Why People Trust You

This is the practical difference between you and a Metahuman, and it shapes almost everything about how your character moves through the world.

Sorcery looks like engineering because it is engineering. It takes time. It needs materials. It produces a written artefact, pages of glyph-and-geometry notation that can be filed, audited, licensed and taught. It fails in ways that can be explained afterwards. And it built the world people live in: every piece of paratech in every Basilica descends from Artificer workings, which is why 115 TE is a date schoolchildren learn, and why the Bengaluru guilds' Glyphcode Standards are a shared professional notation instead of an occult secret.

You are a licensed professional in a dangerous trade. People react to you roughly the way they react to a stormchaser or a reactor engineer, not the way they react to a monster. You face no conscription risk. No surviving statute persecutes you. You can practise openly with ordinary professional discretion. That is an enormous advantage, and you should play it like one.

## The Sorcerer's Road

The path to become a sorcerer is a very long one which begins between 15–17 (location depending) when one enters an academy for the occult arts. The next decade is spent learning the complex glyphcode notation, the basics of paratech theory and mechanics. Only those who pass a series of hidden tests over the course of their studies are deemed qualified with the right attributes needed for a favorable chance at surviving the Rite of the Ineffable Truth and becoming a full-fledged sorcerer. This approval places the initiate on a secondary path of higher arcane learning, and only opens once the candidate reaches the age of 25.

## The Rite of Ineffable Truth

You ultimately do not get sorcery by studying. You get it by witnessing the mechanisms beneath the world's skin and surviving with your mind mostly intact.

The Rite displaces you. For a moment you are somewhere else, most likely the Ineffable Halls, where a sudden extradimensional intruder receives the Mechanism's full and undivided attention for one terrible millisecond.

In that moment you witness the Ineffable Truth. All the knowledge of the multiverse unspools in front of you, consensus reality buckles, and a frail mortal mind collapses under the weight of the impossible things underneath it. When you come to, you find you carried a tiny piece of the Truth back with you, and out of that fragment come the eldritch instincts you need to work the Occult Arts.

Most candidates survive. Not all of them. New Tenochtitlan's Academies claim the best attrition rate in the world and decline to publish the number.

### What It Costs

Everyone who comes back with their mind intact pays the same two prices.

The first is **Eldritch 1**. You saw what the arcane equations describe, and you begin play with a point of Eldritch that permanently reduces both your maximum and your current Stability. Nobody else in the setting starts with any. You did not buy this as a flaw. It is the entry fee, and it means every Sorcerer sits one point closer to coming apart than an equivalent peer.

The second is a **Stigmata**. The Mechanism takes something, and almost always it takes a piece of you and puts a piece of itself back in the gap. Plenty of practitioners hold that this makes you a node of the machine, and that it may be shaping decisions you experience as freely your own. Few care to sit with that idea for long. See the [Creation Guide](/character-creation/makingsorcerer/) for the full Stigmata table.

## Mana Sight

This is what the Rite actually grants. It is not an Art, you cannot buy it, raise it or lose it. Every Sorcerer has it.

Your vision captures the shift and strain of raw mana: its currents, tension, glyphic architecture, and the fractured seams where reality was stitched together. Strands of planar force and eldritch geometry flicker through your sight, at times revealing truths better left unseen.

**What it gets you:** +1 die on any roll to read, analyse, repair, defeat or forge paratech, glyphwork, wards, or an active magical working. Mundane rolls only, never an Art activation. You also recognise another Sorcerer's Stigmata and School on sight, automatically.

**What it costs you:** there is no off switch. Somewhere dense with working — a reactor undercroft, a hearthstone gallery, a wyrdstorm, the Ruby Coast — the noise imposes **+1 difficulty on all rolls, Art activations included**. Difficulty still caps at 10, and where the noise would result in an impossible roll difficulty it cannot be attempted.

Your notation is private. Arcane equations are pages of glyphs and geometry, unintelligible to anyone without Mana Sight. Another Sorcerer reads them at professional speed whatever their School. Nobody else can — to everyone else, it is irrational squiggles, which is why nobody has ever successfully prosecuted a sorcerer on the basis of their own notes.

## Where You Stand With the Law

You are Nightfolk, and you register under the **Pax Nocturnorum** like anyone else. For a Sorcerer this is routine, uncontroversial, and mostly an administrative burden. Registration is a licence, and licences are how the trade works.

Sorcery in the Basilicas comes with paperwork. Bengaluru guild papers, a New Tenochtitlan Academy diploma, a La Paz Witches' Market stall licence, a Promethean Institute fellowship, an Artificer's guild seal: your character should hold at least one of these, or have a good reason not to. Backgrounds like Resources, Contacts, Library and Mentor are easy to justify here in a way they are not for Metahumans.

The line that divides the law-abiding sorcerer from the renegade warlock with a writ for their execution is **using their magic to provide an inhuman advantage over their fellow man**. Fascination on the unaware, a curse laid on a rival: those are Pax breaches, they go to the Order of Vigiles, and a confirmed breach ends in a writ.

<div class="callout">
<strong>Binding, and why it is the licensed act.</strong> Compelling an incorporeal being, a ghost or a spirit or worse, to service, silence or a place is the one Working the Basilicas regulate as an act rather than as a profession, because the thing you bind has interests and sometimes relatives. Every Basilica wants a filed writ before an Ossian settles a haunting or a Wayfarer pins an awakened object to a duty. Doing it without the writ gets you the case file rather than a fine.
</div>

In Purgatory, the Promethean Institute is your natural home and employer. The Sovereign Club funds Artificers and expects return on their investment. The Circle of Bone & Ivy needs Ossians badly enough to overlook most things. The Ashen Court pays better than any of them and asks for worse, and oftentimes illicit, feats.

### Traveller's Advisory: Salamanca

Sorcery is highly illegal in Salamanca outside the church-sanctioned Teúrgo. There is no visitor's exemption, and no guild paper from anywhere means anything on the meseta. You cannot hide what you are: the Stigmata is visible, and the Society of Leopold is trained to read them. Practising is prosecuted as heresy, and the trial is not the part to worry about. A Sorcerer who enters Salamanca is a Teúrgo, extremely well hidden, or a short story.

## Where You Meet the Other Splats

**Psi is barred**, in both directions, at any price. An exception exists only where staff have written a numbered register entry naming the circumstance, the cost and the approving authority, and no such entry exists. Until one does there is no route, and no ruling at the table creates one.

**Paratech prosthesis sits well in you.** The Rite already seated leystone in your body, so your flesh tolerates installed paratech that rejects, drifts or malfunctions in anyone else. Sorcerers are the only splat for whom heavy augmetics carry no cost at all, which is one reason so many paratech engineers are sorcerers and so many sorcerers are paratech engineers.

**Ghouling** — see the Lilum [Vitae & the Cost of Immortality](/denizens/kindred/vitaeandcost/) page for details.

**Embrace, the Dreaming, the Hungry Dead.** If you are Embraced, taken as Lost, or rise as Hungry Dead, you lose every Path and your Mana pool and are reborn fully as your new type. **The Stigmata remains. Eldritch remains.** There is no hybrid, and a vampire with a brass arm and Eldritch 2 and no magic is one of the more tragic things this setting produces.

**Being fed upon** costs you Essence directly, and a Sorcerer at 0 Mana is in Attrition, which is not immediately dangerous to the predator. That is exactly why Sorcerers are a popular meal. You do not pay Essence to heal, so you escape the worst of it, but a Sorcerer drained empty is a Sorcerer who cannot ward the door.

**Awakened mages.** You are not Awakened, you cannot become Awakened, and you are extremely tired of the question. Professionally, most practitioners hold that whatever the Awakened were doing, it was not engineering. A minority hold quietly that the Awakened were reading the same notation without the Rite, and that this is exactly why the world needed patching.

## What You Are Good At, and What It Costs You

Your first strength is acceptance. Sorcerers are socially accepted, professionally licensed and economically necessary. Your work is visible and auditable, and it is the foundation of paratech. You can practise openly with ordinary discretion, hold licensed employment, and expect favorable reception in any Basilica but one.

Your second is Mana Sight. You read the infrastructure of the world, and your notation is a private language no outsider can decode.

Against that, nothing you do is fast. Every Working needs notation, components and time. A first-dot Path takes an action and the good ones take minutes to hours. A Sorcerer caught unprepared is a mortal with a brass arm. What divides a good sorcerer from a dead one is the ability to discern in the morning what the evening will require.

And the price is on your sheet. The Stigmata is permanent, unhealable, unhideable. Eldritch is something you start with and something staff will keep awarding as you advance. Sorcerers are the least stable splat in the setting by design: alone among the splats they always open with an Eldritch point already spent, a permanent Stability point few others pay, and it puts an unspent-freebie Sorcerer into *Slipping* on day one. Every Sorcerer who reaches for the edge of the discipline pays again, because the invention gate means writing new magic at the top of your range costs mind, in increments you chose. None of it reverses.

## The Six Schools

A School represents a specific magical paradigm and personal philosophy. The New Tenochtitlan Academies teach all six, the guilds of Bengaluru and Bogotá are dominated by one apiece, and the Teúrgo of Salamanca insist they are none of them.

Each School is a single Path of five dots with three named practices, called **Traditions**. At creation, along with one of the Common Paths, you declare one Tradition, and it is permanent.

| School | Traditions | Attribute Priority | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Artificers](/denizens/sorcerers/artificers/)** | Alchemy, Enchanting, Leycraft | Mental / Physical / Social | The Path of the Forge — materials and manufacture |
| **[Augurs](/denizens/sorcerers/augurs/)** | Divination, Precognition, Fortune | Mental / Social / Physical | The Path of Consequence — reading the load on the system |
| **[Druids](/denizens/sorcerers/druids/)** | Healing, Purification, Greenwork | Social / Physical / Mental | The Path of Verdure — environmental and biological systems |
| **[Ossians](/denizens/sorcerers/ossians/)** | Mediumship, Necronics, Necromancy | Mental / Social / Physical | The Path of the Bone Ledger — the death-substrate branch |
| **[Wayfarers](/denizens/sorcerers/wayfarers/)** | Conveyance, Fascination, Spirit Awakening | Social / Mental / Physical | The Path of the Open Road — transit and interface |
| **[Fury Shapers](/denizens/sorcerers/fury-shapers/)** | Hellfire, Shadow Casting, Stormcalling | Physical / Social / Mental | The Path of Rage — the energy-release branch |
