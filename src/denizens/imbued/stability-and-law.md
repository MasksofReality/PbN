## Stability

Everything in the core rules applies. Permanent Stability is Conscience + Self-Control minus Eldritch. You start at Eldritch 0 and cannot buy Eldritch at creation.

Two triggers are added on top of the standard list. Mark if, this scene, your character:

* Refused or abandoned the Edict's compulsion when it was plainly in front of them, or
* Obeyed the Edict's compulsion against their own judgment, and somebody was harmed by it.

### Eldritch

Imbued accrue Eldritch faster than anyone except Sorcerers, and unlike Sorcerers you get no gate benefit from it whatsoever. The award condition is narrow: the Storyteller gives 1 Eldritch when you have acted on something the Sight showed you that no scholarship could have told you, and the acting mattered. As a guide rather than a rule, no faster than roughly a point every several months of play, and the Storyteller should say up front when a scene is a candidate.

Every point permanently reduces both your maximum and your current Stability. You do not get it back while the knowledge remains, and the knowledge does not leave.

The career has a shape and the shape is downward. A character who works for a decade and sees what the work shows them ends at a low permanent Stability with no route back above it. That is not a balance problem waiting to be fixed. It is what the splat is for.

### The Instruction

Once per lunar month, standing in for the first Breaking roll of that month and stacking with nothing else.

At 0 current Stability, when a further trigger would force the Conscience roll, you may instead accept an Instruction. The Conscience roll does not happen, and your current Stability resets to your permanent rating.

The Instruction is specific, written by the Storyteller, and not negotiable. It names a thing to do and a deadline, and the deadline is never longer than a lunar month. It is not always monstrous. It is never explained.

Carry it out and nothing further happens; the month's stand-in is spent. Refuse it or fail it and the Conscience roll does not come back for you. You lose 1 permanent Stability and take a lasting derangement, immediately, as though you had botched the Breaking roll. Accepting an Instruction is logged toward Requisition.

**Cathartic Vice** is chosen at creation as it is for everyone. *Cruelty to Those Who Deserve It* is on the core list and it is the obvious Imbued pick.

## Dying

You have the standard seven Health levels and no second track. Wound penalties apply exactly as the core rules state, Sacred Art activations included.

Soak is Stamina + armour against bashing and lethal at difficulty 6, and Stamina alone against aggravated. Art damage stays unsoaked whatever its type, unless the Art produces an ordinary physical effect.

Healing is the mortal table, both columns, unmodified. Bashing at a level an hour. Lethal and aggravated at the mortal rate, which is a level a day at best and a level every two months once you are Incapacitated. Nothing supernatural reaches you. Mortal medicine, rest and time are the whole of it.

Past Incapacitated, further lethal or aggravated kills you the way it kills any mortal. Endure and Finish It change when you fall over, not whether you die.

### Three Endings

**Death.** You die the way anyone else dies. What follows is irregular, and the Restless Dead have noticed. Imbued dead do not reach the soul rivers, and no shades have ever been proven to have been an Imbued in life. No enclave in the Shadowlands has an explanation anybody believes.

**Silence.** Everything stops. You diminish back into the powerless mundane you once were, now blind and with a long enemy list you have no means of fighting anymore. This is the secret fear of every Imbued, though one few meet, as it requires revoking your edict out loud and doing its exact opposite to cause it to happen.

**Recalled.** At permanent Stability 0 the Imbued simply vanishes. Where they go, or if they are even alive, is unknown. As it is believed the force that made them is the one taking them away, it is commonly known as being *Recalled*.

## The Vigil Codicil, and the Law

You are not a BRD. You hold a separate instrument.

The Codicil was signed in 183 TE as an annex to the Pax Nocturnorum, three years after the treaty, when the first Imbued to be positively identified under registration law produced a problem nobody had a box for. A BRD is a deviant: a citizen whose nature is inconvenient. An Imbued is a citizen taking instructions from a power that was not at the table and does not answer summonses. The League would not put that under the same registration and the Shadow Directorate would not put it under any registration at all. The Codicil was the compromise, and everybody signed it holding their nose.

Your status is Warranted, administered by the Order of Vigiles, who did not want the job and inherited it partly because of a naming coincidence that both bodies have stopped finding funny. A Warrant names you, your Calling, and the scope you are sanctioned to act within.

**What it buys you:** legal existence first, since an Imbued without a Warrant is prosecuted as an unregistered Nightfolk. Then evidentiary weight, because a Warranted Imbued's attested perception is admissible in a Vigiles inquiry, and your word alone can open an investigation. That is a great deal of power and it is the single most resented clause in the document. Last, standing to act under a writ of execution without holding a guild licence.

**What it costs you:** the Warrant is revocable at the discretion of the issuing chapter, without cause, without inquiry and without appeal. Revoking BRD status requires a confirmed breach and an investigation; revoking yours requires an officer's signature. You traded due process for legal existence and every Imbued alive knows it.

It also obliges you to report any Instruction you receive. Most Imbued do not. Concealing an Instruction is grounds for revocation, and it is also, in practice, the single most common thing an Imbued lies about. Renewal is annual and in person, at the issuing chapter.

## Where You Cross Other Kinds

The sorcery and psychic bar does not engage, because Sacred Arts are neither. You cannot awaken, you cannot become Restless Dead, and you cannot be ghouled: vitae does not sit in you. That last one is not the agony clause that applies to sorcerers and psychics, which is a scene with a bad outcome. For an Imbued, nothing happens at all.

A Mundane Human may become Imbued, with Storyteller approval and an event in the fiction. Never at creation on the mortal side, and never player-elected. Vitality is consumed and replaced by a Pneuma pool. The character takes a Calling, its Affinity Art at 2 and Discernment at 1, and gains the Sight and the anchored pattern in full. It is a change of splat rather than a crossing.
