You were taken.

Something reached into your life and lifted you out of it, and what came back was not what left. You spent a durance in a place with no calendar, serving a thing that wanted you for one particular quality and burned the rest away. Then you got out, or you were let out, or the door simply stopped being locked. You came home to a world that had gone on without you, and found something already sitting in your chair.

That is the short version. Every Lost has a longer one.

## A Note on Old Words

The Lost descend from older stories and older games, and some of the vocabulary has shifted. If you have played this splat elsewhere, three things will trip you:

**Your Arts are called Contracts.** Bargains struck with the Dreaming: power in exchange for cost. They are rated one to five dots, exactly like any other Art in the game.

**Glamour is your Resource Trait, and your Essence Pool is Glamour plus five.** In fiction, Lost say "Glamour" for both. On the sheet they are two numbers and only one of them is written down.

**Wyrd is a word, not a number.** There is no Wyrd rating on a Lost sheet. When a Lost says their Wyrd is rising, they mean their Glamour, and Glamour rises when their best Contract does. Wyrdstorms, the Wyrdborn, wyrd-twisted country — all of that is unchanged. It is a property of the world, not a stat.

Two more, briefly: Clarity is called Stability here, and the trait some books call Courage is Grit. Seeming Blessings and Curses are not purchases. They are imposed by the fiction. You cannot buy them, sell them, or get rid of them.

One thing that is genuinely different: the Veil, as older material uses it, does not exist. What exists is BRD registration and the Pax Nocturnorum. The Lost have a Mask; the League has a legal regime. They are not the same thing and they fail in different ways.

## What Every Lost Has

None of these cost anything. None of them can be bought, sold, or taken away. And none of them add dice to a Contract roll, because nothing does.

**The Mask.** Every Lost wears a human appearance over their true Mien. You do not switch it on. It is simply always there, and it is the only reason BRD registration is possible at all.

| Who | Sees Your Mien? |
|---|---|
| Ordinary mortal | No |
| Anyone at Eldritch 2 or higher | Yes |
| Anyone using a relevant Art | Yes |
| Another Lost | Always |
| Other Nightfolk below Eldritch 2, with no Art | No |
| Mundane cameras and recordings | No |
| Vigiles-grade sensory paratech | **Yes** |

That last line is the one that shapes Lost politics. Your Mask is worn over a mortal frame that is still, structurally, there, and a good enough instrument finds the seam. You pass BRD intake, and then you carry a standing annotation on your file forever. New Colombia's genealogists, Salamanca's Leopoldines and every Vigiles chapter on the League understand this in the abstract and hate it in the particular. It is a large part of why so many Lost end up on the Isle.

**Glamour Sense.** You perceive the presence and absence of Dreaming-stuff as a baseline sense. No roll, no cost. You can tell whether a place, a person or an object has been touched by the Dreaming, and you feel a Wyrdstorm coming before the instruments do. That last one is worth actual money to a stormchaser crew out of Ulaanbaatar, and every Lost in the trade knows it.

**Fae Healing.** You mend faster than a mortal and you can spend Essence to mend faster still. Full details on the [Glamour, Essence & Stability](/denizens/lost/glamour-and-stability/) page.

**Hedge-Born.** You can find and pass through an existing Hedge gate with no roll and no cost, and you can survive inside the Hedge indefinitely. Navigating it is a mundane matter: Wits + Survival, or Perception + Arcane, at difficulty 6, adjusted for how strange the stretch is. Making a way where no gate exists is a different thing entirely and needs the [Contracts of the Hedge](/denizens/lost/hedge-contracts/).

The Hedge charges everyone it does not recognise. Recognition comes from being native to the Dreaming, from holding Hedge-Born or its Dreamborn equivalent, or from being named aloud by a guide who holds one of those and can be heard doing it. Everybody else loses 1 Essence per scene inside — 1 Vitality if they are mortal — and takes a cumulative +1 difficulty on every roll, stacking each scene, until they leave.

The drain is the visible half. What the Hedge actually does to an unrecognised traveller is wear at who they are: a fog of forgetfulness that thins identity by degrees. Plenty have lain down in the moss and not got up. If you are guiding people, narrate the toll as forgetting rather than as tiredness, whatever the sheet records.

On the Wandering Isle this matters more than it does anywhere else on Earth. The Bitterthorn bleeds into the Dreaming in dozens of places, the Ghostlit ruins open at night, Blackwind Valley is barely a Hedge so much as the Dreaming with a coastline, and the Mistcrown interior has defeated every expedition ever mounted. A Lost is the only reliable guide any expedition into that country will get. Guides are hired, well paid, and frequently not returned.

**Dreamer's Distance.** Part of you is already somewhere else, and it is very hard to move something that is not entirely here. Any Art used to alter your memory, your identity, or your perception of reality is made at +1 difficulty, to a maximum of 9. It does not stack with itself, it does not scale, and it grants no dice.

**Keeper's Mark.** You kept a trace of your Keeper's nature: one small, specific, permanent affinity related to your durance. Write it on the sheet at creation and have your Mentor approve it. It is always minor and always real. A Lost kept by a Keeper of tides always knows the state of the tide. A Lost kept by a Keeper of clocks always knows the exact time. A Lost kept in a kitchen can name every ingredient in anything they taste. It grants no dice and no difficulty modifier, and if a proposed Mark would change a roll, it is too big.

## What Every Lost Carries

These are imposed the same way the strengths are. You cannot buy them away, negate them with a Merit, or override them with a Seeming Blessing.

### Cold Iron

Telluric or meteoric iron, worked by hand and never through a forge. The mythic poison of the Fae. In 200 TE it is rare, expensive, and held almost entirely by the Order of Vigiles, the chartered aberrant-hunter guilds and the Society of Leopold, which is the only reason any of you walk around at all.

Ordinarily nothing in this system makes a weapon more or less effective against Nightfolk. This is the exception, stated plainly:

- A cold iron weapon deals **aggravated** damage to a Lost, and aggravated is unsoakable. No Stamina, no armour. The damage pool itself is unchanged; a cold iron knife is still Strength + 1. Only the type changes.
- Sustained skin contact deals 1 bashing per round, and that bashing is unsoakable too.
- A Lost bound or worn in cold iron — manacles, a collar, a wire — cannot spend Essence and cannot activate any Contract at any rating, including the free ones. For the duration you are a mundane human with a strange face.
- Cold iron wounds cannot be closed with Essence at all. No price, no exception. A cold iron wound heals at the mortal rate or it does not heal. That is what makes cold iron the world's answer to a Fae rather than merely an expensive knife.
- A Lost killed by cold iron leaves an ordinary human corpse, which is precisely why the Society of Leopold prefers it.

A called shot at +2 difficulty is how a hunter puts cold iron against skin on purpose rather than by luck. Every guildhall in Nairobi teaches it.

### The Fetch

Every Lost who had a life before the Dreaming has a Fetch: a construct left in their place by their Keeper, carrying their memories and their name, living their life in their absence.

A Fetch is not a copy and not a twin. It is a thing that has been being you for years and has opinions about how you were doing it.

Meeting yours is a Stability trigger and one of the most destabilising things that can happen to a Lost. Storytellers may bring Fetch complications in at any time.

### Wyrdstorms

Inside an active Wyrdstorm you are empowered and unmoored at once.

Every Contract activation is at −1 difficulty for the duration. Treat 3 as the practical floor and do not stack it with more than one other reduction.

You also lose 1 current Stability per scene spent inside the storm. No roll, no trigger, no appeal.

Stormchaser crews pay Lost guides very well and lose them fairly often.

### The Keeper's Claim

A Keeper retains a spiritual claim on what they made. This does not mean yours is hunting you; most Keepers have other toys. But attention can be drawn — by an unusual Token, by a working that reaches deep, by a name spoken in the wrong place — and a Keeper whose attention has been drawn may try to reclaim what it thinks is its.

### Registered, Watched, Structurally Suspicious

Your Mask is a supernatural disguise that mortals cannot see through, and every authority on the League knows it. That is the whole of the political problem. Nothing about it is a mechanic. It is simply the water Lost characters swim in, and the reason so many of them end up somewhere the water is warmer.

### Living in a Stasis Field

A Hearthstone pins local reality to a fixed pattern so it cannot be renegotiated. Wyrdstorms slide off. Aberrants and hostile intrusions get turned back by the field rather than by any wall or watchman, which is why a village with a Minor Hearthstone and no fortifications is safer than a stone keep without one.

The field does not reject Nightfolk. Registered BRDs live inside Basilicas as a matter of ordinary law, and unregistered ones walk through the walls of every Basilica on the League without the stones taking any interest. What the field turns back is what comes at it.

Fae find the inside of a stasis field physically unpleasant. The standard comparison is a wool sweater against bare skin. It is constant, it is bearable, it does not fade with habituation, and it costs nothing on any sheet. No dice, no difficulty, no drain. It is just the texture of Fae life in the Third Era, and it is worth playing.

It also produces the central irony of that life. Glamour pools where humans congregate, and humans congregate almost exclusively inside stasis fields. Every Fae in a Basilica lives somewhere that chafes, because that is where the food is. It goes a long way toward explaining why the Fae population of any given Basilica runs thinner than its vampire or shade population, why so many Lost end up on the Wandering Isle, and why the City-Isle of Purgatory — which has no Hearthstone of any tier and never has — holds the Nightfolk population it does.

## The Six Seemings

Every Lost is one of the six shapes a Keeper carved a mortal into. Blessings and Curses come with the Seeming, cost nothing, and cannot be removed.

| Seeming | Nature | Attribute Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **[Beast](/denizens/lost/beast/)** | Those That Survive the Jungle Wilds · Wildlings | Mental / Physical / Social |
| **[Darkling](/denizens/lost/darkling/)** | They Who Creep in Shadows · Spooks | Mental / Physical / Social |
| **[Elemental](/denizens/lost/elemental/)** | Those That Are Prized and Adored · Puppets | Physical / Mental / Social |
| **[Fairest](/denizens/lost/fairest/)** | Those of Wicked Hearts and Wicked Minds · Thorns | Social / Mental / Physical |
| **[Ogre](/denizens/lost/ogre/)** | They Who Become the Monsters · Brutes | Physical / Social / Mental |
| **[Wizened](/denizens/lost/wizened/)** | They Who Live for the Task · Fix-its | Mental / Physical / Social |

Below Seeming sits **Kith**: the specific shape the Dreaming chose for you. A Beast is a Wolf-kith or a Corvid-kith. A Fairest is a Mirror-kith or a Bloom-kith. Kith is flavour — no dice, no difficulty modifier, no Contract access — and it shapes your Mien and how a Storyteller describes you when the Mask slips. Name your own and have your Mentor approve it.

## Who Else Lives in the Dreaming

The Lost are not alone in the Dreaming. The **Dreamborn** are Fae natives of the Hedge, a people rather than a rank, and a **[Rare, playable path](/denizens/lost/dreamborne/)** in their own right. The **Wyrdborn Gentry** are the godlings who did this to you — see [The Wyrdborn and the Houses](/denizens/lost/wyrdborn-and-houses/) for the Triune who govern the Wandering Isle and the Houses that offer a path to power outside your own Seeming.
