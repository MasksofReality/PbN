*They Who Become the Monsters · Brutes*

## Who They Are

The Brothers Grimm had one thing right: the stories are darker than the world lets on. Ogres know this personally. They were born of violence and harrowing circumstance, shaped by Keepers who wanted a monster they could point at things and say *hurt that*. The Dreaming made them into something terrible, and somewhere in the making, something happened the Keeper did not intend. The monster started thinking.

Ogres can be gentle. The gentlest of the giants hold something terrifying just under the surface, not because they want to frighten anyone but because that is simply what they are now. They know happiness sometimes requires heinous acts to protect. They know that never crossing an Ogre is genuine wisdom. They also know, more quietly, that they are still the person who was taken. They are still that person. They have just been made into something else as well.

**Blessing: The Strong.** Ogres are built like the titans they resemble, and built to keep standing. +1 die on all Strength-based rolls, including mundane damage pools, feats of strength and physical intimidation, and +1 die on all Stamina-based rolls, including soak. Mundane rolls only. Neither scales.

**Curse: Dim Wit.** Ogres are not unintelligent; many are very smart. They are easily swayed and slow to revise a read once it is formed. −2 dice on all Wits-based rolls. It does not modify initiative, which is a flat total rather than a dice pool. They see things clearly eventually. Just not always quickly enough.

## Contracts of Stone

**● Stone Skin** — *Free · reflexive*

The Ogre's skin hardens. +1 soak die against lethal for the rest of the scene, and it can be activated reflexively as a blow lands. At Glamour 2 it is a subtle change of texture, noticeable only to someone looking closely. At Glamour 5 it is visibly stone.

*Catch:* Taking a hit intended for someone else. (−1 difficulty; on a success the activation is automatic for the rest of the scene.)

**●● Mountain's Endurance** — *1 Essence*

The Ogre ignores all wound penalties for one scene and cannot be knocked down or staggered by any force short of a vehicle. They remain aware of every injury and simply do not let it slow them.

*Catch:* Protecting someone who cannot protect themselves.

**●●● Titanic Blow** — *2 Essence · once per scene*

The Ogre's next single physical strike is loaded with Glamour. +3 dice to the damage pool, rolled and soaked as an ordinary attack, and the blow breaks through barriers, walls and obstacles that would ordinarily need equipment.

*Catch:* Striking to break something that imprisons or traps somebody else.

**●●●● The Mountain Does Not Move** — *3 Essence*

For one scene the Ogre is anchored to reality itself. No force moves them against their will: telekinesis, supernatural strength, vehicles, environmental force, magical compulsion, all of it fails. They do not brace or plant. They simply do not move.

On top of that, any attack during the scene that does not deal aggravated damage is ignored outright. Not soaked, not reduced. It does not register.

At the end of the scene the Ogre must move, and it costs them: −1 die on all Physical pools for one scene while they work the stillness out of their joints.

*Catch:* Someone you are protecting is behind you, the threat is in front of you, and everyone in the scene knows it. You are the wall.

**●●●●● Living Fortress** — *5 Essence*

The Ogre's body becomes genuinely stone. Skin hardens to rock, wounds seal, and they are immune to all non-aggravated damage for one scene. In this state their unarmed attacks deal aggravated, they shatter stone walls and structural barriers, and they absorb environmental damage that would destroy anything softer. Movement slows: −2 dice on all Physical actions not based on raw strength or endurance.

When the scene ends the Ogre cracks back to flesh and every wound taken during it lands simultaneously. Roll Stamina at difficulty 8 or fall unconscious from the combined impact.

*Catch:* You activate this at 0 Essence, paying the Willpower and the physical toll of a Push with nothing in reserve. The fortress only fully forms when you have already given everything else.

## Contracts of Wrath

*Ogre retribution: direct, physical, earned.*

**● Mark of Fear** — *Free · Contested on Grit + highest Art*

The Ogre's presence becomes overtly threatening to one target, who is compelled to stay out of arm's reach for the scene. This is not terror. It is the primal recognition of something dangerous.

*Catch:* The target has already expressed fear of physical harm. (−1 difficulty.)

**●● Hex of Weakness** — *1 Essence · requires touch, Contested*

The Ogre lays a real Wyrd diminishment on a target they touch: −2 dice on all rolls using one Physical Attribute of the Ogre's choice, for the scene. Genuine weakening, not a perception of it.

*Catch:* Against someone who has harmed someone weaker than themselves in the last scene.

**●●● Shatter Confidence** — *2 Essence, Contested*

The curse strikes at the target's certainty in themselves. −2 dice on all rolls for one scene, and the target cannot spend Willpower to enhance a roll for the duration.

This is Wyrd-shaped doubt rather than mind control, which is why it works on beings that shrug off supernatural mental effects. Note that it does not prevent spending Willpower for anything other than enhancing a roll.

*Catch:* Against someone who genuinely believed they could not be harmed.

**●●●● The Weight of What You Did** — *3 Essence, Contested, Conditional*

The target begins carrying the weight of everyone they have hurt, and it is not a metaphor.

For the rest of the current scene they take −2 dice on all Physical actions. Thereafter the curse becomes a standing condition, **Bearing the Weight**, which does nothing at all until the target is in a scene with someone they have harmed. In any such scene the −2 applies from the moment that person is present, without a roll and without the Ogre being anywhere near it.

The condition lifts when the target makes genuine amends to someone they wronged, in a scene, at staff's call — or after thirty days, whichever comes first. There is no per-scene bookkeeping: one line on the sheet, and a trigger that either fires or does not.

They cannot sleep restfully. They know exactly what they are carrying and why.

This cannot be placed on a target who is not genuinely guilty of the harm in question. The Wyrd does not empower false accusation; against an innocent target the activation simply fails and the Essence is spent.

*Catch:* You personally experienced equivalent harm from this person. Not heard about it, not watched it happen to someone else. Carried it. The curse draws on what was done to you.

**●●●●● Unmaking** — *5 Essence, Contested, Resolved*

The Ogre unmakes one specific thing the target built with their own hands or their own work. You cannot unmake what they merely inherited or were given.

As with Ruin, the mechanical object is a Background, named at activation and destroyed in the next scene where it matters. The position is lost and every process that would restore it fails. The structure comes down. The reputation collapses and cannot be rebuilt in that community. The Background is struck from the sheet and its XP is credited back.

Where Ruin is a curse of consequence, Unmaking is a curse of authorship. The Ogre can only unmake what the target made, which is a real restriction. It is useless against inherited money, against a title someone was handed, against anything the target did not earn. Against a self-made person it is devastating, and that is the point.

Afterward the Ogre cannot use any Wrath Contract for one week.

**Cost: 1 current Stability, direct, no roll.**

*Catch:* The thing being unmade was used against you. The relationship they leveraged, the reputation they used to discredit you, the position they used to hurt you, the structure they built to hold you. You are not destroying something they love. You are dismantling the weapon they made out of it.

## Contracts of Fleeting

*Endurance as an act of will against impossible odds.*

**● Titan's Rush** — *Free*

The Ogre moves at double speed for one turn, closing or breaking away with the momentum of something much larger than they look. Unlike the Beast's Speed Burst, the Ogre's rush carries weight. Obstacles in the path get shouldered aside.

*Catch:* Closing distance to protect someone from an attack. (−1 difficulty.)

**●● Unstoppable** — *1 Essence*

For one scene, mundane obstruction fails against the Ogre. Doors, locks, barricades and terrain do not impede them. This does not make them faster. It makes them unobstructed.

*Catch:* The thing in your way is keeping you from someone you love.

**●●● The Long Fight** — *2 Essence*

For 24 hours the Ogre maintains any physical activity — combat, labour, running, climbing — without fatigue penalties. At the end they must eat, drink and rest at least four hours, or begin taking every suppressed penalty at once.

*Catch:* The effort is protecting a community rather than an individual.

**●●●● The Untiring Body** — *3 Essence*

For 24 hours the Ogre's body does not fatigue at all. Sleep deprivation, hunger and exertion produce no mechanical consequence. At the end, all of it lands: a 12-hour sleep they cannot be woken from without being dealt damage.

Mechanically identical to the Long Chase entry of the same name.

*Catch:* Someone who cannot keep up needs you to endure far past what is reasonable. The endurance only unlocks when it is entirely in service of someone else's need.

**●●●●● The Final Stand** — *5 Essence*

The Ogre declares a Final Stand and names its purpose aloud. For one scene, damage is tracked but never marked past Crippled, and the Ogre acts at full capacity regardless of what is accumulating. Every blow lands and every blow hurts. None of them puts the Ogre down.

When the scene ends, all suppressed damage applies at once. If that kills them, they die then. Not during, and not before the purpose is finished.

If the declared purpose is abandoned, the power ends immediately and the damage lands on the spot.

*Catch:* You are already at Incapacitated, or the blow that triggers this would take you there. The Final Stand answers when the body has already given out, and not before.
