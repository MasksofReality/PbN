## The Pool

Your Essence Pool is your Glamour plus five. Glamour climbs on its own to match your highest-rated Contract. You never buy it.

Because Glamour comes off your best Contract, that Contract always rolls double its own rating, and everything else rolls its own rating plus that same figure. A Fairest with Vainglory 5 and Mirror 2 rolls ten dice on the first and seven on the second.

## Midnight

At midnight server time you regain 5 Essence and your current Willpower refreshes.

This assumes you have had the ordinary opportunity to be near living feeling. A crowded street, a bar at closing, a market, a child laughing two floors up. Where a story has made that assumption a lie, midnight gives you nothing: a cell, a sensory-deprivation ward, a Leopoldine holding room, cold iron on your wrists, a stretch of the Dreadmire where nothing has felt anything in a century.

There is no condition that feeds a Lost when the assumption fails. Other Fae have one. You do not. A Lost held somewhere joyless simply does not eat, and it is one of the sharpest differences between the Lost and anything else living in the Dreaming: the Dreamborn always has somewhere to go, and you do not.

## Harvesting

The Lost's ordinary way of feeding is harvesting: taking emotion or wonder off a mortal who is already having it. One Essence per two post rounds, roleplayed, visible to anyone in the room.

It has a real texture. You draw on a feeling somebody is already carrying and you make a meal of it. Sharpening a joke until the room laughs harder than it meant to. Telling someone a story that frightens them beautifully. Standing close to a grieving stranger and letting the grief run through you. While it happens, the mortal feels it more, not less. Afterward comes the flatness. For an hour or so, the thing they felt is harder to reach.

Harvesting is not feeding. It takes no Vitality, deals no damage, adds nothing to anyone's cycle tally, and generates no Tether.

It can still be ugly. Manufacturing terror in somebody so you can eat it is a scene, and whether it is a scene your character would recognise themselves in is exactly the question Stability exists to ask.

## Draining

You can also just take it. Instead of drawing on a feeling somebody is having, you put a hand on them and pull the Glamour out of what they are. The difference between the two acts is roughly the difference between listening to someone and going through their pockets.

Draining is feeding in every respect. It runs on the standard escalation table, transfers at one point per Essence, inflicts 1 lethal per 2 points taken (1 per 3 from a genuinely willing donor), accumulates against the victim's cycle tally, takes Health levels past empty, and accrues Tether when the victim is Nightfolk. Being Lost makes none of that cheaper.

It needs sustained physical contact, so the chase, the grapple and the pin are ordinary combat. There is no roll to drain faster. There is only the decision to stay latched.

The price is Stability and it is not optional. Draining a mortal always marks *fed on someone who had no way to refuse*, because a mortal cannot meaningfully refuse a Lost and mortals always count as helpless. Take enough to wound and you mark *fed to serious injury* as well. Two triggers in one scene is a two-point loss if the Willpower roll fails, and it fires every single time.

**So why would anyone?** Harvesting is the same rate, costs the victim nothing, leaves no tally and no Tether. Draining is strictly worse, and that is the point. A Lost who drains is not doing it for efficiency. They are doing it because they are angry, or in a hurry, or because they have stopped seeing the person in front of them as a person. The sheet should be able to record that somebody made that choice.

## Pushing

You may pay 1 Willpower and 1 aggravated either in place of a Contract's Essence cost entirely, or to buy a single extra die.

Aggravated heals slowly and you cannot make it heal much faster, so buying dice this way is almost never right. Substituting for a capstone's five Essence at the moment it matters usually is.

Pushing only ever covers a base cost. Where a Contract charges an extra Essence surcharge on top, you pay that in Essence.

## Running Empty

At 0 Essence you cannot activate any Contract that costs anything. One-dot Contracts keep working.

At the end of every scene you spend at 0, roll permanent Willpower at difficulty 6. On a failure you become a conduit of the Dreaming for the rest of the scene. Your own fears and the fears of everyone near you leak out of you as waking nightmares: uncontrolled, unaimed, and thoroughly visible. On a botch it lasts until the next midnight and you get no further roll.

A Lost in that state is a BRD violation walking around. The nightmares are unmistakably supernatural, they happen in front of witnesses, and the Order of Vigiles has never once accepted "I was hungry" as a defence.

**The pull.** At 0 Essence you also feel the Hedge as a physical direction. The nearest thin place, the Bitterthorn briars, a Ghostlit ruin, a door that should not be there. Acting on it is a choice. Ignoring it is a difficulty your character feels in the scene, not a roll.

## Stability

Your permanent Stability is Conscience + Self-Control, minus any Eldritch you have picked up. Most Lost sit somewhere between four and eight. Current Stability starts at that maximum, falls through the game's standard trigger list, and comes back through Catharsis at one point per cycle.

### The Lost's Own Triggers

These are additional to the universal list, not instead of it. Mark one if your character, in this scene:

- Spent the whole scene inside the Hedge or the Dreaming without returning to the mortal world
- Was seen in their Mien by a mortal who did not already know what they were
- Broke a bargain they freely struck, or refused a debt they had acknowledged, including a Catch claimed in bad faith
- Was in the presence of their Fetch or their Keeper

### Losing and Gaining Directly

Two things move your current Stability without any roll at all, and both are losses. Standing inside an active Wyrdstorm costs one point per scene. And six Contracts charge a point in their own text: Unmake the Memory, The Perfect Stranger, Ruin, The Clean Slate, Living Legend, and Unmaking. Each says so where it lives.

One thing gives a point back.

**The Honoured Bargain.** Once per cycle, a Lost who genuinely met the Catch on a Contract of rating 4 or 5 restores 1 current Stability. No roll.

The Dreaming pays you a point of composure for a bargain honoured at the top of its own scale. That is the whole transaction. It does not stack with Catharsis: you restore one point per cycle from one source or the other, never both. It requires a capstone-tier Contract, which most Lost will not hold for a year of play. And it requires the Catch to have actually been met. The Catches at four and five dots are, without exception, conditions that cost you something real. Claiming one falsely is a trigger in its own right.

None of this touches permanent Stability.

### Mien Bleed

Your Mask holds as long as you do. As current Stability falls, the true face starts showing through.

| Current Stability | What the Mask Does |
|---|---|
| **8–10** | Nothing. Seamless. |
| **6–7** | Small wrongnesses at the edge of vision. A shadow with the wrong number of joints. A reflection half a second late. Mortals do not consciously register it. They just find you memorable in a way they cannot place. |
| **4–5** | The Mien bleeds through under stress. A mortal looking directly at you in a charged moment sees something. Whether they believe it is their problem. Whether they report it is yours. |
| **2–3** | Constant. The Mask holds in stillness and fails in motion. Living as a registered BRD is now a daily act of management, and your Vigiles file is getting longer. |
| **0–1** | The Mask fails outright in any moment of genuine emotion. You are what you are, in public, on the record. |

### Breaking, and the End

At 0 current Stability, Breaking works as it does for everyone. A further trigger forces Conscience at difficulty 6. A failure costs 1 permanent Stability and resets current to the new maximum. A botch adds a lasting derangement.

At permanent Stability 1, the Lost is **Taken Back**. The Dreaming finishes what the Keeper started. The Mask stops being a disguise and becomes the lie; the Seeming becomes the whole of the person; what is left is a creature of the Dreaming wearing a name it no longer answers to. That is a staff conversation, and the outcomes are the usual three: retirement, conversion to NPC, or a final scene of the player's choosing.

Your Mask is your anchor. It is the last piece of who you were before. Lose it and there is nothing underneath holding the shape.

### The Hollow Night

Once per lunar month, when a trigger would force a Breaking roll, you may instead go into the Hedge and lose yourself in it until dawn. You take the current Stability loss as normal. You do not roll Conscience and you do not risk permanent Stability.

The price is paid in play, and it is real.

You come back with a Stability trigger already marked, because you spent a scene wholly inside the Hedge, and it counts toward whatever scene you return into. More to the point, you are unreachable for a full in-character night. Not busy. Not slow to answer. Gone, with no way to be contacted, found, or brought back early by anyone short of a Wyrdborn.

That last clause is the whole cost in a persistent game. Take the Hollow Night on the wrong night and you miss the thing you were needed for, and the Dreaming does not care which night it was.

Note also what you are surrendering to. The Hedge is a place other things have standing in, and something with a Title can find you there.

Once per lunar month. Staff track it.

## Hurt, Healing and Dying

### The Track

Seven levels: Bruised (−0), Hurt (−1), Injured (−1), Wounded (−2), Mauled (−2), Crippled (−5), Incapacitated.

Elementals get eight. Their Lifebound Blessing adds a Battered (−0) level at the top. It does not scale with anything and it cannot be lost, sold, or bought a second time.

### Soak

Stamina + armour at difficulty 6. Aggravated is unsoakable, cold iron included. Damage from a Contract is unsoaked unless the Contract produced an ordinary physical thing: a Beast's claws soak, a Darkling's curse does not.

### Healing

| Damage | Free Rate | With Essence |
|---|---|---|
| Bashing | 1 level per hour of rest | 1 Essence: 1 level per turn |
| Lethal | 1 level per day of rest | 1 Essence: 1 level per scene |
| Aggravated | The mortal lethal rate, unaccelerated | 3 Essence + one full week's rest per level |

The Essence route for aggravated is slower than resting for the first two health levels and much faster for the last two. A Lost with a shattered leg should take the week. A Lost with one aggravated scratch should just rest.

Cold iron aggravated cannot be closed with Essence at any price.

And remember the trap: the resource that closes your wounds is the resource a predator just took. A Lost who has been drained and injured in the same night has a problem that compounds.

### Past Incapacitated

Further lethal or aggravated kills a Lost exactly the way it kills a mortal. There is no fae exception, no last-breath clause, and no Essence price that buys one.

What differs is the body. Your Mask fails at the moment of death, and within a scene the body collapses into whatever the Dreaming made of you: thorn, ash, brine, cold ember, a drift of moths. There is no corpse to autopsy and none to bury. Where a Lost falls, a Bitterthorn briar comes up inside a year, whatever the soil, and wherever in the world it happened. A briar in a Basilica courtyard is a Vigiles case file, a map of where a Lost died, and a standing invitation to whoever finds it first.

For the Vigiles the whole business is an evidentiary nightmare, and everyone involved knows it. It is exactly why an unsanctioned hunt that ends in a dead Lost is so hard to prosecute and so easy to allege.

Cold iron is the exception. Killed by cold iron, you leave a body.
