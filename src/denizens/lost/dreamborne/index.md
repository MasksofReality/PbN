## What You Are

Four kinds of things answer to the word Fae. The **Tuatha** are primordial expressions of the Wyrd, each one a facet of a single larger gem: unique, and never separate. The **Wyrdborn Gentry** are godlings native to Arcadia, fashioned by the Tuatha and fitted with Titles that bind and define their whole existence. The **Lost** are mortals abducted into Arcadia by the Gentry and remade there as servants and playthings, and Fae society treats them as its lowest caste.

You are none of those. You are Dreamborn, and you were never kidnapped, never fashioned, never handed a Title. You coalesced out of the Dreaming Shallows: centuries of folklore, shared nightmare, cultural memory and dramatic necessity, thickening until they had a body. A Gentry is defined by a Title that was made for them, plus whatever else they have bartered, stolen or won. You are defined by a story that humanity keeps telling, wearing the shape the telling gave you.

That dependence runs both ways, and it is the whole of your condition. Human imagination sustains you, so you cannot live in the alien depths of Arcadia, and you cannot survive humanity forgetting you. You go where stories are thick on the ground: the crowded Basilicas, the caravan fires along the Ulaanbaatar routes, the stubbornly medieval villages of the Grenveld, the Bitterthorn where the jungle bleeds into the Dreaming, and the bars of Purgatory where every third patron has a story about someone they should not have followed home.

<div class="callout">
<strong>One piece of gossip, before the rules start.</strong> Some Dreamborn insist, loudly and without a scrap of evidence, that the eldest of the Triune is one of theirs rather than a Gentry: a shapeshifter older than any Title, wearing oversight the way a Pooka wears a coat. The Triune do not comment. Neither, wisely, do the Vigiles.

## Building One: Four Choices

Your character comes out of four decisions. Only the first of them does heavy mechanical lifting at creation, in the way a Clan or a Tribe would.

Short version: the Fable is the story, the Aspects are the body, the Ban is the rule, and the Mien is whatever you want to see in the mirror.

| Layer | What It Is | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Fable** | The story you are: the dramatic role, not the species. | Sets your Aligned Motifs, your Boon, your Dramatic Law and your attribute priority. |
| **Mien** | What you look like when the Mask comes off. | Pure description, freely chosen. |
| **Aspects** | Two mythic traits that make the Mien real. | Each grants one Gift and one Frailty. |
| **Ban** | The folkloric rule that binds your kind of thing. | An absolute prohibition, and the handle mortals use on you. |

## The Mask and the Mien

You live behind two faces, and you will spend your existence managing the gap between them.

The **Mask** is the mundane human disguise that mortal eyes, sensory paratech and unawakened cameras see. It makes an eight-foot Champion read as a burly dockworker and a Sepulchral Keeper as an unhealthily pale archivist. It is an innate glamour rather than an active working: it costs nothing, it is always up, it cannot be dispelled, and a paratech aether sweep does not register it as a spell in progress. This is the single largest civil advantage your kind has, and every Dreamborn old enough to have opinions knows it.

The **Mien** is the true mythic shape of the story. Anyone with Eldritch 2 or higher sees it, as does anyone piercing the illusion with supernatural Arts. It is not a costume. It is what a hundred generations of frightened, delighted people agreed you look like, and you had no vote.

Here is the trap. The Mask covers your appearance. It does not cover your behaviour, and behaviour is what gets Dreamborn killed.

### Registration and the Pax Nocturnorum

You register as a Benign Reality Deviant under the Pax Nocturnorum, and you will pass intake more easily than almost any other Nightfolk. Your file is short, your aetheric signature is quiet, and you photograph beautifully.

Then a Wayfarer cannot decline a riddle from a Vigile at a checkpoint. A Firebrand cannot let an unjust order pass in his hearing. An Innocent lifts a foreman's watch in front of forty witnesses. Dramatic Laws and Bans are the classic breach, and every experienced Dreamborn in the Basilicas keeps a rehearsed strategy for the moment their story insists on being told in public. A confirmed breach revokes your BRD status and invites a writ of execution to the Aberrant Hunters.

The Order of Vigiles knows exactly what works on the Fae. Their restraint kits are cold iron, hand-forged in Salamanca and Tabriz at ruinous cost, and their intake questions are built to make you demonstrate a compulsion in front of a witness. Assume the officer across the table has read a folklore primer more recently than you have.
