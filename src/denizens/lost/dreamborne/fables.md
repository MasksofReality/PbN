Pick your Fable first. It is the story you are, and everything downstream hangs off it.

Each entry gives you four things:

- **Aligned Motifs.** Two of them. The first is your Affinity Motif, the identity choice the core rules make mandatory, and it must be one of the two Arts you start with.
- **Boon.** An innate advantage on the mundane sheet. A Boon never adds dice to a Motif roll.
- **Dramatic Law.** A Stigmata-class trait: the fiction assigns it, you pay nothing for it, and nothing removes it. Where a Law can be defied at all, the entry tells you what defiance costs. Defying your Law in a moment that matters always marks the Dreamborn Stability trigger.
- **Attributes.** Your 7/5/3 priority. Staff may approve swapping second and third where a concept plainly demands it. The primary is fixed.

On top of those two Aligned Motifs, every Dreamborn also carries the Motif of the Mask on the Aligned track.

## What a Boon Is Allowed to Be

Useful to know if you are weighing two Fables, and necessary to know if you are writing a new one. Every Boon here is worth exactly one of the following:

- +2 dice on a narrow mundane pool, gated by a stated circumstance;
- +1 die on a broad mundane pool, or on a defence, for people other than yourself;
- one capability with no dice attached: a thing that is simply true of you, such as not being surprised inside your own charge.

A Boon doing two of those jobs at once has been written wrong. Take it to staff for review.

## Dice Never Stack

Where a Boon, an Aspect Gift and a Motif would all add dice to the same roll, take the highest single bonus and ignore the rest. That is the whole rule, it is not negotiable, and it exists because the alternative is a Hunter with Ironbark and the Cut Trail rolling nine dice to follow a footprint.

Difficulty modifiers are a different animal. They are not dice, they do stack, and they stack up to the core cap of 10, past which the action is impossible rather than hard.

## 1. The Champion

*The one who stands and fights so others need not.*

Etin of the mythic wars. Valkyrie. Bogatyr. Oni duellist. The masked luchador of New Tenochtitlan whose bouts are older than the Basilica. The folk hero born out of the village's tavern tales.

- **Aligned:** Hunt (Affinity), Crown
- **Attributes:** 7 Physical / 5 Social / 3 Mental
- **Boon, Held Ground:** +2 dice on mundane rolls to hold a position against a superior force, or to fight a named opponent in single combat. It does nothing for a fight the Champion started at an advantage. No mundane threat moves them once they have accepted a fight, which is a fact about them rather than an immunity, and needs no roll from either side.
- **Law, The Answered Challenge:** a Champion cannot decline a challenge to single combat made openly, to their face, by someone willing to face them alone. Nor may they strike a foe who has not been offered the chance to yield. *Defiance:* spend 1 Willpower, and lose the use of your Affinity Motif for the rest of the scene.

## 2. The Sovereign

*The one whose word binds.*

Sidhe noble. Erlking. Yaksha-king. The hag-queen's disinherited cousin.

- **Aligned:** Crown (Affinity), Hearth
- **Attributes:** 7 Social / 5 Mental / 3 Physical
- **Boon, Unbowed Bearing:** +2 dice on mundane Social rolls to command, judge or overawe, but only while exercising or defending an authority you have actually claimed in the fiction, and never in service of seduction, deceit, or a request made as an equal.
- **Law, The Sovereign Word:** a Sovereign cannot break a promise given freely in their own voice. There is no roll and no defiance. The promise holds until the one it was given to releases them. Where circumstance makes a promise impossible to keep, mark the Stability trigger, and go without your Affinity Motif until you have made restitution in a played scene.

## 3. The Warden

*The one who guards the threshold.*

Alux of a tended field. Domovoi. Church grim. Gargoyle. Dvarapala. The shrine-thing at Chino that has eaten two burglars and one auditor.

- **Aligned:** Hearth (Affinity), Hunt
- **Attributes:** 7 Physical / 5 Mental / 3 Social
- **Boon, Ward of the Charge:** name a charge at creation, being a place no larger than a city block, a single person, or a single object. Within or beside that charge you cannot be surprised, and you know the moment anything crosses into it. No dice, no roll, and no exceptions for stealth, Arts or paratech.
- **Law, Bound Roots:** away from the charge your Motif activations suffer +1 difficulty, rising to +2 after a full cycle away. A Warden cannot knowingly permit harm to their charge in their presence. *Defiance:* none available.

## 4. The Hearthkeeper

*The one who takes you in.*

Huldrekin. Brownie. Tomte. Zashiki-warashi.

- **Aligned:** Hearth (Affinity), Forge
- **Attributes:** 7 Social / 5 Mental / 3 Physical
- **Boon, Sanctuary's Warmth:** inside a space you have claimed with The Warm Threshold, anyone who has accepted your food, drink or shelter gains +1 die on Willpower and Psyche rolls to resist fear, compulsion, frenzy or Attrition. It does nothing for you. The hearth protects guests.
- **Law, The Hollow Back:** a Hearthkeeper cannot refuse food, warmth or shelter to anyone who asks peacefully at their threshold, and cannot harm a guest who has eaten under their roof until that guest has left it. *Defiance:* none available. Forced to violate it, you mark the trigger and lose Sanctuary's Warmth until you have kept guest-right unbroken for a full cycle.

## 5. The Wayfarer

*The one who knows the road.*

Eshu. Rübezahl. The wandering monk of the high passes. The caravan-luck of Ulaanbaatar. The stranger at the crossroads who asks a fair price for a true direction.

- **Aligned:** Road (Affinity), Hollow
- **Attributes:** 7 Mental / 5 Physical / 3 Social
- **Boon, The Open Crossroad:** +2 dice on mundane Survival rolls, and on Perception rolls to read terrain or find a route, while outside the walls of a Basilica. Inside them it does nothing. A Wayfarer always knows the way they came, which takes no roll and is occasionally the only thing that saves their companions.
- **Law, The Wanderer's Debt:** a Wayfarer cannot refuse a fair trade of story, riddle or news offered by a stranger. Pass a full lunar month without once leaving the walls of the Basilica you are in and every roll suffers +1 difficulty until you do.

## 6. The Trickster

*The one who overturns by absurdity.*

Pooka. Kitsune. Coyote-shaped things. Till Eulenspiegel. Reynard. Whatever the Mallocat actually is.

- **Aligned:** Mask (Affinity), Misrule
- **Attributes:** 7 Social / 5 Mental / 3 Physical
- **Boon, The Sideways Truth:** +2 dice on mundane Subterfuge rolls to misdirect, while obeying your own Law, meaning the truth wrapped in a joke, a riddle, hyperbole or a lie. A flat, well-told lie gets nothing. Witnesses to the joke remember the joke far better than the face that told it.
- **Law, The Tangled Tongue:** a Trickster cannot deliver an important truth plainly. It has to arrive wrapped in a joke, a riddle, a wild exaggeration or an outright lie. *Defiance:* none available. The truth can always be got at. It simply cannot be handed over.

## 7. The Maker

*The one who builds impossible things.*

Menehune. Dwarf-smith. Tomte of the workshop. Wayland. Vishvakarma. The undocumented master artisan no Bengaluru paratech guild will out to the Vigiles.

- **Aligned:** Forge (Affinity), Hearth
- **Attributes:** 7 Mental / 5 Physical / 3 Social
- **Boon, Hand of the Craft:** +2 dice on mundane Crafts rolls, and on Technology rolls to repair paratech, given the time to do the work properly. Improvisation under fire gets nothing. A Maker needs neither light nor plans, ever.
- **Law, Sunlight's Cessation:** work left unfinished when the sun breaks cannot be touched again until nightfall, and forcing it warps the materials and ruins the piece. A Maker also cannot knowingly leave a flawed thing of their own making in someone else's hands, and must mend it or take it back. *Defiance:* spend 1 Willpower to walk away from a flaw for one scene.

## 8. The Keeper

*The one who knows what was buried.*

Sluagh. Banshee. Night-hag. Baba Yaga. Grave-warden. One of the Dreadmire thirteen, on a night when she is being pleasant.

- **Aligned:** Hollow (Affinity), Crown
- **Attributes:** 7 Mental / 5 Social / 3 Physical
- **Boon, The Long Ledger:** +2 dice on mundane Investigation and Arcane rolls concerning the dead, the buried, or the deliberately forgotten. It does not extend to the living, the current, or the merely private. Those are the Hollow's business, and they cost Glamour.
- **Law, Secret for Secret:** a Keeper cannot give away a secret except in trade for another of comparable weight, and cannot destroy a record, a testimony or a grave. *Defiance:* none available.

## 9. The Beloved

*The one who cannot be looked away from.*

Siren of the northwest reefs. Swan maiden. Selkie. Apsara. Incubus-shaped. The singer everyone in Purgatory has a story about and nobody has a photograph of.

- **Aligned:** Longing (Affinity), Hollow
- **Attributes:** 7 Social / 5 Physical / 3 Mental
- **Boon, The Turned Head:** +2 dice on mundane Social rolls to be believed, excused or forgiven by someone who could plausibly find you beautiful or pitiable. This is neither seduction nor command. It is the Boon of the one nobody wants to think ill of.
- **Law, The Unanswered Heart:** offered sincere devotion, whether a true gift, a confession, or their name spoken with feeling, the Beloved must acknowledge it and answer it within the scene, kindly or cruelly. They may not simply walk away untouched. *Defiance:* spend 1 Willpower.

## 10. The Reveller

*The one desire moves through.*

Satyr. Maenad. Clurichaun in his cups. The wine-yaksha. The drum-spirit that turns a Nairobi guildhall wake into a three-day festival.

- **Aligned:** Longing (Affinity), Road
- **Attributes:** 7 Social / 5 Physical / 3 Mental
- **Boon, Intoxicating Revelry:** +2 dice on mundane Performance rolls, and on Social rolls to keep people drinking, dancing and staying, inside a celebration you are part of. Loosening the tongues of the room is Revel's Contagion's job, and that costs Glamour.
- **Law, Thralldom to Passion:** confronted with immediate sensory indulgence, a Reveller spends 1 Willpower or partakes. They cannot be the first to leave a feast.

## 11. The Firebrand

*The one who will not kneel.*

Clurichaun. Prometheus-shaped. Robin-of-the-hedge. The strike-song they still sing in the Gold Reef deep shafts, which nobody wrote and everyone knows.

- **Aligned:** Misrule (Affinity), Crown
- **Attributes:** 7 Social / 5 Physical / 3 Mental
- **Boon, Stir the Cauldron:** +2 dice on mundane Leadership and Intimidation rolls to turn a crowd against someone holding authority over it. One person is not a crowd, and a crowd that already agrees with you is not a roll.
- **Law, The Quick Temper:** roll Self-Control at difficulty 7 to walk away from an open insult, an unjust order given in your hearing, or a fight you were invited into. Fail and you act.

## 12. The Innocent

*The one nobody suspects.*

Piskie. Kobold. The changeling child. The small thing at the shrine gate that answers questions in rhyme. A Grenveld hedge-child who has been nine years old for ninety years.

- **Aligned:** Misrule (Affinity), Hearth
- **Attributes:** 7 Physical / 5 Social / 3 Mental
- **Boon, Beneath Notice:** nobody reads an Innocent as the threat in the room. Hostile mundane characters do not act against you first while anyone else is present, and a witness recalling your distinguishing features must succeed on Perception + Alertness at +2 difficulty. No dice. It is simply how people are about children.
- **Law, The Magpie's Eye:** an Innocent cannot leave an unattended valuable where it lies. Roll Self-Control at difficulty 6 to refrain. Fail and you take it.

## 13. The Hunter

*The one who follows the trail to its end.*

Firbolg. The Wild Huntsman. Herne. Wolf-shaped things. The uncanny partner a Nairobi hunter-guild does not list on its roster. The shadow the Dusktooth raptors give way to.

- **Aligned:** Hunt (Affinity), Road
- **Attributes:** 7 Physical / 5 Mental / 3 Social
- **Boon, The Unbroken Trail:** mundane trickery cannot break a Hunter off a trail they have taken. False trails, doubling back, laid scents, borrowed clothes, a body double, a crowd walked through: none of it works, and no roll is made for it. Supernatural concealment still works, and paratech scent-scrubbing works if it is good enough to erase the trail rather than confuse it.
- **Law, The Hunt Unfinished:** once a Hunter has named a quarry aloud, they cannot abandon the pursuit while the trail is warm. It ends in a taking, or in a release freely granted by whoever set them on it. *Defiance:* spend 1 Willpower per scene of abandonment.

## 14. The Returner

*The one who has died before and will again.*

The corn-king cut down every harvest and mourned properly each time. The girl sacrificed to the sea who walks out of the tide every spring. The cat on its fourth life. The salamander the Gold Reef smelters leave bread for.

- **Aligned:** Elements (Affinity), Hollow
- **Attributes:** 7 Physical / 5 Social / 3 Mental
- **Boon, Died Before:** threats to your own life have no purchase. Mundane intimidation predicated on killing you fails without a roll on either side, and you always know, to the hour, how long you have been dead. This Boon is deliberately thin, because the Returner's budget is spent on the Pyre.
- **Law, The Appointed Fate:** a Returner cannot flee the death that belongs to their story. Where the manner of their own ending is present and offered, whether the pyre, the flood, the harvest blade or the long fall, they may not run from it and may not let another take their place in it. *Defiance:* spend 1 Willpower per scene of refusal.

### The Pyre

The rest of this splat is built on the risk of permanent death, and the Returner does not repeal that. What they buy is one reprieve per lunar month, at a price, under conditions a canny enemy can deliberately deny.

**When it triggers.** When you would be destroyed under the rules on [Glamour, Stability, Body & Creation](/denizens/lost/dreamborne/mechanics/), meaning a Guttering Returner reaching the midnight reset unaided, or any aggravated damage past Incapacitated, the Pyre may fire in place of destruction. Guttering resolves first, so a Returner whose friends save them has spent nothing.

**What it needs.** Your native element must take the body during or shortly after the scene: the fire consumes you, the water closes over you, the earth swallows you, the air scatters you. A body held away from the element does not come back, which covers a sealed vault, a Vigiles morgue drawer and a sorcerer's stasis field. Cold iron denies the Pyre absolutely, whether it dealt the wound or merely holds the remains. This is precisely why Hunters who have read the file bring shackles as well as guns.

**What returns.** At the next midnight reset you reconstitute whole within a mile of where you died, carrying nothing you owned. Health track clear. Glamour pool at 0, so you return in Attrition, and the first thing most Returners do on coming back is fail that roll in front of somebody. Current Stability at 0, under the core allowance for splat-defined movement of the current pool. Your permanent rating is untouched.

**What is lost.** No memory of the scene in which you died. You wake knowing only that you have been dead again, and roughly how long for.

**How often.** Once per lunar month. A second death inside the same month is a real one, and the Pyre does not fire twice.
