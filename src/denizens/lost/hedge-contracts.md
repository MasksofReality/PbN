The only teachers are Wyrdborn. No Lost can teach this tree, however high their rating, because the thing being taught is not a technique. It is a set of permissions, and only something with a Title can grant them. In practice that means a House, and it means the patron wants something. The gate here is not staff being cautious. It is the fiction being honest about who owns the doors.

**It costs a slot.** Three Seeming Contracts plus the Hedge is four Arts, which is the ceiling. A Hedge-walker will never hold another Seeming's tree.

**Why it exists.** Every splat on the game can point at what it does for the rest of the setting. The Garou fight what comes through. The Sorcerers built the Hearthstones. The Wizened make the tools. The Lost's answer is the Hedge. The Bitterthorn bleeds into the Dreaming in dozens of places, the Mistcrown interior has defeated multi-month expeditions, and the Isle's whole geography is a problem that a Hedge-walker at five solves and nobody else can. This is the tree that makes a Lost worth hiring.

*(A note on names: the Hedge, the Woods Between and the Dreaming Shallows are the same place — the outer fringe of the Dreaming, furthest from the Wyrd and nearest the spirit and mortal realms. Lost say the Hedge. Dreamborn say the Shallows. Both are right and neither should be corrected in play.)*

## ● Read the Thin Places *(Free)*

The Lost knows where the world wears through. Within roughly a mile they sense every Hedge gate: direction, distance, whether it stands open or shut, and a rough impression of what lies on the other side. They also know when a gate has opened nearby in the last day, and which way whatever came through was walking.

*Catch:* Standing on ground where a gate opened within the last day. (−1 difficulty.)

## ●● Open the Way *(1 Essence)*

Where the world is merely thin, the Lost makes a door. The gate is stable, obvious to anyone present, and holds for one scene or until the Lost closes it. Companions equal to net successes may pass through with them.

This is the difference between a Lost who can use the Hedge and a Lost who commands it. Hedge-Born lets you walk through a door that already exists. This one puts a door in a wall.

*Catch:* You are opening the way for someone fleeing something that will kill them.

## ●●● Guide *(2 Essence)*

The Lost takes a party across and the Hedge does not charge them for it. For the duration of a single crossing, every companion the Lost has claimed is exempt from the toll — no Essence or Vitality drain, no accumulating difficulty — and cannot be separated from the group by the Hedge's habit of quietly rearranging who is walking next to whom. Crossing time is halved.

Claimed companions must be named aloud at activation and must be able to hear it. The Hedge is very literal about who was invited.

*Catch:* You are guiding someone who trusted you with something they could not afford to lose.

## ●●●● The Straight Path *(3 Essence)*

The Hedge is not a distance. It is an argument about distance, and at this rating the Lost wins it.

A crossing that would take days takes a single scene, and the party emerges precisely where the Lost intended. Into a warded building, into the middle of the Bitterthorn, into the Inner Reaches that have defeated every expedition ever mounted. The Lost need not have been to the destination. They need only to have been told about it accurately, by someone who was there.

Two things it cannot reach. The Governor's Manor, ever, by any means. And the domain of a Wyrdborn who has not consented, which simply fails and wastes the Essence. Amaia's palace, Blackwind Valley and the Dreadmire are closed doors unless the thing that lives there says otherwise.

*Catch:* The destination is somewhere you were held during your durance.

## ●●●●● Treat With the Thorn *(5 Essence, Contested)*

The Lost compels a genuine bargain out of a being of the Dreaming: a hobgoblin, a briarfolk elder, a Fae-Hag of the Dreadmire, a Dreamborn of real standing. The being must hear the offer in full, may not answer with a deliberate falsehood, and must keep the terms if it accepts. It is under no compulsion to accept, and the ones worth bargaining with rarely accept the first offer.

Note the shape of that compulsion carefully. It cannot lie to you. It is not required to answer plainly. A Trickster forbidden from delivering an important truth plainly satisfies the Contract with a riddle. A Keeper who cannot give away a secret except in trade satisfies it by naming a price. A Dreamborn bound by its own word may still evade, riddle and refuse for as long as its wit holds out. The truth can always be got at. It cannot be handed over on demand.

More broadly: a Dreamborn's Ban and its Dramatic Law are older and deeper magic than any Contract, and no Contract at any rating overrides them. Where a Contract would force a character to violate something imposed on them by their own nature, the nature wins and the Contract achieves whatever it can achieve around it.

Wyrdborn are wholly immune. A Titled being does not treat under someone else's Contract, and attempting it is remembered.

The binding runs both ways, and that is not a drawback. It is the mechanism. The Lost is held to the terms with exactly the force the other party is. Breaking a Hedge-oath is a Stability trigger and costs 1 **permanent** Stability. No roll, no Conscience check, no appeal. The Dreaming does not forgive a bargain sworn in its own house, and a Lost who has broken one can be smelled by everything that lives there.

*Catch:* You are bargaining for someone else's life, and the thing you are putting on the table is your own.
