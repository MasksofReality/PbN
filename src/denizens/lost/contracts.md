A Contract is a bargain with the forces of the Dreaming: power in exchange for cost. Each is rated one to five dots, each level building on the one below.

## The Roll

**Contract rating + Glamour, difficulty 6.** Ones cancel successes. That is the whole system.

No Attribute contributes. No Merit, Blessing or trait of any kind adds a die. Anything that helps a Contract helps it from the difficulty side. Wound penalties apply exactly as they do to any other roll.

## Costs

| Rating | Essence |
|---|---|
| ● | Free |
| ●● | 1 |
| ●●● | 2 |
| ●●●● | 3 |
| ●●●●● | 5 |

One-dot Contracts are free, always. A Lost with Glamour 5 rolls six dice on something that costs nothing, which is why every one-dot entry in this document is written as reliable utility rather than a weak version of the tier above it.

## Contests

Where a Contract is contested, both sides roll at difficulty 6 and you read the acting player's successes minus the defender's.

A supernatural defender rolls permanent Willpower + their highest Art. Against fear, horror, or any compulsion to flee or yield, they roll Grit + highest Art instead. A mortal defends on permanent Willpower alone (Grit alone against fear), and only if they are actively and knowingly resisting. Against an NPC, use the standard tier subtraction.

**Helpless targets are not contested.** A Contract used on a mortal who is unaware, or who has no supernatural means of resistance, is uncontested. You roll and read the effect off raw successes. The price is a Stability trigger: *used an Art on a victim who is helpless or unaware*, and mortals always count as helpless. The power is free. The cost is what it does to you.

Where an entry below says **Contested**, the contest happens whether or not the target is helpless. That word is used sparingly and on purpose.

## Reading Effects

Where a Contract does not state its own scaling: one success achieves the stated effect, and each net success past the first extends it one step along whatever axis it has. Duration, range, targets, degree.

Where an entry does give bands, they run 1 / 2–3 / 4+ net successes, because no acting pool exceeds ten dice and anything keyed at five-plus does not happen against a peer.

## Damage

One health level on a successful roll, plus one more per net success past the first, unsoaked, of whatever type the Contract names. Where a Contract instead hands you a physical weapon — a Beast's claws, an Ogre's stone fists — that weapon works like any other weapon and gets soaked.

## The Catch

Every Contract level has a Catch: a specific narrative or social condition which, when genuinely met, waives that activation's Essence cost entirely.

It waives the cost and nothing else. Same roll, same difficulty, same effect. A Catch is not a bonus. It is a discount the Dreaming gives you for being honoured on its own terms.

Catches take shapes like these: using the Contract on someone who wronged you in the past day; performing a specific act, like whispering a secret or standing in shadow or going hungry; targeting someone who freely gave you a gift today; using the ability while genuinely afraid rather than tactically.

One-dot Contracts have no cost to waive, so their Catch reduces that activation's difficulty by 1 instead, floor of 3. Where a one-dot Contract is passive and rolls nothing at all, its Catch is pure flavour and the entry says so.

Three things to hold on to:

**Catches are meant to be met.** Storytellers should put Catch conditions within reach rather than hiding them. A Catch nobody can ever satisfy is a typo.

**Claiming one falsely is a Stability trigger.** The Dreaming notices. So does the table.

**Only one Catch per cycle gives Stability back,** and only at the top of the scale, through the Honoured Bargain. Every other Catch, at every other rating, pays out in Essence and nothing else.

## How Long Things Last

This is a persistent game, not a table. A month is not a sentence here. It is thirty days of somebody checking a modifier before every roll, in scenes the Lost who cast it will never be in.

So every lasting effect in this document is one of four shapes, and nothing else:

| Shape | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Resolved** | It happens in a scene and it is over. The consequences persist; the mechanic does not. |
| **Charged** | It carries a number of uses. Someone spends one to modify a named roll, then it is gone. |
| **Conditional** | A standing note on a sheet that does nothing until a stated trigger fires, then applies for that scene. |
| **Held** | A property of a place rather than a person. It stands while its keeper keeps it and lapses when they do not. Checked once per cycle. |

| Shape | Contracts |
|---|---|
| Resolved | Ruin · Unmaking |
| Charged | The Mark That Follows |
| Conditional | The Lingering Dark · The Weight of What You Did · Emotional Anchor |
| Held | Sanctuary (both trees) · The Living Home |

Nothing here puts an always-on modifier on another character across multiple scenes. That is deliberate.

## How Blessings and Curses Are Balanced

Blessings and Curses come with the Seeming. They cost nothing and nothing removes them.

Where both sides of a Seeming's pair are dice, the Blessing grants +2 dice and the Curse costs −2. Net zero. What differs between Seemings is where the dice sit and how often each side comes up. A Fairest's bonus fires in every conversation and their penalty in every deduction. An Ogre's fires in every fight and every misread.

Two Seemings sit outside the dice ledger:

**Beast pays in control rather than dice.** Mental Block can take a character out of a scene, which is worse than any dice penalty here, so the Beast's Blessing is deliberately the narrowest of the six: one Ability, plus abilities that produce story rather than successes.

**Darkling pays in situation.** Light Sensitive costs −2 dice, but only on attacks and only in strong light, and a Darkling standing in strong light has generally chosen badly. Their Blessing answers on the difficulty side instead, and in the circumstances Darklings actually operate in it is worth considerably more than two dice.

## Kith

Below Seeming sits Kith: the specific shape the Dreaming chose for you. A Beast is a Wolf-kith or a Corvid-kith or a Thing-With-Too-Many-Legs-kith. A Fairest is a Mirror-kith or a Bloom-kith. An Elemental of fire is an Ember-kith or a Forge-kith.

Kith is flavour. It has no dice, no difficulty modifier, no Contract access, and no place on any XP track. It shapes your Mien, it gives your character a word for what they are, and it tells a Storyteller how to describe you when the Mask slips. Name your own and have your Mentor approve it, the same way they approve a Keeper's Mark: quickly, and only to catch the one that is secretly asking for a mechanic.

If a proposed Kith would change a roll, it is not a Kith. It is a Merit, and it costs 2 XP a point like everything else.
