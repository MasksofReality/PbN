## Who Lives in the Dreaming

There are four kinds of Fae and one penal status.

**The Tuatha** are primordial expressions of the Wyrd. Sapient maelstroms of raw creative power, eldest children of the Wyrd, native to Everflux where nothing else survives. You will not meet one. If you do, it will not notice.

**The Wyrdborn Gentry** are godlings of Arcadia, each the living form of an abstract concept fixed by their Titles. Near-absolute within their own domains and considerably less so outside them. Your Keeper was one of these. The Basilican League calls them the Wyrdborn Gentry on paper; most people just say Wyrdborn.

**The Dreamborn** are Fae natives of the Hedge, coalesced from humanity's collective myth. A people, not a rank, and playable in their own right. If you want to play one, speak to a Mentor about the [Dreamborn application](/denizens/lost/dreamborne/).

**The Lost** are you. Mortals taken by the Gentry and remade.

**Hobgoblins** are a sentence rather than a birth. A Hob is a cursed Dreamborn: one who broke a law woven into some Gentry's Arcadian domain, and was transformed into a puppet that cannot act without its master's sanction. The laws are deliberately opaque. That is not an accident.

Lesser dreamthings are a separate matter again. The Shadow Wolves, Gloomdrakes and Vileshrikes of Blackwind Valley, the briarfolk who tend the Bitterthorn, the shadow-people of the Ghostlit ruins. Some are Hobbed Dreamborn. Most are simply fauna of a realm made of imagination.

**Where the Gentry came from is genuinely unresolved.** One account has them as the Wyrd's secondborn, siblings to the Tuatha rather than their offspring, with Titles conferred by an elder sibling's prerogative. The other has them fashioned by the Tuatha and given Titles at their making. The Gentry overwhelmingly prefer the first, for reasons that need no explanation to anyone who has met one. The Tuatha have never commented, and people are generally more worried about not being accidentally erased by their mere presence than about asking questions. Staff may rule either way for a specific Gentry in a specific plot.

What is not open to question: Titles can be bartered for, tricked out of their holders, taken by force from defeated peers, and handed down as reward or assignment. A Gentry holding several Titles holds several obligations with them.

## Where You Stand

**No Fae commands another without a Title.** Authority in the Dreaming runs on Titles and on nothing else. A Dreamborn cannot compel you, you cannot compel a Dreamborn, and neither of you has any legal standing over the other under the Pax Nocturnorum either.

What exists instead is a social hierarchy with real teeth and no enforcement. Many Dreaming natives, Dreamborn and Wyrdborn alike, draw a line between the True Fae, who were always Fae, and the Lost, who were made into it. The word most often used for a Lost — when no Lost is listening, and sometimes when one is — is *fake*. A Dreamborn who speaks of itself and the Gentry in the same breath and of the Lost as a separate and lesser thing is not making a category error. It is stating a prejudice its whole society holds.

The Lost do not accept the framing, and they are right not to. Nobody asked to be taken. Nobody consented to a durance. A Lost is not pretending at being Fae; a Lost is what the Gentry made and then discarded, which is a very different thing from pretending. Lost who have been on the Isle a while develop a repertoire of answers to this, ranging from the courteous to fisticuffs.

In play: deference is expected and never owed, refusing it is a social cost and never a mechanical one, and a Dreamborn leaning on you is a scene rather than a ruling.

**Arcadia.** For the Lost, Arcadia is where the durance happened. That is a separate horror and it needs no rules here.

## The Triune

Three Wyrdborn Gentry — land, sky and sea — granted oversight of the Wandering Isle by the Governor. They are the highest authority a Lost is ever likely to meet, and exactly one thing exceeds them: the Governor. Below them sits the Council of Six.

### The Mallocat — Land

The most dapper of gentlemen. Connoisseur of sweet things and proper behaviour, disarmingly friendly, and a shapeshifter many times older than the other two combined. Do not mistake the cat-shaped exterior for kindness. Cats are hunters. The most deceptively dangerous of the Three.

### The bana'Shee — Lord of the Wandering Skies

Lord of all deals and debts, the overseer of life and death, and once sole overseer of the whole Isle. He was demoted for a bargain struck with an infernal being, a bargain the Governor wound back time to undo. His dreams are true nightmares. Those who hold his attention receive harrowing dreams and the constant sense of being watched, and tend either to go slowly mad or to vanish from the Isle entirely. A good day is a day you do not encounter him.

### Amaia — Lady of the Wandering Tides

Born of a Tuatha Dé Danann and the sea itself. Siren of the Isle and proud of it, seated in a submerged palace of coral and marble off the Siren's Coast, and a regular enough presence in Purgatory. Her title was made from the bana'Shee's shattered one, which is the single subject she will not discuss. She receives all she desires; the Vigiles look the other way on what she eats, and nobody has been bold enough to ask whether that is respect or fear.

### The Laws of the Triune

Dreaming law. It binds the Lost of the Isle, and it is enforced by beings for whom *enforcement* and *appetite* are not always distinguishable.

1. Every Lost who does not willingly serve a Wyrdborn is under the Triune's protection. No Wyrdborn may compel such a Lost without the Triune's express permission.
2. A Lost who defies the Triune's word is held to be a threat to the survival of the Dreaming, and will be hunted.
3. No authority exceeds the Triune except the Governor.
4. Beings of Hellish origin are forbidden in the Dreaming. To bring one in is to accept extermination — and given what such a bargain cost the bana'Shee, this law is enforced with particular feeling.
5. Never jeopardise the safety of your brethren.

**Dreaming law is not Basilican law, and neither overrides the other.** Inside Purgatory's walls the Pax Nocturnorum governs. A Lost who kills at a Wyrdborn's instruction is still a BRD violation, the Order of Vigiles still investigates, and a writ of execution still goes to the hunters. The Council of Six does not recognise *my patron told me to* as a defence, and the Triune have never once intervened on behalf of a Lost who tried it.

That gap is where most Lost stories on the Isle actually live.

## The Houses

Within the Triune's court stand the Houses, each held by a Wyrdborn. A Lost loyal to a Wyrdborn is of that House. Membership carries no dice and no HUD effect, and changing Houses carries real social consequence.

What a House is worth mechanically: it is the route to a Discordant Contract. A patron willing to teach their Lost something their Seeming never gave them. The House's listed Contracts are what that patron can teach. Staff approval and the full 39-XP track still apply on top. The House gets you the teacher, not the discount.

Every House lord can also teach the Hedge, and since no Lost can teach that tree at all, a House is not merely the easiest route to it. It is the only one. What each lord charges differs enormously, and none of them charge in XP.

The Lore entries below are Arcane specialties, not separate Abilities.

### The House of Moonlight — Lord Aydan

A noble manor surrounded by star-filled water, built from moon-rock. Lord Aydan collects people and their stories and is particularly fascinated by humanity's fairytales. His Lost wear the tightest leashes inside his house and the loosest outside it, because Aydan is always watching and learning through those he has shaped in his image.

**Can teach:** Contracts of Mirror · Contracts of Vainglory
**Arcane specialties:** Dreaming history · Mortal fairytales

### The House of the Perfumed Reverie — Lord Phantasy

An androgynous figure of impossible symmetry, or perhaps only a silhouette of perfume and shadow. His domain is a greenhouse-palace that breathes: orchids sigh, roses weep nectar and heartache, and every chamber is saturated with a mood chosen before the visitor arrives. His Lost are either genuinely lost in his allure or walking the mortal world on his behalf. He never forgets them. He will never let them forget him.

**Can teach:** Contracts of Feeling · Contracts of Smoke
**Arcane specialties:** Emotional manipulation · Dreaming aesthetics

### The House of the Leopard's Bane — The Silken Lord

The house of poison, toxin, and equally toxic relationship. The Silken Lord loves everything that pollutes and corrupts mind and body, and keeps a home that reads as an elegant teahouse or an open-floor brothel of lace and silk. Of all the Houses, his Lost find it hardest to leave, and many refuse to try, out of something they insist is love.

**Can teach:** Contracts of Cursing · Contracts of Feeling
**Arcane specialties:** Toxicology · Manipulation and dependency

### The House of the Watery Grave — Lord Ren

The waters around the Isle are vast, and from them come beings of beauty and woe. Lord Ren has a siren's call to the depths and a drowning death for anyone too weak for the currents: equal parts beauty and fury. His Lost are built on the sorrow of the deep and the ferocity of waves. He holds his court at Amaia's sufferance and is very careful never to forget it.

**Can teach:** Contracts of Elements (water Elementals only) · Contracts of Stone
**Arcane specialties:** The deep waters · Siren traditions

### The House of Sails and Swagger — Captain Sparkle Beard Von Fancypants

Ahoy. The Captain's home is the high seas of the Dreaming, and her crew are drawn to adventure and violence in roughly equal measure. Her ramshackle port appears at Sparklebeard's Cove when she is in residence and melts to mist when she sails. She makes merry when she is not pillaging the other Houses, and maintains bitter rivalries with most of them. She will not cross the bana'Shee. Even she knows that line.

**Can teach:** Contracts of Fleeting · Contracts of the Long Chase · Contracts of Stone
**Arcane specialties:** Navigation · Piracy and freedom

### The House of the Cogs — Professor Aether

A studious sort, found in a research facility of impossible innovation that spans all of time. She gathers Lost willing to work hard at restoring the world. She is not certain what it ought to be restored to, but restored it shall be. The ten-year hole in the record is the thing she is actually chasing, and she will not say so.

<div class="callout">
<strong>A note for players:</strong> Lost of the Professor's house are statistically the likeliest in the court to die on the job, generally as experiments that went differently than planned. Sacrifices must be made in the name of science.
</div>

**Can teach:** Contracts of Artifice · Contracts of Elements · Contracts of the Workshop
**Arcane specialties:** Old World history and the Interstice · Leystone and paratech theory
