## Eshtarra's Song

> *"Gaia fell, Her heartbeat still. Ash lies silent on both root and hill. I rose on the wind where Her last breath died; I am the Keening Sky that remains beside."*

When Gaia the Elder died, her children went to ash on her last breath. Fera and Garou alike, gone as though they had never been. What she left behind was a world of human statues and green things that had lost the will to keep growing, because the worldsoul holding them upright was no longer there.

Then Eshtarra the Greensinger did what no spirit before her had managed. She had been the Elder's herald, a great Wyrd spirit once called Danu, and she drew on the raw Wyld and the surging energy of the Great Wyrdstorm until she rose as the Celestine worldsoul of the Earth herself. Now Gaia the Younger, she began to sing. Her song resonated with the very bones of the world, calling life back into it and waking humanity out of stone.

Some of the humans who came out the far side of **the Interstice** were chosen and uplifted. The Fera were remade rather than restored, shaped into what this world needs instead of what the burned one wanted. The Garou became the *Hands of the Earthmother*, the Doers, charged with cleaning the immense corruption the Sundering left behind.

Which means your character is Eshtarra's child, and not one of Gaia the Elder's brought back from being disintegrated. The old grudges have nothing underneath them any more, the old hierarchies were built for a continent that now sits under the sea, and both the Litany and the tribes were rebuilt from the ground up. Nobody alive remembers the time before the End, and everything they think they know about it came from ancestor spirits, who are witnesses with opinions of their own.

## Life as a Garou

Every Gaian Shifter breed descends from human ancestry, from the original human tribes Gaia the Younger chose to serve her about two centuries ago. She had no interest in her Shifters supplanting the humans she had just saved, so she built the blessing to run along core matriarchal lines and to thin quickly with each generation removed from them.

<div class="callout">
Two Garou parents have homid children like themselves, so there is no such thing as a metis here. Set that idea aside if you brought it with you.
</div>

Most Garou, around seventy percent, are **Cityborn**. They are born inside a Basilica and on an ordinary day you could not pick them out of a crowd of citizens. The rest are **Travelers**, the ones whose families stayed outside the walls and outside the regulations that come with them, working as nomadic guides, merchants, and mercenaries, keeping the tribal traditions that most of their cousins traded away for stone walls and safety in numbers.

**Wildborn** are rare and have no Garou ancestry at all. They are natural wolves lifted up and touched directly by the worldsoul through Eshtarra's Spirit Courts. That divine attention comes with expectations nobody could reasonably meet, and most Wildborn end up living apart from Garou society because of it.

Provided they do not meet a violent end, a modern Garou lives about twice as long as a mundane human and stays youthful until the last decade or so. Only a handful of the first generation are still with us, and they are very old indeed.

Here is how the blessing thins across generations:

| My tie to the core lineage is my... | So I am a Garou... | ...or a Human |
|---|---|---|
| Mother | 99% | 1% |
| Grandmother | 66% | 34% |
| Great-Grandmother | 50% | 50% |
| Great-Great Grandmother | 34% | 66% |
| Great-Great-Great Grandmother | 1% | 99% |

## The Dates That Shape a Garou's Politics

**Around 50 TE:** the Conclave and the Rite of Weighing. Fifty years into their service, the shifters concluded they did not have the power to meet the mandate and said so before the Celestines. The courts agreed the case had merit, and then refused ever again to bless a whole tribe or lineage, because blessing whole tribes is precisely what the old Garou squandered. Judges of each court now weigh one soul at a time. Only five courts were willing to take part. The rest declined, and their paths are locked away behind staff permission.

**75 to 157 TE:** the Basilicas. First the Hearthbind Ritual, then the Reconstruction. Garou went from wilderness guardians to residents with addresses.

## Purgatory

The City-Isle is neither wilderness nor safe. Outside the shimmer you have the **Dusktooth Jungle** and its shadow-teleporting theropods, which hunt in coordination. **Blackwind Valley**, where the bana'Shee tolerates only his own and takes his tribute in body parts. Then there is the **Bitterthorn**, which may well be a single organism and which eats people up through the floor, and the **Mistcrown**, which rearranges itself when it is watched.

Inside the shimmer you have five factions, a Council of Six, and a great many people who would very much prefer the aberrant problem stayed outside where they have been told it belongs.

The **Circle of Bone & Ivy** is the natural home for one of us. They ward portals, close rifts, and keep the peace between the material world and the spirit one. They are supernatural-majority and they welcome the Gifted openly. The **Order of Vigiles** takes Philodox seriously. The **Promethean Institute** cannot run an expedition without somebody who can talk to whatever is already living there, and the Circle spends half its time cleaning up after Institute expeditions, which is a campaign's worth of work on its own.

## The Litany

Nine songs, sung rather than filed. Learn what they mean before you learn to quote them.

**1. *Avert not thine eyes from the land's woe*** — Confront corruption where you find it, or carry word of it to someone who can. You are never required to die on it. You are never permitted to pretend you did not see it.

**2. *Feed not the gluttonous maw of war*** — No Garou makes war on Garou. Disputes over territory, honour, insult, or command go to the moot and the Philodox rather than to the claw. Challenge is lawful. It is fought to submission before witnesses, never to the death and never in secret.

**3. *None stand above the common good*** — No lineage, court, tribe, or rank stands above another. Authority is a task you are handed for a purpose. You answer for it while you hold it, and it goes back to the community when the purpose is finished. The first share of the kill goes to the hungriest rather than the highest.

**4. *Thou must sing thine own song, not another's*** — Worth is deeds. No Garou inherits glory and no Garou inherits guilt. What your line did, what your tribe was, what the old Nation broke: none of it is yours to claim and none of it is yours to carry. What you did yourself is both.

**5. *Fall not upon those from whom thine first arose*** — Never feed on humanity, never prey on them, never use your gifts to rule them. They are the root you grew from and they are the future.

**6. *Thy good word is as my own; shame not thy mother*** — A pact made is kept as made, renegotiated openly, or broken openly with the cost accepted. Never quietly. Your word given is Eshtarra's word carried.

**7. *Ye are as my hands; sever not my fingers*** — The packmate who falls to Harano is caught rather than culled. The elder who fails is carried rather than sent away to die usefully. Your worth does not expire when your strength does.

**8. *Sing thine shame as loud as thy glory, lest hubris overtake ye*** — Keep the record honest. The old Nation's failure is remembered as failure. The ancestors are witnesses and nothing more. What cannot be known, and the Interstice heads that list, does not get invented to fill the gap. No deed inflated, no shame buried, no story bent to flatter the teller or the tribe.

**9. *Remember always their dread did bury more of them than of thee*** — In the Week of Torches, four humans died for every Nightfolk killed. A frightened crowd is more dangerous to itself than to you. This one asks for restraint rather than secrecy: use Crinos inside the walls only as a last resort, never show claws in a human dispute, and hold up the Pax.

## The Basics

**Soaking damage.** Roll Stamina plus armour plus your form bonus at difficulty 6 against bashing and lethal. Aggravated cannot be soaked at all, and nothing in this system will ever hand you a way around that.

**Healing.** Bashing closes at one level per round, free. Lethal closes at one level per minute of rest, or instantly at a cost of one Gnosis per level. Aggravated heals at the same rate a mortal heals lethal, and nothing speeds it up.

**When lethal takes you past Incapacitated,** your body shuts down. You are helpless. You cannot act and you cannot be roused, and you regenerate lethal at the resting rate until you come back up on your own. Further lethal will not kill you, it only keeps you down there. Garou are very hard to kill with ordinary violence, and this is where that fact lives.

**When aggravated takes you past Incapacitated,** you die. Obsidian, fire, and your court's own material are the reliable routes, which is exactly why everyone who has ever needed a Garou dead hoards them, trades them, and uses them. No Rite brings you back.

**Kinship Sense.** You know another Gaian Shifter or Earthblessed on sight, always, with no roll, and no mundane disguise will hide one from you. A supernatural disguise is a different matter: roll your Art plus Gnosis against theirs.

**Spirit Sight.** You perceive the Pale Between laid over the material world without doing anything to make it happen. You cannot touch it without stepping sideways, but you are never unaware of what is standing next to you. Stepping sideways plays out as a scene, and there is no roll for it on this sheet. The Gauntlet is damaged in this era, and the Circle of Bone & Ivy holds firm views about who gets to open what.

## The Six Tribes

Tribe is community, culture, and calling. Eshtarra's first act was to make her children understand that their predecessors nearly finished themselves off fighting each other while the real enemy won. The inter-tribal wars are over. Tribes keep their identities and their arguments, and working alongside another tribe is competence rather than betrayal.

Each tribe grants a **Gift of Eshtarra**, a mundane-only bonus that never adds dice on an Art roll, and each imposes a **Tribal Cost**, which is Stigma-class: nobody pays for it and nothing removes it.
