# Dhampir

## *Those born caught between life and death*

---

## Who they are

A Dhampir is the living child of an undead sire and a mundane human mother. They breathe, eat, bleed, sleep, and age. They also carry a corruption that should not be in a living body, and everything about their existence follows from that one contradiction.

Most descend from the Hungry Dead, the only undead lineage that can reliably sire life at all. A smaller number descend from the Restless Dead, and specifically from body-usurpers: shades who wear stolen living flesh. Both groups produce Dhampir often enough that most Basilicas have a handful.

### Growing up half dead

*There is no healthy version of this childhood.*

The Hungry Dead have used their children as daytime servants for centuries, filling the role Ghouls filled for the Cainites of old and still fill for some Lilum today. A Dhampir is an investment expected to be useful above all else, and knows it early. They run the errands the sun makes difficult. They hold the leases, sign the papers, answer the door. Some are treated well, the way a valuable tool is treated well. Most find out eventually, at the first hint of defiance, how cruel someone who was twisted by hell’s tortures can be to their own children.

Those sired by the Restless Dead get one of two extremes with almost nothing in between. Either the shade is a smothering presence that has decided this child will live the ideal life they never got. Every failure is an insult, every success insufficient, and the obsessive nature of shades mean they never give up. Ever. Or the shade loses interest, wanders off after an obsession, and never comes back. Both produce adults with a particular relationship to being watched.

Jaded and resentful Dhampir become Aberrant Hunters at a rate no other splat comes close to. There are countless theories about why: Biology, immunities, the fact that they can work daylight hours. Every Dhampir hunter has heard all of them and has their own answer, which is usually shorter and about their parent.

### What they know about themselves

Every Dhampir learns the two facts that define them, and there is no gentle way to hear either.

The first is that they age slowly. That sounds like a gift for about a decade.

The second is that it does not matter. A Dhampir who dies always rises, and rises as the same kind of undead that sired them. However virtuous they try to be with the life they have, the ending is the same.

## Dhampir in the Third Era

Dhampir register as BRDs (Belign Reality Deviants), and whether they should is a running argument. They are alive, they read as human to every mundane test, and most Basilicas register them anyway, on the reasonable grounds that a Writ of Execution is a bad thing to argue about after the fact. A minority never register and pass as human for decades, which works right up until it doesn't.

Purgatory has the highest per-capita Dhampir population in the world, for two reasons. Everyone here came from somewhere else, and nobody's stated origin is challenged. Purgatory is also full of the *sires*, so a Dhampir here is never more than a few streets from the thing they are running from. Some of them did not come here to run.

Dhampir use the universal creation standards, unchanged.

### Attribute priority

You set the priority. Dhampir are flexible that way. The arrangement below is only a suggestion, and it reflects the most common profile: children raised to be useful intermediaries.

| Category | Priority | Starting Points |

### Your Arts at creation

Skein is your mandatory Affinity Art. Every Dhampir has it, and it is the only Art that belongs to them rather than to a parent.

Your second Art comes from the Aligned Arts list of the splat that sired you. You will find that list in the splat's own entry in the register. One of the two sits at 2 and the other at 1, your choice. Restricted and Discordant Arts are closed at creation as normal, and no freebie point may buy a Discordant or Restricted dot.

### Cathartic Vice

Pick one at creation as normal. A few come up often in this splat. Treat them as prompts, not as a menu:

* Compulsive Confession (telling a stranger the whole thing, once, at three in the morning)  
* Cruelty to Those Who Deserve It  
* Reckless Indulgence in things a body can only enjoy while it is still alive  
* Fighting

## Quick, and what fuels your Arts

Your Resource Trait is **Quick**, used in the old sense, as in *the quick and the dead*. Quick always equals your highest-rated Art. You never buy it, and you add it to your Art rating on an Art roll.

You have no Essence pool. You pay for Arts out of Vitality, at the mortal figure:

> **Vitality \= Stamina \+ 7**

A mortal's Vitality cannot fuel Arts without a Nightfolk Aspect, and Death's Legacy is that Aspect. Swapping Vitality for Essence is the only change. Everything else about Vitality applies to you unchanged, including the parts you will not enjoy.

Recovery is health-gated. Vitality returns at midnight at a rate equal to your unmarked Health boxes. Healthy, you refill faster than any Nightfolk. Mauled, you refill 2 a night. Crippled, 1\. Incapacitated, nothing at all until you start healing.

Your fuel is meat, so every point a predator takes off you is a point you cannot spend. Draining a Dhampir disarms them the same way it disarms a vampire. And there is no second pool to fall back on. One pool per character, always. Vitality is it.

Costs are standard, paid in Vitality:

| Art rating | Cost |
| :---: | :---: |
| 1 | free |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 5 |

A starting Dhampir with Stamina 2 holds 9 Vitality against a Nightfolk peer's 7 Essence, and refills up to 7 a night against their flat 5\. On paper you have the deepest tank in the setting.

The counterweights all come from one fact. The tank is your body: it is edible, it stops refilling the moment you get hurt, and running it dry puts a living human being into Attrition. Your acting pool still tops out at 10 (Art 5 plus Quick 5).

### Filling the tank back up

Refilling is deliberate. It happens in fiction, it is always roleplayed, and it runs at 1 Vitality per 2 post rounds:

> **Consuming blood or raw flesh that was recently alive.** A butcher's bucket, or a fresh kill opened in the field while the meat is still warm.

It does not have to come from a person, and most Dhampir are careful that it does not. It also looks exactly like the way their undead parent feeds, which is why a great many Dhampir are touchy about the subject.

If you take it from a living person, that is feeding, with everything that entails. No reduced rate, no gentler version, no Dhampir exemption.

### Ambient recovery and Attrition

Ambient recovery at midnight uses the mortal rule, your unmarked Health boxes, rather than the flat 5\. Willpower refreshes fully as it does for everyone. Presumption failure hits you unusually hard: a Dhampir with no food, no rest, and no chance to take what they need recovers nothing, and unlike most splats, they get no written exception to sustain them anyway. Starving a Dhampir works.

Attrition applies as written. At 0 Vitality you cannot activate any Art with a cost, though free Arts still work. If you are still at 0 at the end of a scene, roll permanent Willpower at difficulty 6 or lose control for the rest of it. A botch runs until the next midnight reset.

Attrition on a Dhampir is the mortal entry: a classical reality break. Psychosis, not frenzy, not a feeding rage. Your body is human and it breaks the way a human body breaks. It is the one thing about you that is entirely ordinary.

### Pushing

On any Art roll, you may push. Spend 1 Willpower and take 1 aggravated damage, either to cover the cost entirely or to buy exactly one die. Aggravated heals at the lethal rate, and nothing accelerates it. A marked box is also a point of nightly recovery gone. Push twice in a bad week and you have traded your tank for a spiral. Save it for the emergency you would not otherwise survive, and expect to feel it for a month.

## Death's Legacy

Every Dhampir carries a stain bequeathed by a parent who should not have been able to make them. It is not a power. You cannot spend it, remove it, buy it off, or improve it.

### The Stability penalty

> **On a failed end-of-scene Stability roll, a Dhampir loses one additional point of current Stability beyond the number of contributing triggers.**

Everything else about Stability is unchanged. Triggers still count once per scene however often they fired, the roll is still permanent Willpower at difficulty 6, and the trigger count still does exactly one thing: it sets the size of the loss. It is never also a difficulty modifier. A botch adds another point on top of that, so a botched roll in a two-trigger scene costs you 4\.

At Willpower 3 you fail this roll a little over a quarter of the time. Across ordinary one-trigger scenes that means you bleed at roughly twice the rate of anyone else, and across catastrophic four-trigger scenes it is closer to a quarter again. Disasters were already punishing without help. The penalty makes a *normal bad night* cost you something, and it keeps Catharsis at 1 per cycle from ever quite catching up.

### Extra triggers

Alongside the core list, mark:

* **Obey your Daddy** — you did something at your sire's word that you would not have done at anyone else's.  
* **Saw what you will become** — you witnessed your sire's kind acting monstrously, close enough that it was about you.  
* **Took a step down the Path** — you fed on a living person, or used your bequeathed Art in a way anyone watching would have called your sire's work.

The core triggers are not softened, removed, or reinterpreted for you, and staff remain the only route to Eldritch.

### Low Stability

The ladder applies unchanged. One rung needs special attention. At **Unmoored (2-3)**, Attrition madness can trigger at *any* Vitality level. The reality break no longer needs you to be empty. It only needs you to be far enough gone.

Breaking works as written. Permanent Stability 1 is the floor and starts a staff conversation: retirement, NPC conversion, or a final scene of your choosing. For this splat there is a fourth option, and both parties will know what it is.

## Skein, the Fated Art

You have exactly one Art of your own. It belongs to no other tree, and no other splat may hold it under any circumstance. It is the mandatory Affinity Art for every Dhampir ever made.

Skein reads like fate's consolation prize for a wretched lot in life. It weaves fortune. It does not create, destroy, heal, charm, or compel. It leans on the small joints of causality: the slip, the jam, the shot that was always going to go wide. It is the only thing in this system that can reach back and take an action away after it has already happened.

**Art roll:** Skein rating plus Quick, difficulty 6, 1s cancel successes. Attributes never contribute. No trait, Merit, Stigma, or Background ever adds dice to this roll. Wound penalties do apply. Difficulty caps at 10, and an attempt whose accumulated modifiers push past 10 is impossible rather than expensive.

Two limits govern the whole Art. Both are stated here once rather than repeated on every dot.

The first is that Skein never touches an Art activation roll to improve it. It grants no dice to an Art roll and no reroll of one. Improvements to an Art roll come from the difficulty side or not at all, and fortune is no exception. Skein can *cancel* another Art's effect at dot 5\. It can never make one work better.

The second is that Skein bonuses land on mundane rolls only. Contested defences count as mundane for this purpose, since they are not Art rolls.

### ● Cat's Landing (free)

You land on your feet. Falls that would bruise another person do not bruise you, and you do not fumble in the dark, on wet stone, or on a surface that has decided to move.

Once per scene, on a mundane roll made purely to avoid harm rather than to accomplish anything (a dodge, a grab at a ledge, getting out of the way, breaking a fall), reduce that roll's difficulty by 2, to a minimum of 4\.

Free Arts are meant to be reliable, which is why this is a difficulty reduction rather than bonus dice. It does not stack with itself.

### ●● Borrowed Luck (1 Vitality)

Declared **before** the roll it affects. Target yourself or one person you can see. Lasts one action.

| Net successes | Effect |
| :---: | :---: |
| 1 | \+1 die |
| 2-3 | \+2 dice |
| 4+ | \+3 dice |

Mundane rolls only, never an Art activation. Uncontested on a willing target. An unaware target counts as helpless, so that roll is uncontested too, and it marks the trigger.

### ●●● Crossed Path (2 Vitality)

The world develops an opinion about someone. Straps part, sightlines fill, the floor is wet where it was dry.

| Net successes | Effect |
| :---: | ----- |
| 1 | \+1 difficulty on the target's next action |
| 2-3 | \+2 difficulty on the target's next action, **or** \+1 difficulty on every roll of one named activity for the rest of the scene (choose on activation) |
| 4+ | \+2 difficulty on one named activity for the rest of the scene, **and** the first roll the target fails this scene is treated as a botch |

Contested against a resisting target. Name the activity specifically enough that anyone could recognise it in play: "shooting", "climbing", "lying to the Vigiles", not "acting". Remember the cap. If accumulated difficulty on an attempt would exceed 10, that attempt is impossible and is not made.

### ●●●● Cut and Retie (3 Vitality)

Once per scene. After a roll has resolved in your presence but before its consequences are narrated, it is made again. **The second result stands**, better or worse, including for you.

Eligible: mundane rolls, damage rolls, soak rolls, and contested defences. Not eligible: Art activation rolls, Stability rolls, and any Willpower or Psyche roll.

One net success is enough on your own roll or a willing ally's. Against an unwilling target it is contested as normal.

### ●●●●● The Debt Called In (5 Vitality)

Once per scene. Name one action that has just been resolved, taken by one actor you can see. You call in a debt the world did not know it owed you.

| Net successes | Effect |
| :---: | :---- |
| 1 | The action's effect drops to its minimum: one damage level, one success, the least it could possibly have done |
| 2-3 | **The action did not happen.** The actor stands where they stood, whatever they spent is spent, the round is gone |
| 4+ | As above, and the actor's next action this scene fails automatically |

Contested against a resisting actor.

This can cancel an Art whose activation roll already succeeded, and it is the only effect in the system that does. It does not refund the caster's Essence, and it does not make them any happier about it. It cannot restore the dead, undo damage that has already begun to heal, or reach an effect that has left the scene.

This is your answer to being the weakest thing in a room full of Nightfolk, and it is why careful elders find out whether a Dhampir is present before they commit to anything. A contest between peers sits at zero expected net, so circumstance modifiers decide it.

### Botches

A botch on any Skein roll spends the Vitality, fails the attempt, and rolls Backfire d10 as normal. **Overrun** on Crossed Path or Cut and Retie is memorable and entirely your problem. **Attention** marks the "witnessed something that should not exist" trigger and walls off the memory. If your luck has attracted notice, you may find out what noticed you later, in dreams, on staff's schedule.

## Bequeathed Arts

You may hold Arts from **one** other tree: the Aligned Arts list of the splat that sired you.

| Sire | Tree | Fuelled by |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Hungry Dead | Dharmic Arts | Vitality (Quick) |
| Restless Dead | Shadow Arts | Vitality (Quick) |

Your first bequeathed Art is chosen at creation from your sire's Aligned list and advances on the Aligned track (0/2/4/6/8, 20 XP to 5). Further Arts from that same tree advance on the Nonaligned track (1/3/5/9/13, 31 XP to 5), and each needs an in-fiction teacher of that splat as well as staff approval. Never at creation.

Any Art marked Restricted within that tree stays Restricted, and the default answer for a non-member is no. Discordant is closed to you entirely: Discordant covers another group within your own splat, and a Dhampir has no groups.

The four-Art limit applies, so Skein plus at most three bequeathed.

Bequeathed Arts work exactly as written in their own splat document, with the same effects, difficulties, durations, and costs. You pay for them out of Vitality like everything else. The Art does not change. Only the energy fueling it does, and that is also the energy fueling your living body.

Your Quick comes from your highest-rated Art, whichever tree it sits in, so pushing a bequeathed Art to 4 makes you a better Skein caster. That is deliberate, and it is the temptation at the centre of this splat: the fastest way to get good at your own gift is to get good at your parent's.

## What cannot touch you

**Toxins, disease, drugs, and poison:** any recovery roll or extended action against a disease, toxin, drug, or poison needs only half the successes it otherwise would, rounding up. Where staff set a duration instead of a roll, halve it. This covers venoms, refined leystone exposure, alcohol, and anything an alchemist in Antananarivo can produce. It does not cover aggravated damage, magical afflictions, curses, or anything an Art did.

**You cannot be Ghouled.** Vitae is inert in you. Drinking it does nothing at all, and a domitor who tries has spent their Vitae on a bad joke.

**You cannot be Blood Bonded.** No feeding-based bond, leash, or dependency mechanic from any splat attaches to you.

None of that makes you safe. You are not immune to Arts, to compulsion, to being fed on, or to a Writ of Execution, and your bequeathed Art works on you like anyone else's.

## The slow clock

You age normally to adulthood and then at half speed, so your apparent age is 18 plus half the years you have lived past 18\.

| Real age | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 | 120 | 140 | 160 | 180 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Looks** | 29 | **39** | 49 | **59** | 69 | **79** | 89 | **99** |

The clock is halved, not stopped. Dhampir live somewhere around two centuries, and then the body fails the way any body fails. Almost none of them live to see it, because two centuries is a long time to be a monster hunter with a mortal soak pool.

For play: a Dhampir old enough to remember the Ascendancy of Man (153-180 TE) looks fifty and was an adult during the Week of Torches. One old enough to remember the Reconstruction would look seventy and be lying about at least one thing.

## Damage, death, and the Inevitable Path

**Health track:** the standard 7 levels. Wound penalties apply to every dice pool, including Art activations, with the standard exemptions for soak, Willpower and Psyche, and Stability rolls.

**Soak:** Stamina plus armour, as a mortal. No aggravated soak, ever, from any source available to you.

**Healing:** mortal rates, with one advantage. Bashing and lethal heal one row faster on the mortal table, so Wounded and Mauled heal at the Bruised-to-Injured rate, Crippled at the Wounded rate, and Incapacitated at the Crippled rate. Aggravated is untouched, heals at the unmodified lethal rate, and nothing accelerates it. This blunts the spiral where an injured Dhampir cannot refill the pool they need to defend themselves. It does not remove it.

**Past Incapacitated:** further lethal or aggravated damage kills you, exactly as it kills a mortal. No dying state, no torpor, no second track.

**The Inevitable Path:** a dead Dhampir rises as the same kind of undead that sired them. Always. It does not depend on the manner of death, the state of the body, the presence of a sire, or anything your character believes about it. Decapitation, fire, and total destruction have all been tried. What they change is how long it takes and what comes back.

This ends the Dhampir character. What rises is a new character on the sire's splat sheet, built to the sim's standards. It needs staff approval like any other character on a Restricted splat. It is not a continuation and it inherits nothing from the old sheet: not Skein, not Quick, not the immunities, not the slow clock. Whether it inherits the *person* is the open question of this splat, and it is the one every Dhampir is asking.

## Being fed on

Dhampir are a favoured meal and every Dhampir knows it.

You hold Vitality, and that makes you a preferred meal for a Nightfolk. One point taken gives the predator 1 Essence. You take 1 lethal per 2 points against your own 24-hour cycle tally, or 1 per 3 if you are freely willing.

Feeding disarms you, because every point taken is Art fuel gone, and a drained Dhampir sits at 0 with no costed Arts and an immediate Attrition problem. Past empty, further feeding takes Health levels 1:1 as additional lethal, and that damage has to begin healing before your Vitality starts refilling at all.

The Utkinov Effect applies to you, because you are not a mundane human for Tether purposes. Feeding on the same Dhampir more than once in a lunar month accrues Tether on the predator at \+1 per drain, with all the usual consequences at 3\.

## Contests and resistance

You do not count as a Mundane Human in a contest. You hold Arts, so your contested defense is permanent Willpower plus your highest Art, or Grit plus highest Art against fear, horror, or supernatural compulsion. Being alive does not leave you helpless before an Art.

The Mortal Resistance option is therefore not available to you, and you do not need it. Wound penalties never touch a contested defense, an Attrition roll, or a Stability roll.
