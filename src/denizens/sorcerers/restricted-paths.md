<div class="callout">
<strong>Restricted.</strong> Both Paths below need a teacher and staff permission before you can learn either — neither is ever available at creation. Both advance on the Nonaligned track (31 XP to 5) once learned. Neither belongs to a School, and neither can be bought with freebies under any circumstance. They are restricted for opposite reasons: Chronomancy is restricted because of what it touches. Invocation is restricted because of what touches back.
</div>

## Chronomancy: The Path of Infinity

One hard limit, and it does not move. No rank of this Path reaches, addresses, observes or infers anything about **the Interstice** or the Sundering. The Blank Decade is not a plot this Path solves, and a Working aimed into it does not fail. It is impossible, and it is not attempted or paid for.

**●○○○○ Residue** *(free)*. Read what happened in this spot, up to a week back, as it happened rather than as a symbol. Contradicts witnesses, which is its main professional use. *One action.*

**●●○○○ Blink** *(1 Mana)*. Accelerate your personal timestream, granting an extra action or one automatic success on any Dexterity-based roll until the end of your next turn. *One action.*

**●●●○○ Recover** *(2 Mana)*. Pull a small object forward from the moment of its destruction, up to a year back. The timeline is unchanged; the object exists again. It must have been personal to someone you can name, which is why this cannot be used on money. *Five minutes.*

**●●●●○ Temporal Displacement** *(3 Mana)*. Your future self borrows your current mana to yank one willing target forward up to an hour into the future. During this time the target effectively ceases to exist and can neither act nor be acted upon. Usable once per week; costs one action in the moment, but you must cast the spell afterwards to close the loop. *Fifteen minutes; etched green leystone.*

**●●●●● The Closed Loop** *(5 Mana)*. Step bodily into the past at this location, up to a century back, for five minutes per net success, and act. Anything you change was always changed; nothing you do creates a contradiction, because the loop was already closed and you are how it closed. Anachronistic matter is ejected back to the present when the time expires, including you. *One hour or more; heartglass or refined leystone.* **Requires a prearranged ST scene. Marks *witnessed something that should not exist*.**

## Invocation: The Path of the Purchased Name

The calling, warding, bargaining with and binding of things that are not from here. Where an Ossian channels a ghost that is already in the room and a Wayfarer wakes a spirit that was already in the door, this Path reaches across and **brings something through**. That act is what the Basilicas cannot license, because the thing on the other end is a party to it and nobody has ever successfully served paperwork on a party to it.

Four kinds answer, of which you specialize in one: **Demons** (NOT the Fallen, who are beyond mortal reach), **True Fae** (Dreamborn & Wyrdborn), **Spirits**, and **Shades**. The grammar is the same for all four. What differs is what they want, what they cost, and what happens to you when the Vigiles find out.

**Access is by name.** Names are not taught. They are bought, inherited, or found written somewhere the finder should have left alone. Each name obtained in play grants either one specific entity or one lesser Court of a single kind. Holding a name in a kind is what lets you work ranks 1–3 against that kind at all; ranks 4 and 5 need the specific name of the specific thing. A Sorcerer who holds three names in three kinds is a Sorcerer three separate powers have a file on. Finding a name is how staff award Eldritch to a character who wanted this.

**●○○○○ The Circle** *(free)*. A warded circle that nothing of one named kind crosses, holding for one scene. It works from the outside and from the inside, and which side you are standing on is the only interesting question this rank ever asks. Nothing here calls anything, and the Sorcerers who stop at this rank are the sensible ones. *One action.*

**●●○○○ The Lesser Name** *(1 Mana)*. Call a minor entity of a kind you hold any name in. It must attend you for ten minutes. It is under no obligation to be useful, truthful or quiet, and it will remember your face. *One minute.*

**●●●○○ Bargain** *(2 Mana)*. A binding exchange: one service or one truthful answer, for one price it names. You do not roll to set the price. The Working determines only whether the terms hold; staff determine the terms, and the terms are the scene. A bargain struck here binds you as firmly as it binds the thing, and there is no jurisdiction in which you can appeal it. *Five minutes.*

**●●●●○ Compulsion** *(3 Mana)*. Bind a called entity to a service for one day per net success, without a bargain and without its consent. Contested, and the thing may spend its own Willpower to resist. Requires its specific name. Succeed and you have a servant that is counting. Fail and it knows your name, has your face, and is no longer bound by anything. *Fifteen minutes; etched green leystone.*

**●●●●● The Greater Name** *(5 Mana)*. Call something whose name you should not have, and hold it for the length of one bargain. Staff write this scene as an event, and it is the last purely voluntary decision the character makes about this Path. *One hour or more; a significant cost that is not material.*

### How the Four Kinds Differ

The rank does not change. What answers does.

**The Infernal.** Bargainers by nature, and the only kind that will offer terms before you ask for them. Compulsion works on them and they consider it a formality in a longer negotiation.

**The Fae.** They keep terms exactly and mean none of them. A Bargain with the Fae is resolved on the wording rather than the intent, always, and staff should write the wording down. Compulsion against a True Fae is contested at +2 difficulty to the Sorcerer, and a failed Compulsion against one does not merely release it — it constitutes an insult, which among the Fae is a transaction of its own. The Dreadmire coven's standing terms are the worked example: a game of riddles, or a year and a day.

**Spirits.** The kind closest to legitimate work, which is exactly what makes it the trap. A filed writ covers binding a spirit that is already present. No writ has ever covered calling one that was not. A practitioner who calls a spirit and then binds it under a genuine writ has committed one offence and documented it themselves.

**Shades of the Dead.** This reaches the dead who are not here — beyond an Ossian's instrument, beyond remains, beyond the room. What it buys is reach; what it does not buy is any of the Ossian's craft. It cannot read an Anchor, cannot settle a haunting, cannot lay anything to rest and cannot Unmake. Ossians remain the only permanent answer in the setting and nothing on this Path changes that. What it does instead is summon a shade away from wherever it was anchored, which the Circle of Bone & Ivy considers a professional obscenity and most Ossians consider unforgivable on grounds they will not explain to you.

### Where You Stand With the Law

Graded, and the grades matter.

| Kind Called | Consequence of Confirmed Use |
|---|---|
| **Spirits** | Vigiles case file. Licence suspension pending hearing, and the hearing goes badly. Survivable once. |
| **Shades** | A hearing, plus the end of your professional life among practitioners. The Circle of Bone & Ivy does not use the Vigiles for this. |
| **Fae** | No statute covers it, because none has survived contact with the question. The consequence is not legal and does not arrive on a schedule. |
| **Infernal** | A writ of execution. Not a hearing. Unchanged, and every Basilica honours it, including the ones that honour nothing else. |

**Calling is never licensed, for any kind, anywhere.** There is no writ, no permit, no Academy exemption and no Institute fellowship that covers it. A Sorcerer who explains to a Vigile that they only called a minor spirit is explaining the aggravating factor.

### What This Path Never Does

* It never calls, compels or bargains with anything from **the Interstice**, and a Working aimed there is impossible rather than failed.
* It never crosses the **Governor's Toll** or the coastal warp of the **Wandering Isle**, in either direction.
* It never reaches a **living** being of any splat. This Path addresses what is not from here; a person is not extraplanar and never becomes a valid target.
* It never produces the Ossian's **Quietus**, in whole or in part.
* **Compulsion is not permanence.** Nothing here produces a standing servant beyond its duration, and a Compulsion allowed to lapse produces a free entity with a grievance and your name.

<div class="callout callout--rare">
<strong>Two things for storytellers, and they are why this Path exists.</strong> First, nothing here is ever safely repeatable. An entity dealt with twice is an entity with a relationship to the character, and relationships of this kind only ever accrue in one direction — across all four kinds, and the kinds talk. Second, this Path generates Eldritch faster than any other route in the setting, which means it generates permanent Stability loss faster, which means the natural arc of an Invoker is a character who becomes unplayable. Permanent Stability 1 is a conversation and a retirement. That is the genre.
</div>
