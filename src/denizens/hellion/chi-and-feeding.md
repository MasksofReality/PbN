## Chi

Chi is stolen life energy. It is your food, your fuel and your magic, and unlike your western cousins you can take it from almost anything that lives or has been made holy. Human and Nightfolk blood and flesh, yes, but also the standing power of a shrine, a spring, a graveyard, a well-kept stretch of earth, and the spirits of a neighbourhood.

Your Chi rating rises automatically to match your highest-rated Art. It is never bought.

| | |
|---|---|
| Chi at creation | 2, from your Affinity Art at 2 |
| Essence pool at creation | 7 |
| Maximum pool | 10, at Chi 5 |
| Ambient recovery | 5 Essence at the midnight reset, along with a full Willpower refresh |
| Nightly upkeep | 1 Essence per cycle, charged at the reset before recovery, simply to rise and stay risen |

The upkeep is the first thing settled each cycle, so a Hungry Dead who ends a night at zero wakes at four rather than five.

### The Sustaining Condition

The midnight allowance assumes you had some ordinary chance to sustain yourself offscreen. Where you demonstrably did not, staff withhold it, and a bound Hungry Dead is not a renewable battery.

### Spending Chi

Healing injuries. Activating Dharmic Arts. The surcharges some Arts carry at their highest dot. One Essence buys you a scene of seeing Chi move through the land, the dragon lines and the living bodies around you, which is the trick every newcomer learns first and the one that makes the city legible.

**Chi is not a strength stat.** You cannot spend it to boost Physical Attributes. Physical enhancement runs through the Demon Arts and nowhere else.

You cannot create Ghouls and you cannot Blood Bond. Neither is a matter of skill or dedication. Your ichor does not do that, and no Art in the roster changes it.

### At Zero

You cannot activate any Art that costs Essence. Your free first-dot Arts still work.

You are also in Attrition, and at the end of every scene you spend there you roll permanent Willpower at difficulty 6. Fail and you frenzy for the rest of the scene: you attack whoever is in reach and you feed. Botch and it holds until the next midnight reset with no further roll.

Left at zero through a full night and the day after it, you go into torpor. See [Injury, Torpor & Final Destruction](/denizens/hellion/injury-and-death/).

## Feeding

Feeding is the ugliest thing on your sheet and the document is not going to dress it up.

There is no roll to feed. Once the victim is held, willing or unable to interfere, you take a point per round at first, then two, then three, then four, the rate climbing the longer you stay latched. Every point you take is one Essence you gain, up to your maximum. Break contact and the rate resets to the bottom of the ladder. Getting hold of a victim who does not want to be held is an ordinary chase, grapple and pin on the mundane sheet, and no Art makes you better at swallowing.

Every two points you take costs the victim a level of lethal damage, three points per level if they are freely willing. The tally belongs to the victim and runs until midnight, so two of you sharing a mortal wound them twice as fast. A wounded victim also refills more slowly than a whole one, which means gorging destroys the supply. Elders have known this for two hundred years and newcomers rediscover it monthly.

Feeding on another Nightfolk works at full value with no penalty. The deterrent is entirely social, and it is a real one, because the Fellowships read repeat predation on peers as the signature of something that has stopped being a person. Repeat it and you accrue Tether: every drain of the same Nightfolk beyond the first inside a lunar month binds you a little further to them, and at three points everyone else's Essence yields you half value while any roll to refuse that person or act against their interests runs two difficulties harder. It fades a point per month of complete abstinence. They never have to know.

### Your Sources

| Source | What It Gives You |
|---|---|
| **Human or Nightfolk (fresh blood)** | Standard feeding, standard rate, standard damage. The victim feels all of it. |
| **Human or Nightfolk (living flesh)** | One Essence per level of lethal damage you carve out. It converts injury directly and never touches their pool, so it leaves no feeding tally, but Tether still accrues against Nightfolk. Deeply taboo everywhere, and the single most reliable way to lose your footing on any path. |
| **Animals (fresh blood/living flesh)** | One Essence, whatever the animal. A rat and a horse are worth the same to you and both are beneath the dignity of most. |
| **Breath** | At Dharma 3, you can draw from the exhalations of a living crowd at the Replenishment rate. Nobody is hurt and nobody notices. |
| **The ambient world** | At Dharma 5, you can take Replenishment from the world itself. |

The ladder from hunting to hosting is the arc your character is climbing, and everything above Dharma 2 on it has to be earned in play.

### Feeding on Another Player's Character

**Every time, in advance, out of character.** Ask in IMs, agree what happens, then play it out. There is no "but my character would." A player's agreement is also what makes a donor willing for rules purposes; nothing your character does in the fiction can manufacture it, and any compulsion, Geas or Tether on the donor voids it entirely.

If you want to feed without negotiating, feed on a background mortal you describe yourself. That option is always open and nobody has to approve it.

## Using Dharmic Arts

Every Art is a five-dot ladder, learned in order.

| Dot | Cost | What It Buys |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Free | A passive or always-on expression of the Art. No cost, no roll, and the thing your character is known for. |
| **2** | 1 Chi | A short, sharp application. One suggestion, one crossing, one scene of an edge. |
| **3** | 2 Chi | Real power, and usually the point at which bystanders stop reading you as unsettling and start reading you as supernatural. |
| **4** | 3 Chi | Scene-changing. Gates, proxies, marks left on other people. |
| **5** | 5 Chi | Legendary, usually with a surcharge of 1 Willpower on top, and it always leaves evidence. |

**The roll.** An Art roll is Art rating plus Chi at difficulty 6, with ones cancelling successes. Nothing else ever adds dice to it. Not a Merit, not a soul, not a Stigma, not your Dharma. Where an Art gets better, it gets better on the difficulty side.

Wound penalties do apply. A Crippled capstone rolls worse than a healthy novice, and that is the point.

**Self-only effects and unresisting targets need no contest.** Pay the cost and read the result off your raw net successes. A mortal is always helpless before an Art unless they spend a Willpower to become actively resisting, which they may do once per scene.

**Against something that is knowingly resisting**, the target rolls permanent Willpower plus their highest Art at difficulty 6, or Grit plus their highest Art where what you are doing is fear, horror or a compulsion to flee. Mortals defend on Willpower alone, Grit alone against fear. Your net is your successes minus theirs. Against an NPC, subtract their tier figure instead of rolling for them.

Read graded effects in bands of one success, two to three, and four or more. Against a peer your expected net is zero, so circumstance is what decides the outcome, and staff hand out circumstance modifiers freely.

**Damaging Arts are never contested.** Roll, and the effect deals one Health level on any success plus one more per net success past the first. Art damage is not soaked unless the Art produces an ordinary physical effect, which claws, conjured blades and thrown objects all do. Where the effect crosses open space as a discrete stroke the target may dodge on Dexterity plus Athletics, each success cancelling one of yours. Where it starts at the target there is no dodge.

**Natural weapons.** Claws, fangs, bone edges and demon armaments are ordinary physical effects. You attack with Dexterity plus Brawl, damage rolls as a weapon pool at the rating given, and the target soaks normally. Spending 1 Essence on the strike upgrades that one blow to aggravated, which nothing soaks.

**Pushing.** Once per Art roll you may spend 1 Willpower and take 1 aggravated to cover the base Essence cost outright, or to buy exactly one extra die. Buying dice is a bad deal on purpose. Covering the cost of a fifth-dot Art when your pool is empty is not, and where an Art carries a surcharge, Pushing covers the base cost only.

**The ceiling.** Difficulty caps at 10. Once accumulated modifiers push past it the effect is impossible, not expensive. It is not attempted and nothing is paid for it.

<h3 id="the-infernal-tell">The Infernal Tell</h3>

Using any Demon Art (Black Wind, Iron Mountain, Jeweled Eyes, the Red Panoply, Tears of Hell) where mortals or lesser Dharmas can perceive it makes everyone present worse. For a number of hours equal to the highest Demon Art rating used in the scene, they resist their own darker impulses at one difficulty higher than usual. This lands on Psyche and Willpower rolls, never on Art rolls.

Most Demon Arts are not subtle either. Tears of Hell opens a wet vertical eye in your forehead. The Red Panoply splits your skin. Consider the room.

<h3 id="the-soul-gate">The Soul Gate</h3>

No Art may be raised above the cap set by where its keyed soul sits in your ranking. Your soul order decides which miracles you will ever be able to learn, and that decision is made at creation and lived with.

| The Art's Keyed Soul Is Your... | Art Caps At |
|---|---|
| Dominant | 5 |
| Ascendant | 4 |
| Quiet | 3 |
| Recessive | 2 |

An Art keyed to two souls uses the better of the two rankings. An Art keyed to Balance uses your Ascendant cap.
