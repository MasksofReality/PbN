## **Building a human**

Humans use the universal creation standard without modification. There is no human variant of this table.

| Element | Standard |
| :---- | :---- |
| Attributes | 7 / 5 / 3 across Physical, Social, Mental, priority set by your Profession |
| Abilities | 13 Talents / 9 Skills / 5 Knowledges, from the permitted V20 list |
| Psyche | 7 dots across Conscience, Self-Control and Grit, minimum 1 each |
| Willpower | 3 |
| Backgrounds | 5 dots, one of which your Profession requires you to place somewhere specific |
| Freebie points | 21\. Attribute 5, Ability 2, Willpower 2, Psyche 2, Background 1, Merit 1 per point |
| Nature and Demeanour | Both mandatory |
| Cathartic Vice | One, mandatory, staff-approved |

Only three derived values need recording. Stability is Conscience plus Self-Control, since humans hold no Eldritch at creation. Vitality is Stamina plus 7, and humans are the only characters who track it. Health is the standard seven levels on one track.

### Spending 21 freebies

You will not be spending 11 of them on an Art dot, which gives you the largest effective creation budget in the game. Three purchases are worth more to a human than to anyone else.

**Willpower** is the mortal defence. You defend against Arts on permanent Willpower alone, or Grit alone against fear. It also pays for your Professional Practice and for the mortal resistance clause, out of the same nightly pool. Willpower 5 at creation is the standard recommendation.

**Psyche** feeds Stability directly, so every dot raises both your ceiling and your starting pool. With 7 dots and a minimum of 1 in Grit, the highest a human can start unaided is Stability 6\. Freebies push that to 8, which is the top of the Composed band and higher than most Nightfolk will hold for long, because Eldritch arrives on their sheets far more readily than on yours.

**Stamina** sets Vitality, and feeding damage lands at 1 lethal per 2 points taken. For a mortal in this setting, Stamina is a survival stat as it never was before.

Willpower 3 is the core baseline, and this document does not raise it. If mortal fragility ever needs a patch, a higher mortal baseline is the lever, and it belongs here rather than in the universal table: a freebie discount would only invite players to awaken their way around it.

### Abilities

There is no Security, Research, Counseling, History or Occult in this system, and Technology is a Knowledge rather than a Skill.

| You mean | Buy |
| :---- | :---- |
| Lockpicking, safecracking, bypassing physical security | Larceny |
| Source work, archival method, historiography | Academics |
| Following a trail of evidence | Investigation |
| Pastoral care, counselling, reading distress | Empathy, or Medicine for clinical work |
| Supernatural scholarship: taxonomies, doctrine, what a Garou is | Arcane |
| Glyphcode notation, paratech maintenance, Old World scitech | Technology |

Arcane is a taught subject now. Post-Pax, the supernatural is a curriculum: registries, splat taxonomies, ward theory, Directorate law. It is learned the way any subject is learned, and the first three dots can be bought freely, at creation or after. Going past the third dot means learning enough about how the cosmos works to attract the attention of things that are bad for your mental health, and it carries a mandatory point of Eldritch. Arcane 4 requires staff approval and a dedicated scene, and a Mundane Human must transition to Sorcerer in the process. You cannot take Arcane above the third dot at creation.

### Backgrounds

Humans can have Allies, Alternate Identity, Contacts, Fame, Influence, Mentor, Resources, Retainers, Status. A clinic, a workshop or a stilt-house is a use of Resources. Standing in a Grand Faction or with the Vigiles is Influence or Status. In Purgatory, Alternate Identity is close to civic infrastructure, since nobody's stated origin is challenged and nobody's stated origin is believed.

Backgrounds are story-gated in both directions: you buy them where play has established the circumstance, you can lose them in play, and XP is credited back when the loss came from plot rather than your own choice.

## **Where the weight actually is**

Every human advantage is structural.

The most obvious one is the sheet itself. Every point of XP you earn goes to Attributes, Abilities, Merits and Backgrounds. A Nightfolk spending on Arts is not spending here, and Art dots raise their Resource Trait as well, so they will keep spending there. After a year of play the human simply has more of the sheet filled in, and the gap only widens.

The second is standing. A human working inside a human institution, as a recognised participant, holds a circumstance advantage: a difficulty reduction of 1 on the relevant Social, Investigation, Law, Politics or Finance roll. This is the Standing modifier—the ordinary circumstance lever, not a new bonus. In Purgatory the institutions that qualify are the licensed watch, the harbour and guild offices, the Order of Vigiles, the Promethean Institute and the Sovereign Club. It does not apply in the Ashen Court or the Circle of Bone and Ivy, where a mundane human is tolerated at best, and it never applies to someone bluffing their way into a body they have no place in. That is Subterfuge.

Standing comes with paperwork attached. Humans file complaints, testify, register grievances, pull records and appear before the Council without any of it being a problem. In a city where five factions prefer their business unrecorded, the ability to make something official is closer to a weapon than it sounds.

Then there is the Pax, which sits on your sheet and costs you nothing. Using an Art on an unconsenting human is precisely the predatory conduct that revokes BRD status and sends a writ, and the core rules already charge a supernatural a point of Stability for using an Art on a helpless mortal. Mortals always count as helpless. The Vigiles investigate. The Hunters execute. A Nightfolk who leans on a human in public has handed that human a live grenade with their own name etched on it—which is why the careful ones do it where nobody is looking, and why a human who is seen is safer than a human who is alone.

Alongside that, consider what is absent. No BRD registration, no writ risk, no Tether, no Backfire, no frenzy, and no Attrition unless somebody has drained you to nothing. The botch table's Art rows are dead letters on your sheet. When you botch, you have failed badly in public, like a person. Your Stability track is equally quiet: you start where most Nightfolk start, at Eldritch 0, but nothing about being human erodes it afterward. No Art use charges you a point, no splat condition bleeds it, no bloodline mechanic sits underneath it. Humans come apart more slowly than anything else in the setting.

Last: once per scene, you may spend 1 Willpower to actively resist a single use of an Art, forcing a contest that would otherwise be uncontested, or to reroll a failed defence in a contest already underway. Learn this clause. It is the difference between a bad night and a *really* bad night.

### And the costs

You are fully mortal. Soak is Stamina plus armour against bashing and lethal, aggravated is unsoakable, and healing runs the mortal table: a month to recover from Crippled, two from Incapacitated. Nobody is spending Essence to close your wounds.

You are also the pantry. A Stamina 3 human holds 10 Vitality. A predator who drains you dry will stay latched past the fourth round and leave five levels of lethal behind; while those wounds knit over weeks, your Vitality crawls back at two points a night. Most humans never learn what happened to them. The law is on your side, and the law arrives afterward.

You will lose most contests against Arts—permanent Willpower alone, Grit alone against fear. A creation-standard human defends on 3 dice, or 5 if you took the advice above. It will be said plainly at application; make sure you have heard it.

And you are legible. In most Basilicas that means an address and a tax record. In New Colombia it means genealogy as devotional practice, which makes it the best place on Earth to trace a bloodline and the worst place to hide one. In Purgatory it means the Vigiles have a file, the Ashen Court has a copy, and somebody sold them both.

The last cost is not on the sheet at all. The Pax is twenty years old. It replaced twenty-seven years of law that called your character's neighbours vermin, and the generation that wrote those laws is not entirely gone. Humans are the majority whose parents did the hunting.

## **Professional Practices**

Each Profession grants one Practice: something the character can do because of who they are in this city, and for no supernatural reason at all.

A Practice costs 1 Willpower and may be used once per scene. Unless the entry says otherwise, no roll is made and staff narrate the outcome. Practices are mundane. They are never rolled as Art plus Resource, they cannot be read with Intelligence plus Eldritch, they cannot be dispelled or contested as workings, they carry no Backfire, and they never add dice to anything. Where a Practice does call for a roll, it is an ordinary roll, and it can fail like one.

Each Profession also carries a Stigma: the standing liability that comes packaged with its Practice.

Willpower is the price because it is the only currency a human has. It refreshes fully at midnight, and it is the same pool that pays for mortal resistance and for overriding an urge at low Stability. A Willpower 5 character has five points a cycle and three things worth spending them on. That tension is the cost.

Practices also leave traces. Every Practice works through somebody's system, and in Purgatory every system belongs to a faction. A used Practice is a fact somebody can buy.

## **Merits**

Merits cost 1 freebie per point at creation and 2 XP per point afterward. Once play has begun they are story-gated, and a lost Merit is not refunded. A Merit describes the character rather than their circumstances; if one becomes untrue, staff may allow you to reassign it rather than credit it back.

### Human Merits, any Profession

| Merit | Cost | Effect |
| :---: | :---: | :---- |
| Local | 1 | Name one district: the Mangrove road, the harbour wards, a Grenveld village. Difficulty drops by 1 on Streetwise, Contacts and navigation rolls there. |
| Prepared | 1 | Once per scene, you have on your person one mundane item you could plausibly be carrying. |
| Stubborn | 1 | Once per cycle, reroll one failed Grit roll. |
| Scarred Over | 2 | One extra die to soak bashing only. |
| Unremarkable | 2 | Difficulty rises by 2 for anyone attempting to recall, describe or trace you through witnesses. |
| Hard to Rattle | 2 | Difficulty drops by 1 on Grit rolls against a category of horror you have faced before and survived. Name it with staff. |
| Trusted Face | 2 | Name one institution you have no formal place in. Staff grant you the Standing modifier there anyway. |
| Registered Donor | 1 | You hold a filed, lawful feeding arrangement with a named BRD. Draws on you count as freely willing, at 1 lethal per 3 points, and the paperwork is a defence in a Vigiles inquiry. It is also a leash: you generate no Tether, so your donor is not becoming dependent on you. Consider what that says about how replaceable you are. |
| Second Wind | 3 | Once per scene, spend 1 Willpower to ignore wound penalties for a single action. |

### Implants

Any implant that confers capability is bought as its own Merit, requires the Grafted Merit (see the Ghostwright), and is story-gated after creation. All of them carry the same standing liability: what is bolted to your nervous system is scitech, and scitech is unreliable near a wyrdstorm or in a disturbed aetheric zone. Staff raise the difficulty by 1 or 2 in those conditions, or shut the implant down outright. In Purgatory, where the ambient field is dense and never quite still, that is not a rare event.

| Implant | Cost | Effect |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Optical Suite | 2 | Difficulty drops by 1 on sight-based Perception rolls at distance, in poor light, or on fine detail. |
| Grid Jack | 2 | Difficulty drops by 1 on Technology rolls made in direct physical contact with a paratech system. |
| Concealed Mount | 2 | One named tool or small weapon is always on you, including where it should not be. It survives an ordinary search and reads as tissue on a casual inspection. |
| Reinforced Frame | 3 | One extra die to soak bashing and lethal. Obvious on any medical examination and on most careful ones. |

## **Stability, triggers and Eldritch**

The core trigger list applies in full, the end-of-scene roll is unchanged, and a trigger counts once per scene however often it fires. This document adds triggers. It softens nothing.

Mark one of these if your character, in this scene:

**Denied what they saw.** Talked themselves, or someone else, out of the truth in order to keep their life intact.

**Used their standing against someone who had none.** Turned a record, a credential, a complaint or a uniform on a person with no way to answer it. In Purgatory that includes the Pax itself: informing on a BRD you knew was innocent, or letting a writ proceed on evidence you knew was thin. The machinery that protects you kills people, and using it is a choice.

**Broke the specific faith their Profession runs on.** Each Profession names its own. Where an added trigger and a core trigger describe the same act, mark one.

**Witnessed something that should not be seen.** Because Nightfolk are publicly known, much of their existence no longer shocks. A registered Lilum drinking from a filed donor at the next table is a regular sight. A Garou changing shape in the street is a public-order matter, not an existential one. A Lost telling stories in a park about their Keeper is good entertainment. The trigger fires when humanity's "new normal" fails to contain the horror you have actually witnessed. Working examples:

* The Mistcrown noticing you, or anything the Inner Reaches do with distance and time.  
* The bana'Shee's attention.  
* Someone grown into the Ruby Coast reef, red and smiling and not quite alive or dead.  
* A Grenveld village that just lost its two hundred and first resident, on schedule, to nothing.  
* Anything the Dreadmire coven says about the Governor while refusing to say what "It" is.  
* Standing next to a Working that goes badly wrong—the kind that draws Attention—and watching the caster come back from wherever they went with a hole in their memory they can feel but not open.

### Eldritch, for humans

Eldritch is what a mind pays to see the inner workings of the cosmos. For a human, the trade buys exactly one thing: the Intelligence plus Eldritch roll to read a Working by sight. Every dot also charges a permanent point of Stability that never comes back.

Eldritch 1 is awarded only after repeated, undeniable contact the character chose not to look away from. One impossible night is a trigger, not a dot. A season on the thread is a dot. A second dot is awarded only for method: an Archivist reconstructing the Interstice from primary sources, a Physician cataloguing what heartglass does to tissue, a Ghostwright following a reactor conduit further than the schematic goes.

The human roads to Eldritch in this setting are all survivable and none of them free. The Interstice. The Inner Reaches. The quintessence reactors. Heartglass and its appetite. The Governor. What aberrants actually are, as against what every respectable text says they are. What a waygate is really cutting through, and where its passengers go for the second they are inside it. And where the Awakened went.

## **Draining, death and the two ways out**

**Health.** Seven levels, one track, wound penalties as written. No second track.

**Soak.** Stamina plus armour at difficulty 6, against bashing and lethal. Aggravated is unsoakable, and no Merit here grants aggravated soak.

**Healing.** The mortal table, of which this splat is the baseline. Aggravated heals at the lethal rate and nothing accelerates it.

**Being fed upon.** Feeding takes Vitality at one point per Essence gained. The loss carries no wound penalty. Damage is 1 lethal per 2 points taken, or 1 per 3 if you are freely willing, which under the Pax usually means filed. The tally belongs to you rather than to the predator and resets at midnight, so two predators in one night share one count. Vitality returns at midnight equal to your unmarked Health boxes: the uninjured refill fast, the battered barely at all, the Incapacitated not at all.

**Humans generate no Tether.** The Utkinov Effect is a property of aspected essence, and mortals do not produce it, however regular the arrangement. A human herd is safe to keep, which is exactly why humans are farmed rather than hunted, and why a filed donor arrangement is more secure for the Nightfolk than for the human.

**At 0 Vitality.** Pallid, freezing, barely lucid. Further feeding takes Health levels one for one as lethal, and that damage must begin to heal before Vitality starts refilling.

**Attrition.** A human drained to nothing enters Attrition like anything else. At the end of each scene you remain at 0 Vitality, roll permanent Willpower at difficulty 6\. On a failure you lose control for the rest of the scene, and for a human that means a classical reality break. On a botch you do not come back until the next midnight reset. This is the only route by which a Mundane Human reaches Attrition at all, and it always takes somebody putting you there.

**Past Incapacitated.** Further lethal or aggravated damage kills you. No corpus, no anchor, no vessel, no clause that turns it around. A dead human is dead, and in Purgatory a body is a commodity: the Vigiles look the other way where Amaia is concerned, the Bitterthorn does not return what it swallows, and the reef keeps what grows into it. Death is not automatic, not owed, and not a build plan.

### The first way out: Seeing the Ineffable Truth

A mortal who sees something they should not have may gain a permanent Eldritch 1 and become a Sorcerer, taking Occult Arts on the Nonaligned track. This requires staff approval and an in-fiction scene.

As a Sorcerer you lose everything you had on your prior sheet and transition to the Sorcerer ruleset.

### The second way out you didn't want to take: Abduction by the Gentry

The Gentry are mercurial beings of such godlike power that human law helplessly looks the other way at whatever they do. Selfish in the extreme, and lacking the spark of creativity that makes humans what they are, they sometimes steal a Mundane away to suit their own whims, with little interest in consent. Those who escape and claim sanctuary under the sacred law of guest right before any of the Triune are off-limits for recapture, and count thereafter as one of the Lost.

As a Lost you lose everything you had on your prior sheet and transition to the Lost ruleset.

### The third way out that isn't: Ghouling

A living human regularly fed Vitae becomes a Ghoul. Ghouling is a state rather than a change of splat. The character stays a Mundane, with all that entails. They gain no Vitae pool; instead they pay for their domitor's Arts out of Vitality, at twice whatever the Art normally charges, and they may learn only those Arts, capped at the second dot.

A Ghoul fed within the last lunar month stops aging and heals lethal damage at the bashing rate—one level an hour. Aggravated is unchanged. They also acquire the domitor's leash: an unbuyable physical dependency that raises the difficulty by 2 on any roll to act against the domitor or to refuse them. It is chemical, not telepathic. There are no commands and no mental override.

What ghouling reliably provides beyond that is a domitor and a Vigiles inquiry waiting to happen, because the line between a filed donor arrangement and a fed thrall is exactly the line the Pax was written to police.

Sorcerers and Metahumans cannot take this road quietly. Mana and Psi both reject Vitae, and the attempt is an agonising internal conflict that requires a storyteller scene and can end in failure or death.

# **The Six Professions**

Each opens with the Profession's place in Purgatory, then its numbers. Every suggested build sums exactly to 7/5/3, 13/9/5, five Backgrounds and 21 freebies, so you can lift one wholesale or use it as a starting point.

---

## **Enforcer**

*The watch, faction security, and the people who keep order under a twenty-year-old law*

Enforcers keep order in the City-Isle: licensed watch, harbour security, faction muscle, private contractors, Vigiles auxiliaries, and the informal authority that keeps the crowded wards from boiling over. They are not Hunters. Aberrant Hunters are a splat of their own and their business is writs and the wilds. An Enforcer's business is this street, tonight.

An Enforcer knows Purgatory's rhythms: which wards are quiet, who the players are in a dispute, when to step in and when to let something burn itself out. In a city where five factions each hold their own definition of order and the Council of Six has to be woken up to arbitrate, that judgement is the job. The law is twenty years old. Nobody agrees on what the Pax means on the ground. The Enforcer decides, one call at a time, and lives with it.

They are also the humans most likely to be first at something that is not a registered anything: a body that was not killed by a person, a face nobody can hold in memory, a door in a wall that has no door. What they do next is the most character-defining question this Profession offers.

**Attribute priority:** Physical 7, Social 5, Mental 3 **Required Background:** at least 1 dot of Contacts (watch, Vigiles, or faction security)

**Suggested build**

* Attributes: Str 3, Dex 4, Sta 3 · Cha 3, Man 3, App 2 · Per 3, Int 1, Wits 2  
* Talents (13): Alertness 3, Brawl 3, Intimidation 3, Athletics 2, Streetwise 2  
* Skills (9): Firearms 3, Drive 2, Melee 2, Larceny 1, Stealth 1  
* Knowledges (5): Investigation 2, Law 2, Politics 1  
* Backgrounds (5): Contacts 2, Allies 1, Resources 1, Mentor 1  
* Freebies (21): Stamina to 4 (5), Willpower to 5 (4), Grit \+1 (2), Firearms to 4 (2), Intimidation to 4 (2), Warranted (2), Read the Room (2), Prepared (1), Local (1)

**Practice: Known Quantity.** Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene, and the system works for you. Enter a sealed location, pull a Vigiles record or a harbour manifest, get a patrol to attend. No roll. It works because you are a recognised part of the apparatus, which means it also works on the record.

**Stigma: On the Roster.** Your shift pattern, your address, your colleagues and your file all exist, and in Purgatory a file is a tradeable good. Staff may always establish where you were and when. Any roll to conceal your identity or whereabouts from an institution takes a difficulty increase of 1\.

**Added trigger: wore the authority for it.** Mark if you used your standing to do something to a person that you would have refused to do without it.

**Enforcer Specific Merits:**

* *Warranted* (2): a recognised watch or Vigiles-auxiliary credential, so official cooperation is other people's default assumption.  
    
* *Read the Room* (2): once per scene, ask staff one factual question about imminent violence in the scene, such as who is armed or who is about to move.

**Faction fit.** The Order of Vigiles is the obvious home. The Sovereign Club pays better and asks worse. The Promethean Institute needs expedition security. An Enforcer with Ashen Court contacts is either compromised or very good at their job, and telling the difference is the campaign.

## **Broker**

*Merchants, fixers, and the people who know the price of everything*

Purgatory is a freeport where every Basilica's goods, agents and secrets cross the same crowded streets, and the Broker is what makes that legible. They trade goods, information, favours and access, sometimes all four before lunch. The waygates opened about ten years ago, and an older Broker remembers the before, when getting anything off the Isle meant paying the Governor's Toll in time or magic and being here was mostly a sentence.

The stock in trade is specific to this city: restricted heartglass off the Ruby Coast, goblin fruit out of the Bitterthorn, raptor ichor rendering into unusually pure leystone, Old World salvage, League currency and Basilica futures, and the one commodity Purgatory produces natively, which is obligation. Favours here are recorded, sometimes literally. The Ashen Court supplies and the Sovereign Club launders, they loathe each other publicly and cannot function apart, and the Broker stands in the gap taking a percentage of both.

A Broker's asset is not inventory but reputation. Building it takes a career. Losing it takes an afternoon. Most are not criminals in any meaningful sense. They are people who understand that rules are for those who cannot afford exceptions.

**Attribute priority:** Social 7, Mental 5, Physical 3 **Required Background:** at least 1 dot of Resources

**Suggested build**

* Attributes: Cha 3, Man 4, App 3 · Per 2, Int 3, Wits 3 · Str 2, Dex 2, Sta 2  
* Talents (13): Subterfuge 3, Empathy 3, Streetwise 3, Leadership 2, Expression 2  
* Skills (9): Etiquette 3, Larceny 2, Performance 2, Drive 1, Stealth 1  
* Knowledges (5): Finance 3, Investigation 1, Politics 1  
* Backgrounds (5): Resources 2, Contacts 2, Allies 1  
* Freebies (21): Manipulation to 5 (5), Willpower to 5 (4), Self-Control \+1 (2), Finance to 4 (2), Good Paper (2), The Long Memory (1), Unremarkable (2), Contacts to 3 (1), Allies to 2 (1), Resources to 3 (1)

**Practice: Everything Has a Price.** Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene, and name a specific item, service or piece of information reachable from the City-Isle. It can be found. Staff set the price and the delivery time, and you decide whether to pay. The question is never whether, only what it costs.

**Stigma: The Ledger Runs Both Ways.** Every use of the Practice creates one Obligation you owe somebody. While you carry three or more outstanding, any roll to secure new credit or a new favour takes a difficulty increase of 1\. Staff call them due at their own convenience, and this is Purgatory, so at least one of your creditors is not a person, does not forget, and treats "nothing is free, least of all the things that claim to be" as a statement of law rather than a proverb.

**Added trigger: sold something you said you would not sell.** Write your lines down at creation. The trigger is worthless if nobody knows where they were.

**Broker Specific Merits:**

* *Good Paper* (2): once per scene, produce credible documentation for one claim. Whether it is forged or merely convenient is your business.  
    
* *The Long Memory* (1): difficulty drops by 1 on any roll concerning who owes what to whom, which in Purgatory is most rolls.

**Faction fit.** The Sovereign Club if you want to be respectable, the Ashen Court if you want to be useful. The Promethean Institute is a reliable customer with grant money and no sense of price.

## **Physician**

*Doctors, surgeons, and the people who keep everyone alive*

Physicians are indispensable anywhere. In Purgatory they are something more. The Isle produces injuries that nothing in a textbook covers: ichor exposure from the refineries, goblin fruit in a patient who did not know what they were eating, whatever the Ruby Coast does to living tissue that lingers near it, and the slow effects of an ambient aetheric field that no Hearthstone is flattening. Every other Basilica's clinical baseline assumes a stasis field. Yours does not, and the difference shows up in your patients.

The pharmacopoeia is alchemical, which means Antananarivo reagents at Antananarivo prices, and half your clinical equipment is paratech that fails when the weather turns strange.

Post-Pax, your patients include BRDs, and their physiology is not in the textbook either. Some of them will not survive an honest chart. That is the whole of this Profession's moral problem. A wound recorded as inflicted by a Nightfolk starts a Vigiles inquiry, an inquiry can end in a writ of execution, and the Hunters do not investigate twice. What you write down decides who lives. Every faction knows it, and every faction keeps a quiet relationship with a Physician.

**Attribute priority:** Mental 7, Social 5, Physical 3 **Required Background:** at least 1 dot of Resources (supplies, instruments, reagent access)

**Suggested build**

* Attributes: Per 3, Int 4, Wits 3 · Cha 3, Man 2, App 3 · Str 1, Dex 3, Sta 2  
* Talents (13): Empathy 3, Alertness 2, Expression 2, Leadership 2, Awareness 2, Athletics 1, Subterfuge 1  
* Skills (9): Crafts 2, Etiquette 2, Drive 2, Firearms 1, Larceny 1, Stealth 1  
* Knowledges (5): Medicine 3, Science 2  
* Backgrounds (5): Resources 2, Contacts 2, Allies 1  
* Freebies (21): Willpower to 5 (4), Conscience \+1 (2), Grit \+1 (2), Medicine to 4 (2), Academics 1 (2), Investigation 1 (2), Licensed Practitioner (2), Steady Hands (2), Prepared (1), Contacts to 3 (1), Resources to 3 (1)

**Practice: Everyone Needs a Doctor.** Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene, and reach a person or their file through clinical channels without a roll. A consultation with someone in Vigiles custody. A restricted record. Unchallenged passage through a ward or a quarantine line. Medicine opens doors that warrants do not.

**Stigma: Everyone Knows What You Know.** You hold information about people, and people know you hold it. Expect to be regularly brought someone who wants it: a Vigiles investigator with a form, a faction with a favour, a patient's family with a grievance. Refusing has consequences that have nothing to do with medicine. Disclosing costs you the thing the Profession runs on, and may cost your patient their head.

**Added trigger: broke a confidence.** Mark if you disclosed what a patient told you, or what you found in them, to someone with no right to it. Whatever the pressure, and whatever it bought.

**Physician Specific Merits:**

* *Licensed Practitioner* (2): clinical standing, so you are admitted anywhere medicine is happening, including a Vigiles holding cell.  
    
* *Steady Hands* (2): difficulty drops by 1 on Medicine rolls made under fire, in the dark, or on a boat.

**Faction fit.** The Circle of Bone and Ivy will make a grudging exception for a useful skillset, and the Order of Vigiles will not stop asking. The Promethean Institute funds research and will want the interesting cases; whether the interesting cases consented is your problem rather than theirs.

## **Archivist**

*Historians, scholars, and the keepers of what is known*

Most pre-Sundering knowledge was destroyed. The people who preserve and transmit what remains are not clerks. They are performing an act of survival. Archivists are historians, scholars, librarians and researchers: the people who know where to find what is known, and increasingly what the gaps mean.

The gaps are enormous. The Interstice has no record at all—ten years in which evidence of human activity simply ceases—and survivors came out the other side with a decade unaccounted for. The Itinerant century exists mostly in caravan oral accounts. And Purgatory has the worst record of any League city. It is barely eight decades old, its founding population was exiles and fugitives, and its whole civic manner holds that a stated origin is never challenged and never believed. Your archive rests on false names, and you know it.

What is here instead is volume: League correspondence passing through the freeport, faction ledgers, harbour manifests, Institute expedition reports, and the ruins on the Ghostlit Coast that match no known architecture and never have. There is also the thing every Archivist eventually notices, which is that the record contains people who appear across generations without ageing. That used to be a scandal and is now simply BRD registration, except for the ones whose names predate any registry.

**Attribute priority:** Mental 7, Social 5, Physical 3 **Required Background:** at least 1 dot of Contacts (scholarly, Institute, or archival)

**Suggested build**

* Attributes: Per 3, Int 4, Wits 3 · Cha 3, Man 3, App 2 · Str 1, Dex 2, Sta 3  
* Talents (13): Expression 3, Alertness 2, Empathy 2, Subterfuge 2, Awareness 2, Athletics 1, Leadership 1

**Practice: The Record Remembers.** Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene, and locate a specific piece of historical, legal or administrative information held anywhere in the human record reachable from the City-Isle: manifests, ledgers, League correspondence, Institute holdings, technically restricted material. ## **Shepherd**

*Community leaders, priests, and the voices the people follow*

Every other Basilica has a cultural core. Purgatory does not, and that is the point. It has an exile creole instead of a heritage: elaborate courtesy over total mutual suspicion, obligation tracked like currency, and an allergy to questions about where anyone is from. That leaves the Shepherd holding the one thing the city itself lacks. A person who can build a real community out of dockside wards, Mangrove stilt-hamlets and Grenvalden-descended families has made something no faction can purchase and no Art can counterfeit.

Shepherds here come from everywhere: Salamanca-trained clergy on a long posting, ward representatives who have been fighting the Council since before the waygates, spirit-mediators trained on the Kathmandu model, and people who arrived and gradually became who everyone went to. Some sell threshold wards on the Mangrove road, and an honest Shepherd knows which of their stock is functional, which came from an Artificer, and which is comfort. A mundane human works no magic, and pretending otherwise gets parishioners killed.

Then there is the Grenveld, where no settlement may hold more than two hundred and the two hundred and first drops dead, and where much of the City-Isle's native human stock comes from. Every Grenvalden family has done the arithmetic on a birth. If you shepherd those people, you have been asked questions no doctrine answers.

**Attribute priority:** Social 7, Mental 5, Physical 3 **Required Background:** at least 1 dot of Allies (community members)

**Suggested build**

* Attributes: Cha 4, Man 3, App 3 · Per 3, Int 3, Wits 2 · Str 2, Dex 2, Sta 2  
* Talents (13): Leadership 3, Empathy 3, Expression 3, Alertness 1, Awareness 1, Intimidation 1, Subterfuge 1  
* Skills (9): Etiquette 3, Performance 2, Crafts 1, Drive 1, Survival 1, Animal Ken 1  
* Knowledges (5): Politics 2, Academics 2, Arcane 1  
* Backgrounds (5): Allies 2, Contacts 2, Resources 1  
* Freebies (21): Charisma to 5 (5), Willpower to 6 (6), Conscience \+1 (2), Medicine 1 (2), Recognised Standing (2), The Room Quiets (2), Allies to 3 (1), Contacts to 3 (1)

**Practice: The Voice the People Hear.** Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene, and call on your community for one specific collective action. A watch on the ward tonight. Forty people at a Council session. A door that stays closed to someone. A crowd that disperses instead of forming. They act. Scale grows with your Allies rating, your reputation, and which groups you have actually stood up for.

**Stigma: Held to the Standard.** Your community Background dots exist only as long as their trust does. Break it by your own choice, whether you lied to them, traded on them, or cut a deal that cost them, and the dots are gone with no XP credited back, because a Background lost to your own decision is not a Background lost to plot. Rebuilding is staff-gated and slow.

**Added trigger: spent their trust on something that was not for them.** Mark it.

**Shepherd Specific Merits:**

* *Recognised Standing* (2): licensed clergy, a ward representative, or simply known, so your word carries where the community can hear it.  
    
* *The Room Quiets* (2): difficulty drops by 1 on Leadership rolls addressing ten or more people.

**Faction fit.** Every faction wants you and none of them will say why plainly. The Sovereign Club offers funding for your hall, the Ashen Court offers protection you did not ask for, the Vigiles want you to keep your people calm, and the Circle wants to know which of your congregation is talking to something. Playing a Shepherd means deciding, repeatedly, what your community's trust is worth while being courted by people whose interest you cannot account for.

## **Ghostwright**

*Glyphcode mechanics, salvagers, and the people who make dead technology work*

Paratech runs the world, and the Glyphcode Standards mean a device etched in Bogotá can be repaired in Chino. A Ghostwright is who repairs it. They are the mechanics, salvagers, hackers and improvisers who keep the grid alive, bring Old World scitech back from the dead, and jury-rig things from leystone offcuts that the guilds would price at a year's wages.

Know the boundary, because it defines the Profession. A Ghostwright works no magic. Novel enchantment is the trade of Artificers and Sorcerers. What Glyphcode literacy buys is everything else: reading a formula, repairing it, rerouting it, bypassing it, cannibalising one device into another, and diagnosing why a conduit that passes inspection is quietly wrong. In a world where the qualified practitioners are few, expensive, and mostly employed already, that is not a lesser skill. It is why the lights stay on in the wards nobody bills for.

It also runs straight into the illegal trades. Registration-evasion work—a paratech piece built to hide an inhuman feature from a Vigiles inspection—is the most in-demand and most dangerous product a Ghostwright can offer, because the Pax is built on being able to see what someone is. Add Glyphcode forgery, unlicensed grid taps, salvage that was never declared, and red leystone, which pound for pound holds ten to twelve times what purple does and wants to merge with living things, so nobody sane builds with it and somebody always does.

The parties who notice are the Promethean Institute, which owns discovery and licensing and is on cordial terms with everyone; the Order of Vigiles, whose remit is paracausal crime; and the Sovereign Club, which funds the Institute and dislikes competition. Their attention starts friendly. That is the frightening part.

**Attribute priority:** Mental 7, Physical 5, Social 3 **Required Background:** at least 1 dot of Contacts (salvage, grid crews, or the unlicensed trade)

**Suggested build**

* Attributes: Per 3, Int 4, Wits 3 · Str 2, Dex 4, Sta 2 · Cha 2, Man 2, App 2  
* Talents (13): Alertness 3, Athletics 2, Awareness 2, Streetwise 2, Subterfuge 2, Expression 1, Empathy 1  
* Skills (9): Crafts 3, Larceny 3, Stealth 2, Drive 1  
* Knowledges (5): Technology 3, Computer 2  
* Backgrounds (5): Contacts 2, Resources 2, Alternate Identity 1  
* Freebies (21): Dexterity to 5 (5), Willpower to 5 (4), Technology to 4 (2), Computer to 3 (2), Science 1 (2), Grafted (2), Salvage Eye (1), Unremarkable (2), Resources to 3 (1)

**Practice: If It Runs, I Can Run It.** Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene, and roll Intelligence plus Technology at difficulty 7 to get into a specific paratech or scitech system without the credentials or physical access it requires: a grid node, a Vigiles ward-lock, an Institute vault seal, a salvaged Old World device that has not run in two centuries. Success means you are in. It does not mean nobody noticed. This is an ordinary roll, wound penalties and all, and it can fail like one.

**Stigma: The Institute Takes an Interest.** Track a counter from 0 to 5, rising by 1 in any cycle in which you used the Practice or did significant unlicensed work. At 1 or 2, somebody has a file and a very pleasant recruiter knows your name. At 3 or 4 the interest is active: an Institute contact who is neither hostile nor going away, and a Vigiles inspection that is technically routine. At 5 it converts. The Institute makes an offer that is not really one, or the Vigiles make a case, or the Sovereign Club decides your workshop is a competitor. The counter fades by 1 per full month of clean work.

**Added trigger: gave someone up to keep your own name clear.** Mark if you handed over a person, a workshop, or a location to Institute or Vigiles attention in order to stay out of it yourself.

**Ghostwright Specific Merits:**

* *Grafted* (2): a physiology that accepts modification, and the Glyphcode literacy to keep salvaged Old World cyberware running on paratech life support. Required before any implant. It includes one cosmetic or utility implant that confers no dice.  
    
* *Salvage Eye* (1): difficulty drops by 1 to identify Old World hardware or a leystone grade by sight, and to guess what a device was for.

**Faction fit.** The Promethean Institute is the legitimate path, well-funded and careless with other people's lives. The Ashen Court pays cash for the evasion work. The Circle of Bone and Ivy will keep you on retainer for when something they found starts humming, ticking, or otherwise acting odd.

## **A note on playing a Mundane**

Nightfolk are more powerful in direct terms. More durable, more capable in specific domains, less exposed. Your Willpower 5 defence will lose to a capstone Art most nights it is tested.

What humans have is different in kind. Roots, in a city that has none. The whole XP budget pointed at a single sheet, which after a year means you are simply better at being a person than anyone else in the room. A Stability track with nothing eating it. Access to the institutions that actually run the City-Isle, used openly, by someone with a legitimate place in them.

And the Pax. Read it as a human rather than as a Nightfolk. It exists because 6,000 Nightfolk died in a single panic and four times that many humans died causing it; it holds because both sides remember. Nightfolk need it more than you do. They are registered and you are not. They can lose their status and you cannot. A Physician forty years into practice knows things no elder does. An Archivist a decade into the manifests knows this city from the inside. A Shepherd with their ward's real trust can do something no Art replicates.

The question is not how to compete with the Nightfolk. It is what ordinary people do with persistence, community, and a life lived in the open, in a city where nothing is free, least of all the things which claim to be.

*Humanity survived the end of the world through sheer persistence. You are the proof that it worked.*
