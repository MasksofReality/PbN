*When the end of the world came, it was neither quick nor remotely merciful. The world took decades to die, and worse still, God was a no-show on the apocalypse's VIP guest list. But we are not telling a story about the end of the world. This is a story about what happens once the end has come, gone, and the world still keeps on turning.*
## The Premise

**TL;DR:** *It’s **200 TE** (roughly the mid-23rd century CE). The old world died slowly and painfully, and no god arrived to stop it. Humanity survives in fortified city-states called **Basilicas**, protected by magic and surrounded by monster-infested wilds. Nightfolk live hidden: and, since the Masquerade fell, openly registered: among them. This isn’t the story of the apocalypse; It’s the story of the people living in the half drowned world it left behind.*

The end of the world was neither quick nor merciful: it took **decades** to die, and then the world kept turning anyway. Humanity no longer dominates the planet. It shares the ruins with **aberrations** and resurrected prehistoric predators.

Survival now rests on the old reliables: **strength in numbers, tall walls, and stubborn will.** The nations of the old world are gone. In their place stand the **Basilicas**: fortified city-state metropolises, the only places humanity can truly live and grow. Between one Basilica and the next, the land is wild and deadly. People born outside the walls will sell *everything*: down to their souls: for a chance to live inside them.

**Population:** No more than **500 million** worldwide: A mere **5%** of humanity’s ancient peak.

## State of the World

The world in the 23rd century is so changed as to be unrecognizable. A full two-thirds of the land has sunk, consuming Europe, Australia, Japan, and much of the Americas below shallow seas—Any city that was below 700m above sea level pre sundering is lost to the tides. The land left is largely uninhabited, filled with aberrants, various forms of radiation hazards, and strange anomalous phenomena more often lethal than helpful—such as the universally feared wyrdstorms that twist reality and birth aberrants.

The only safe places from such hazards are places behind a hearthstone’s defensive barriers, as they are able to repel the worst of the Aberrants and deflect Wyrdstorms. Such settlements are rare, countable in the few hundreds globally, and all are anchored to one of the Basilicas, the city-state metropolises that hold more people between them than all the safe small cities and villages combined. Of course, these barriers are not absolute in the face of a powerful and dogged enemy—The fate of Mount Hagen’s people taught the world that all too well.

### **The Basilicas Today**

*What follows is common knowledge — the reputation each Basilica carries in the mouths of traders, travelers, and tavern gossips across the League. Your character has probably heard most of this, whether or not any of it is fair.*

#### *Addis Ababa — “The Granary”*

The Ethiopian highlands became the best real estate on the planet when the waters rose: vast, fertile, temperate, and high above the tide. Addis Ababa feeds a hungry world and knows exactly what that is worth. Its Highland Granaries are strategic infrastructure on par with any Hearthstone, its coffee monopoly is enforced with a straight face and real warships, and its reputation for scrupulous neutrality makes it the League's favorite venue for arbitration. More treaties have been signed over its coffee ceremonies than in any council chamber. Cross Addis and no one will attack you. You will simply, slowly, stop being invited to eat.
The city is highland Ethiopian to the bone, Amhara and Oromo in roughly equal weight, with Gurage trade families disproportionately represented in the Granary guilds and Tigrayan lineages holding much of the officer class. Around them sit Eritrean and Tigrayan highlanders whose own plateau came through the drowning nearly intact; Ethiopian-Somali communities of the eastern uplands, a highland-interior people with no surviving coastal kin; Yemeni merchant houses established by ship in the Incense Fleet era; and small Italian-descended clerical families whose ancestors never left.
If you have business here, learn the coffee ceremony. It is a procedure. Three rounds, and a treaty that fails to reach the third round has failed. Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo fasting calendars govern what the Granaries release and when, which means League grain politics runs on a liturgical clock. Land tenure is remembered in lineage terms centuries after the paperwork burned, so "whose field was that" is a live question and occasionally a lethal one. And the Afar, whose homeland lay below the old sea level and is now open ocean, are remembered here in songs sung by people with no one left to sing them to.

*Languages heard here: Amharic, Oromo, Tigrinya, Somali, English, Arabic.*


#### *Antananarivo — “The Far Island”*

The most isolated Basilica, and the strangest of them. Madagascar's biosphere was already like nowhere else on Earth, and two centuries of wyrdstorms made it something adjacent to a fever dream. The highland city rules an island of towering baobab-things and singing megafauna. Its alchemists have no peer anywhere, because reagents that grow nowhere else in the world grow within a week's terrifying trek of the walls, and its exports are bought sight-unseen at any price. Visitors find the locals warm, unhurried, and unsettlingly casual about the impossible. Getting there is the hard part. Some years the storm-belts let no ships through at all, and Antananarivo does not mind.
The culture is Merina, of the Imerina plateau, with Betsileo terrace-farming communities as the second pillar, and overwhelmingly so: the central plateau was the whole of Madagascar's survival, which makes this one of the most culturally unified Basilicas on Earth. Everyone else here is small and tight-knit. Families descended from Betsimisaraka, Sakalava and Antandroy labor migrants who happened to be working in the plateau cities. Comorian families from the high slopes of Karthala. Réunionnais creole-speakers from the mountain cirques, one of very few Indian Ocean island peoples to come through the whole, who arrive by ship in the years when the storm-belts allow it.
Razana, ancestor veneration, is the organizing principle of Malagasy alchemy: formulae are inherited rather than published, and a reagent lineage is a family lineage. Famadihana, the turning of the bones, has become a fraught rite in a world with confirmed undead, and it carries a further weight here, because many plateau families are the last surviving branch of a lineage whose coastal cousins all died in a single decade. Fady, taboo, functions as a safety protocol. The list of things one does not do in the wyrd-forest is long, specific, arbitrary-sounding, and correct.

*Languages heard here: Malagasy, French, English, Mauritian Creole, Swahili.*


#### *Bengaluru — “The Glyphcode City”*

The old tech city became the new tech city, and only the substrate changed. Bengaluru's engineering guilds wrote the Glyphcode Standards, the shared notation that lets a paratech device etched in Bogotá be repaired in Chino, and that one act of standardization made the city indispensable to every Basilica on the map. The rising seas turned the plateau's edge into a dramatic cliff-coastline, and its harbor-lifts lower cargo hundreds of meters from city to sea in great counterweighted cages. The city is meritocratic, fast-talking, and perpetually mid-argument, and its guildhalls treat a design flaw the way other cities treat a blood feud.
The core is Kannadiga plateau culture, the city itself plus the Kodagu, Chikmagalur and Mysore uplands, holding one of the few South Asian literary traditions with an unbroken living readership. The Tamil population is very large, descended from the hill-station plantation workforce of the Nilgiris, Munnar and Valparai: numerous, organized, historically exploited, and now indispensable to the guilds. Beyond them are Malayali communities from the Wayanad and Idukki highlands; Badaga, Kodava and Toda peoples of the Nilgiris punching far above their old weight; small Telugu and Bengali remnants from the eastern hill pockets and the Darjeeling ridge; caravan-descended Hindi- and Urdu-speaking quarters whose ancestors walked south from the Himalayan hill cities across the Itinerant century; and resident Tabrizi and Persian brokerage houses.
Jati has metamorphosed into guild lineage: hereditary specialization with an open apprenticeship door, which the city insists is meritocracy and outsiders find suspiciously tidy. Glyphcode's base notation descends from Kannada script, a fact Bengaluru mentions constantly and every other Basilica pretends not to hear. Argument here is affection, and a guildhall that is not shouting has given up. The Tamil estate families keep their own reckoning of who owes what to whom, and it does not match the guild ledgers.

*Languages heard here: Kannada, Tamil, Malayalam, English, Hindi, Urdu, Persian, Telugu.*

#### *Bogotá — “The Emerald Vault"*

New Tenochtitlan's great rival on the Andean spine, and it stings Bogotá deeply that the rivalry is not considered an equal one. Its Hearthstone is seated in the old Salt Cathedral, a consecrated underground labyrinth that doubles as the most secure vault in the League, where Basilica elites and magnates store what they cannot afford to lose. Above ground, guilds of goldsmith-artificers carry an unbroken pre-Columbian craft tradition into paratech, and the emerald-and-leystone exchange sets prices half the world trades by. Bogotá plays the long game and usually wins it.
Andean Colombian cachaco culture sets the register: formal, indirect, ceremonious, layered over a deliberate Muisca revival. The Salt Cathedral was a Muisca salt works before it was a church, and the goldsmith-artificer guilds have spent two centuries reclaiming that descent as their charter. Boyacense and Santandereano highlanders make up much of the population, with Antioqueño paisa merchant families running much of the exchange floor. Afro-Colombian communities of the Cauca and Valle highlands live here with no Caribbean kin left at all. Andean Venezuelan lineages come out of Mérida and Ecuadorian Quichua traders down the cordillera. And there is a wealthy, restless quarter of highland Brazilians from the surviving Planalto: a maritime people cut off from the Andes by the new sea where the rainforest used to be, with money, ships, and no Basilica of their own.
Courtesy is the weapon of choice. Usted is used at every register, including for insults. Catholic and Muisca practice are syncretized past the point of separation, and the Salt Cathedral's clergy are also its vault-wardens. The long game is a stated cultural value; Bogotano families measure plans in generations, which is exactly why the New Tenochtitlan rivalry stings so much. Bogotá is winning slowly and nobody has noticed yet.

*Languages heard here: Spanish, English, Quechua, Portuguese (Brazil), Haitian Creole.*


#### *Calgary — “Land of the Beast Lords”*

The prairie survived the drowning of the world by simple virtue of altitude, and the people who resettled it kept the old ranching soul. They just changed what they ranch. Where every other Basilica answers the aberrant question with walls and harpoons, Calgary answers it with a rope, inhuman patience, and a bit of magic. Its beast-tamers have spent three generations proving that some of the Wyrd's monsters can be gentled. The city is full of tamed aberrant beasts, most famously its herds of six-legged fire-breathing bison: Emberbreaths formally, Smoke Puffers locally, prairie drakes in fanciful travelers' tales. Most people think them a myth until they stand at the barrier's edge and watch living proof grazing placidly under its shimmer.
The wyrdtaming techniques are jealously guarded, and the annual Stampede draws travelers from three continents. It is part festival and part public trial of nerve, where tamers break new stock before a roaring crowd. The rest of the League regard the whole enterprise as magnificent lunacy, right up until they place their orders for draft-beasts that can haul a caravan through terrain that eats trucks and people with impunity. Calgarians have the particular calm of people who work daily with things that could eat them, and they will shake anyone's hand, Nightfolk included, with utter sincerity. The only questions that matter here are your work ethic and whether you are good with animals.
Foothills ranching culture runs the place, and it is a real fusion. Anglo-Canadian stockmen, Métis, and Blackfoot (Siksikaitsitapi) horse traditions collapsed into one working practice during the Itinerant Period, when nobody could afford to keep three separate ways of handling animals. Calgary's dominance over the surviving prairie is total, because the lower prairie cities are all beneath the water. Nêhiyaw (Cree) communities of the foothills are diminished but present, their eastern relations lost with the low plains. The Métis settlements here are now the entirety of the nation, the Red River heartland having drowned. Punjabi Sikh families brought the langar tradition that became the model for the city's public feeding halls, and the rest is filled out by Chinese-Canadian ranching and rail-descended families, long-settled Filipino communities, Mexican-descended and Latin American ranch families of the Alberta–Montana–Wyoming high plains, and Franco-Albertan parish remnants and Métis French speakers, all that is left of a French-speaking Canada.
Wyrdtaming is taught as horsemanship rather than sorcery, and the techniques are family property, guarded like ranch brands. The Stampede is an initiation with an audience. Nobody asks what you are; they ask what you can handle. The Sikh free-kitchen ethic and prairie potluck hospitality merged so thoroughly that refusing food in Calgary reads as an accusation.

*Languages heard here: English, Punjabi (Gurmukhi), Cantonese, Chinese (Simplified), Spanish, Tagalog, French (Canada).*


#### *Chino — “The Shrine on the Shore"*

When the seas took Japan they left an archipelago of alpine peaks, and Chino sits at its new waterline. The city grew around the ancient Lake Suwa shrine complex, and its paratech is famously wound through with shrine-craft: wards written as prayer-strips, Hearthstone conduits blessed as often as they are etched. Its wealth comes from the salvage fleets, hard-eyed divers who descend into the drowned sprawl of old Tokyo and Nagoya to bring up Old World relics, and who do not talk about what else lives down there. Reserved and ritualistic, Chino is also among the most Nightfolk-tolerant Basilicas in the League, because the line between a registered BRD and a local kami has always been politely blurry here.
The culture is alpine Japanese, centred on the Suwa shrine complex, and it carries far more than a mountain village should. The Nagano highlands were the old nation's designated wartime redoubt, and the long war put them to use. The government's apparatus, its archives, its dispersed industry, and mass evacuees from the coastal cities were all in these mountains when the world went still, so Chino inherited a nation's administration along with its shrines. Okinawan and Amami evacuee-descended families form a small, tight, memory-keeping community whose islands are gone in a way the mainland is not, and who know it. Ainu families of the Hidaka mountains are proportionally more visible now than before the collapse. Korean highlanders of the Taebaek and Sobaek ranges have joined long-settled Zainichi lineages in the industrial towns, Nikkei Brazilian families from the old factory workforce are among the only people alive with living relatives on two continents, and Chinese salvage crews from the western plateau came for the wrecks and stayed.
The Onbashira, the periodic felling and raising of great pillars, is now the Hearthstone's renewal rite. It still kills people, and that is still considered part of it. Ward-writing is liturgical work: a conduit is blessed as well as etched, and an unblessed one counts as defective no matter how well it functions. The ama diving taboos govern the salvage fleets, who will not name what they saw in the drowned sprawl. Some crews are recovering their own families' houses.

*Languages heard here: Japanese, Korean, Chinese (Simplified), English, Portuguese (Brazil), Cantonese.*


#### *City-isle of Purgatory — “City of Exiles and Mysteries”*

The only major settlement on the Wandering Isle, and the strangest member of the League by a comfortable margin. Purgatory began as a dumping ground, the polite oubliette where the Basilicas sent the too-powerful-to-kill, back when the Governor's Toll made residency a life sentence. Its founding stock of exiles, fugitives, and inconvenient people never quite washed out of its character. The waygates changed everything a decade ago. Purgatory is now a freeport where every Basilica's goods, agents, and secrets pass through the same crowded streets, governed day to day by the Council of Six and its five Grand Factions under the distant eyes of the Triune. It is the most openly supernatural city on Earth, the easiest place in the world to disappear, and the worst place in the world to assume nobody important is watching. They say everyone in Purgatory came from somewhere else, everyone has an angle, nothing is how it seems, and nothing is free, especially the things that claim to be.
Purgatory has no Hearthstone and never has. The Governor's hold on the Wandering Isle admits no competing anchor, and a Hearthstone's stasis field is exactly that. Every attempt to seat one has failed, in ways the Council no longer discusses. What protects the city is the Governor's own regard, which is a less comforting arrangement than a barrier you can watch shimmer. Two things follow. The ambient aetheric field is dense, because nothing is flattening it, and Nightfolk are not merely tolerated here but comfortable. That is why they came. It is why they keep coming.
There is no cultural core, and that is the point. Purgatory has an exile creole instead of a heritage: elaborate courtesy over total mutual suspicion, obligation tracked like currency, and an allergy to questions about where anyone is from. Every person on Earth is represented in small numbers, and none of them are dominant. The census is a work of fiction that the Council finds useful.
Favors are recorded, sometimes literally. Nobody's stated origin is challenged, and nobody's stated origin is believed. Gaelic and Haitian folk protections are commercial products here; you can buy a working threshold ward from a grandmother on the Mangrove road. "Nothing is free, especially things that claim to be" is taught to children as a rhyme.

*Languages heard here: English, Irish, Scots Gaelic, Haitian Creole, Spanish, Chinese (Simplified), Cantonese.*

#### *New Tenochtitlan — “The First Hearth"*

The birthplace of the Hearthbind Ritual and the oldest Basilica, built proudly atop old Mexico City, and it never lets anyone forget either fact. Its Sorcery Academies are the finest in the world, its sorcerer clans are old money and older grudges, and its politics are a blood sport conducted in perfect courtesy. Beyond the barrier, anomalous jungle has swallowed the valley and things that should have stayed extinct hunt in it, which makes dinosaur-hunting both a rite of passage and a lucrative industry. If you want to learn magic, you come here. If you want to survive learning magic, you learn the politics first.
Central Mexican highland (chilango) culture fused with a proud, deliberate Nahua aristocratic revival. The old barrios and sorcerer estates organize around calpulli lineage-guilds and patrician pipiltin houses. Spanish and Classical Nahuatl share equal civic prestige, and the city's identity rests on an unshakable conviction that civilization survived solely because Mexico taught humanity how to make a Hearthstone. Oaxacan highlanders, Zapotec and Mixtec lineages whose mountain valleys survived intact, dominate the precious metal and glyph-carving academies. Purépecha artificers come from the Michoacán highlands, Maya highland families from Chiapas and the Guatemalan cordillera, and Otomí and Mazahua agricultural cooperatives work the elevated chinampas. Chicano and Mexican-American caravan-descended families trace their ancestors' flight from the drowning of the northern coasts and desert basins, and small Central American highland enclaves hold the volcanic spine.
Día de los Muertos here is an active season of necromantic diplomacy, ancestral council, and contract renewal with spirits who hold real property rights. Nobody in this city treats it as symbolism. Academic duels between rival academy houses, el duelo de cátedra, follow strict 18th-century etiquette that masks lethal stakes. Dinosaur hunting outside the barrier is the elite's primary status sport, and a mantle lined with raptor feathers carries more weight at court than a League diploma. The rivalry with Bogotá is bitter because New Tenochtitlan regards Bogotá as an upstart copycat.

*Languages heard here: Spanish, Nahuatl (Eastern Huasteca), Zapotec, Yucatec Maya, Qʼeqchiʼ, Mam, English.*


#### *Kathmandu — “The City of Ten Thousand Shrines"*

TThe Himalaya shrugged off the drowning of the world, and Kathmandu endures in its shadow much as it always has: older than the catastrophe, and certain it will outlast the next one. It is the spiritual capital of the Third Era. Pilgrims of every faith and species climb to it and regard it as something close to holy ground, because the veil between flesh and spirit is thin along the high passes. Disputes between the material and the immaterial get settled here, by mediators who take tea with ghosts. The surface is serene. Underneath runs a bazaar of secrets, relics, and favors owed to things without bodies.
Newar valley civilization sets the shape of the place: the courtyard-and-shrine urbanism, the festival calendar, and the guthi trust associations that maintain both. The guthi model is the direct ancestor of the city's spirit-mediation orders, because a body that exists in perpetuity to maintain an obligation is exactly the right instrument for a contract with something that does not die. Bahun-Chhetri administrative lineages run much of the paperwork. Tamang, Gurung and Magar hill communities fill the valley, Sherpa and Bhotiya high-pass families run the caravans, and Tibetan trade and exile communities make the plateau relationship the city's most important external tie. A Maithil and Tharu remnant, descended from Terai families already working in the Valley, is the whole of what survives of the Nepali plains, whose people drowned where they stood.
The Kumari tradition, a living child vessel of the divine, never lapsed, and in an era of confirmed spirits it is taken very seriously by parties on both sides of the veil. Tea with a ghost is a documented legal procedure, with witnesses and a fee schedule. Underneath the serenity sits a hard fact: with the Terai gone, Kathmandu cannot feed itself and never will. Its holiness is monetized because its harvest is not. The city's neutrality holds because everyone wants continued access.

*Languages heard here: Nepali, Newari, Tibetan, English, Hindi, Maithili, Bhojpuri.*

#### *Johannesburg — “The Deep City”*

Johannesburg was built on the deepest mines humanity ever dug, and the Wyrd found them before the miners came back. Its shafts now yield the richest green leystone seams known, feeding foundries that make it the heavy-industrial heart of the League. If a Basilica runs a machine bigger than a wagon, the odds are Johannesburg's factories built it. There is one exception, and it grates endlessly: New Colombia's stranglehold on paratech weapon research and production is considered peerless. Down in the mines, they say, the deep levels are cordoned and the miners' unions keep their own exorcists on payroll. Everyone on the Gold Reef knows the rule. You do not follow lights, voices, or old friends below the safety line.
Reef mining culture is an industrial creole. Zulu and Basotho traditions form the twin pillars, and Lesotho, every inch of it high above the water, came through the drowning more completely than almost any territory on Earth. But the organizing culture is the shaft crew: cross-ethnic, union-structured, ritually superstitious, and fiercely bounded against management. Pedi communities spread across the Limpopo highveld. Shona are here in very large numbers, the Zimbabwean highlands having survived nearly whole. Swazi come from the Eswatini uplands and Xhosa from the Eastern Cape interior with their coastal relations lost, and Afrikaner highveld and Free State families work alongside Indian South African and Cape Malay communities long resident on the Reef itself, whose Durban and Cape kin all died in the same decade. Tsonga holds the Limpopo lowveld margins.
Fanakalo, the old mine pidgin, survives as the working cant of the deep levels. You can hold a shift meeting in it without any two crew sharing a mother tongue. Stokvel rotating-credit clubs finance everything from a funeral to a wildcat leystone claim. Every crew retains a sangoma-trained exorcist on the payroll, and the union contract specifies it. That rule about lights, voices, and old friends is taught as a chant.

*Languages heard here: Zulu, Sesotho, Afrikaans, English, Sepedi, Shona, Xhosa, Swati, Tswana, Tsonga.*


#### *La Paz–El Alto — “The Roof of the World's Ruin"*

The highest Basilica on Earth, terraced into a canyon rim so far above the new seas that the drowning of the world is, locally, a rumor. Paratech gondola lines that never stop running stitch the districts together, and thin-air foundries turn out alloys no lowland forge can match. The famous Witches' Market operates in the open here and always has. La Paz never really enforced either Masquerade, and its sorcerer-vendors will sell you a blessing, a curse, or an honest appraisal of which one you actually need. The city is insular and vertiginous, and superstitious in the way of people who turned out to be right.
Aymara culture dominates. El Alto is and always was an Aymara city, and the Third Era only strengthened that. Quechua communities form the second pillar, running the length of a cordillera that lost almost nobody, with a criollo-mestizo Paceño commercial class in the canyon below. Afro-Bolivian communities of the Yungas valleys are neighbours and trading partners rather than refugees, their terraces never touched by the water. Andean Peruvian lineages from Cusco, Puno and Huancayo joined overland along the spine, Chilean altiplano herders come up from the south, and a small Guaraní population descends from lowland labor migrants who happened to be working in the highland cities when the world froze.
Yatiri ritual specialists were never illegal here and never pretended otherwise. The Witches' Market is a licensed trade with a guild, a price list, and a complaints process. Ch'alla libation precedes any significant act, including the signing of League contracts, which visiting delegates find charming until they realize that omitting it voids the deal socially. Ayllu kinship-reciprocity governs the gondola cooperatives. The city's smugness is earned. It lost almost nobody, it declined to enforce either Masquerade, and the Pax Nocturnorum eventually agreed with it.

*Languages heard here: Aymara, Quechua, Spanish, English, Guarani.*


#### *Mount Hagen — “The Fallen Bastion”*

Seated in the Melanesian highlands, Mount Hagen was once one of the Great Bastions. The Hag-Queen Rangda besieged it in 130 TE, seventy years ago, and its peers left it to fall. It was held for three years. It is spoken of rarely and traveled to never, because behind its stolen barrier it has become a hell on earth where humans are raised as livestock. Its fate is the reason the League exists, and the name every Basilican diplomat reaches for when an ally hesitates.
Before the fall it was Melanesian highland country: Melpa, Enga and Simbu peoples, organized around moka competitive gift-exchange and wantok obligation networks, with Tok Pisin as the unifying tongue. These were among the luckiest people on Earth, a high, fertile, populous country that lost almost nothing to the water. A small community descended from Australian tableland survivors had made the short crossing, all that reached these highlands from a continent otherwise emptied, alongside scattered Chinese and Filipino trade families and a Lutheran mission establishment that left behind not a population but a language.
The horror is culturally specific and worse for it. Moka, the escalating exchange of gifts that once bound rival clans into peace, has been inverted by Rangda's demon court into a tribute system in which the goods exchanged are human flesh and the escalation never stops. Wantok obligation is twisted by Rangda's curses and still functions among the herded. It is the only reason anyone inside survives, and the only reason escape is nearly impossible, because leaving means abandoning your obligations and suffering horrific magical punishment.

*Languages heard here: Tok Pisin, English, German (liturgical).*


#### *New Colombia — “The Star Spangled City”*

The waters took the coasts and the bombs took the rest, but a nation, New Colombia insists, is a Constitution rather than a place, and they have the original locked in a vault beneath the city to prove it. The city stands on the ruins of old Salt Lake City, high enough that the ocean never reached it at all. What arrived instead was rain. The Sundering's decades of superstorms filled the basin until the old salt flat became a swollen inland sea with no outlet to anywhere. New Colombia calls itself a continuation of the United States rather than a successor, and its origin stories claim its founders fled all the way from Washington DC shortly before that city burned, then endured a century as a caravan of patriots.
It prides itself on its military, in which every able-bodied citizen must serve. The aberrant living statues that roam the pits of the Bingham Canyon Mine a few miles beyond the walls contain the second-highest quality green leystone available, and much of it feeds the paratech weapon works. Everyone considers New Colombian aetheric firearms the gold standard, to Johannesburg's endless annoyance. The League finds the "we are the last light of true civilization" mindset as sanctimonious as it is exhausting, and finds the city indispensable anyway.
The culture is a fusion that New Colombia refuses to acknowledge as one. The Deseret settler culture already rooted in the basin brought congregational organization, doctrinal food storage, obsessive record-keeping, temperance, and mass volunteerism, and it was welded to the patriot-caravan myth of the eastern refugees. The result is a civic religion in which the Constitution is a sacred text kept in a mountain vault alongside the genealogies. The Diné (Navajo), Hopi, Ute, Shoshone and Goshute nations, whose high plateau homelands came through the drowning largely intact, are among the best-surviving Indigenous peoples on Earth and a major political fact here rather than a manageable minority, with treaty claims backed by numbers. The Hispanophone and Chicano population is very large and connected overland to the Mexican highlands. Tongan, Samoan and Hawaiian communities were already living here, a thousand miles inland, for reasons of faith and family; they did not flee, they simply woke as the largest surviving Polynesian population on Earth, in a desert, holding the whole remaining inheritance of a drowned ocean. Chinese and Vietnamese lineages and small Appalachian evangelical congregations from the high ridges round out the city.
Universal service means every adult holds a rank in reserve, and rank leaks into civilian life. Genealogy is a devotional practice, which makes New Colombia the best place in the world to trace a bloodline and the worst place to hide one. If you are playing Nightfolk, treat that as a serious problem. Food storage doctrine translates into strategic reserves that are enormous. The sanctimony the League complains about is sincere, which is what makes it unbearable. And the founding scripture has a hole in it exactly ten years wide, which the vault's own records could close or confirm.

*Languages heard here: English, Spanish, Tongan, Samoan, Hawaiian, Chinese (Simplified), Vietnamese, Portuguese (Brazil).*


#### *Nairobi — “The Hunter's Gate”*

The Rift Valley below Nairobi is now an inland sea. Not an arm of the ocean, which never climbed that high, but a century of superstorm rainfall trapped in a trench with nowhere to drain, and the things that grew in it grew large. Nairobi answered the way Nairobi would and became the aberrant-hunting capital of the world. Every great hunter guild keeps a hall here, the leviathan fisheries land catches that feed the ichor refineries day and night, and half the League's refined leystone fuel passes through its ledgers. It is loud, young, dangerous, and gloriously alive, a boomtown where nobody with a sharp harpoon can die famous. Nightfolk are welcome in the wary way of a city full of professional monster-killers. Prove yourself, and no sudden movements.
Kikuyu highland culture is the numerical and commercial base, but the governing culture is the guildhall: a boomtown meritocracy where lineage buys you nothing and a confirmed kill buys you a great deal. There are no old families in a trade nobody's grandfather practiced. Luo communities come from the lake basin, which sat high enough to survive. Kamba holds the eastern uplands. Maasai and Kalenjin are heavily represented in the tracking and herding trades, Kisii are here in numbers, the Somali commercial quarter is very large and highland-fed, and Indian-Kenyan refinery and finance families run much of the money. Arrivals come overland and steadily from the Rwandan, Ugandan and Ethiopian highlands.
Harambee, pull-together mutual aid, is the legal basis of guild bond-pools: a hunter's debts and their widow's pension come from the same pot. Sheng, the Swahili-English street creole, is the working cant of the guildhalls and mutates fast enough that last decade's slang marks you as an outsider. Nyama choma after a hunt is close to sacramental. The leviathan hunt is entirely Nairobi's own invention, because the Swahili coast and its thousand-year seafaring tradition drowned with its people. The fisheries were worked out from nothing, on a sea that did not exist a century before, by men who learned it by dying at it. The wariness toward Nightfolk is professional rather than bigoted, and the distinction matters enormously to locals.

*Languages heard here: Swahili, English, Luo, Somali, Oromo, Amharic, Kinyarwanda, Luganda.*


#### *Lhasa — “The High Refuge”*

When China burned in the nuclear flames of the War of Ruination, survivors fled inland and Lhasa took them in. The Potala Palace now seats one of the great Hearthstones, and the monastic orders that tend it also tend the largest archive of salvaged Old World and occult texts in existence. Scholars beg for reading access; most die waiting. It is a contemplative, tightly governed city of prayer-wheels and refugees' descendants, and it is also the subject of the League's most persistent ugly rumor: that one of the undisclosed prisons of the Awakened Censure sits somewhere in the mountains above it. Lhasa does not dignify the question. Many take the stony silence as its own sort of answer.
Classical Tibetan highland Buddhist civilization governs here, Gelug monastic administration fused with the hereditary kudrak aristocratic houses who maintain the Potala's Hearthstone and its vast scripture-vaults. The Han Chinese population is massive, descended from millions of Sichuan basin and central plain refugees who fled the nuclear firestorms of the war, now multi-generational citizens running the secular archive preservation and the paratech scriptoria. Hui Muslim merchant lineages, Ladakhi, Bhutanese and Sikkimese high-pass trading families, Uyghur and Kazakh caravan masters, and Qiang and Yi mountain communities from the eastern plateau margins fill out the rest.
Access is the city's entire economy. Reading rights to the archive are granted, sold, inherited, and litigated, and "most die waiting" is a policy rather than an accident. The refuge memory cuts both ways and everyone remembers precisely: the Chinese lineages remember being taken in by people whose plateau they had once governed. The city's flat silence on the Censure prisons is itself a Lhasa cultural form, because the refusal to dignify a question is a recognized rhetorical move here.

*Languages heard here: Tibetan, Chinese (Simplified), English, Uyghur, Nepali, Dzongkha.*


#### *Salamanca — “The Holy City”*

Iberia drowned, but the old golden city on the meseta kept its spires dry, and the survivors of a hundred flooded cities look to it. The new Vatican rose from Salamanca's ancient churches, convents, and university halls, and the city is now the beating heart of the largest surviving faith on Earth. Pilgrim roads converge on it from three continents, its exorcists are requested by name across the League, and its theological archives rival Lhasa's, a comparison both cities pretend not to make. In this theocratic city-state sorcery is highly restricted, illegal outside of the church-sanctioned and church-trained Teúrgo, who insist their powers are holy and come from God.
It is also the stronghold of the Society of Leopold, and therein lies the chill. Registered Nightfolk are technically legal in Salamanca, as the Pax Nocturnorum demands. They are also watched every hour they remain, and they are not given a chance to explain themselves before their heads go flying. Nothing is ever said. Nothing needs to be. The Holy City forgives sinners and punishes their sins with extreme prejudice.
The culture is Castilian meseta Catholicism, restructured around the Holy See and the cofradía, the penitential brotherhood. That is the fact to understand about Salamanca, because the Society of Leopold's local chapters are structured as cofradías, with hooded processional ranks, inherited membership, and centuries of practice at collective action that is never officially anyone's decision. That the old golden university town inherited primacy over Iberia is an accident of altitude; the imperial capital on the Manzanares lies under the water by a matter of metres. Andalusian families come from Granada and the sierras. Pyrenean Catalans out of the high valleys and Andorra, and Pyrenean Basques in a small and intensely cohesive quarter, are both peoples reduced to their mountain remnant. Italian curial families come from the Apennine and Alpine highlands, Carpathian Romanian labor lineages and Portuguese from the Serra da Estrela fill the working quarters, and a long-established Amazigh community out of the Atlas and the Rif is what remains of a whole world, because the mountains of North Africa survived where its great coastal cities did not. Their orthodoxy is never quite considered settled.
The university and the Church are the same institution wearing two hats, and the Teúrgo are its magical faculty: credentialed, tonsured, and answerable. Holy Week processions are the year's organizing event and a display of exactly which brotherhood commands what. Latin is a living administrative language here. Nothing is ever said aloud, because the culture runs on the understanding, which is why the advisory below matters as much as it does.

*Languages heard here: Spanish, Latin, English, Italian, Catalan, Basque, Tamazight, Romanian, Portuguese (Portugal), Arabic.*

##### ⚠ Traveler's Advisory: Salamanca

***If you work magic:** Sorcery is illegal in Salamanca unless practiced by the church-sanctioned **Teúrgo**. There is no visitor's exemption, no professional courtesy, and no "it's legal where I'm from." Guild papers from Bengaluru, an Academy diploma from New Tenochtitlan, a Witches' Market stall license from La Paz — none of it means anything on the meseta. Practicing unsanctioned sorcery is prosecuted as heresy, and the trial is not the part of the process you should be worried about.*

***If you're Nightfolk:** Your BRD registration is valid in Salamanca, but in the most technical of senses. The Society of Leopold is strong here, and the local chapter of the Order of Vigiles is widely rumored to be in the church's pocket. Writs of execution in Salamanca are suspected of being issued posthumously and having all the investigation conformations of acceptable use of force rubber stamped, though the League has never managed to make an inquiry stick. If a Leopoldine takes an interest in you, the paperwork will be in order by the time anyone asks what happened to you.*

***The practical advice**, as any caravan master will give it: transact your business, keep your gifts sheathed, sleep outside the walls if you can, and be on the pilgrim road by week's end. Salamanca is genuinely safe, hospitable, and orderly — for humans who neither work wonders nor keep company with those who do.*

#### *Sana'a — “The Star-Chandlery”*

When Arabia drowned, the Yemeni highlands became an island, and Sana'a's ancient mud-brick towers, already thousands of years old, now crown it. The city rebuilt the incense trade for a new age. Its groves supply the resins and ritual materials that half the world's warding-craft burns, carried by the famous Incense Fleets. But its true fame is the Star-Chandlers, the navigator-astrologer guild whose charts predict, with unsettling accuracy, where the Wandering Isle will surface next. How they do it is Sana'a's best-kept secret, and every faction on Purgatory's Council has tried to buy it, trade for it, or steal it.
Zaydi highland Yemeni culture runs the city: tribal confederation politics, sheikhly arbitration, and the vertical extended-family life of the tower-house, where a lineage occupies a building floor by floor and generation by generation. Hadhrami families from the surviving plateau wadis rebuilt the city's commercial reach. The Malay-world communities of the Incense Fleet trade, Sundanese, Minangkabau, Batak and Javanese highlanders whose uplands survived as Yemen's did, were reconnected by ship two centuries after the sea lanes died. Soqotri islanders from the high Haghier massif keep a biosphere as strange as Antananarivo's. Somali and Ethiopian highland communities live across the water, and an ancient Yemeni Jewish remnant of the northern highlands is central to the silversmith and star-chart trades.
The mafraj, the top-floor sitting room with its qat, coffee, and hours of talk, is where every real decision in Sana'a is made, and no outsider gets business done without being invited up. Incense is measured ritual rather than ambience; what is burning tells you what kind of meeting this is. The Incense Fleets are a reconstruction rather than an inheritance. Sana'a knew a network had existed and spent two centuries sailing out to find the other end of it, which tells you everything about the city's temperament. The Star-Chandlers guard their method as tribal patrimony rather than trade secret, which is why buying it has always failed. You cannot purchase a lineage.

*Languages heard here: Arabic, English, Hebrew, Malay, Indonesian, Somali, Amharic.*


#### *Tabriz — “The Bazaar of the Third Era”*

Everything is for sale in Tabriz, and always has been. Its Grand Bazaar was old before the Old World fell, and today its covered miles are the largest marketplace on Earth, where League currency, Basilica futures, caravan shares, and quieter merchandise change hands under one roof. Its signature craft is glyph-weaving, enchantment worked into textile rather than etched into leystone, and a Tabrizi carpet that keeps a room warm, silent, or unnoticed costs more than a house.
The city is Azerbaijani Turkic and was always an Azeri city, and the asnaf guild structure of the Bazaar is the central institution of civic life. The guilds license, arbitrate, insure, and discipline, and the League's caravan brokerage runs on their seals. The Iranian plateau lost only its shorelines, which makes this the most intact large civilization of the Old World, and the Bazaar's survival a matter of continuity rather than revival. Persian administrative and scholarly families come from the surviving plateau cities. Kurdish highland communities are here in both great dialect traditions. Armenian merchant houses hold unbroken pre-collapse ledgers worth more than gold. Anatolian Turkish caravaneers come off the high steppe, Hazara, Tajik and Pashtun traders out of Kabul and the Hazarajat, whose mountains came through unusually well, and Baluchi caravan clans from Quetta and Kalat, alongside small Assyrian and Talysh communities.
Ta'arof, the ritualized courtesy of offer and refusal, is the actual negotiation, and foreigners who cut straight to the price lose money without understanding why. Carpet-weaving and glyph-weaving are the same craft lineage, and a master weaver's mark is a legal signature. Nowruz resets the commercial year, and debts that survive it are considered blood matters. The chaikhana is where deals are overheard on purpose. Tabriz sits at the hinge of the only unbroken overland route left on Earth, and prices accordingly.

*Languages heard here: Persian, Azerbaijani, Kurdish (Kurmanji), Kurdish (Sorani), Armenian, English, Turkish, Dari, Pashto, Baluchi.*


#### *Ulaanbaatar — “The City That Chose to Stop”*

The steppe clans wandered through the whole Itinerant Period and were better at it than anyone, and when they finally raised a Hearthstone they did it on their own terms. Ulaanbaatar is the caravan capital of the world. Its Great Caravanserai outfits, insures, and blesses the overland trade of three continents, and its stormchaser fleets ride toward wyrdstorms to harvest fresh leystone from the twisted ground behind them, which is the most profitable insane profession in the Third Era. Half the city still lives in gers by choice and empties onto the steppe each season. Hospitality is sacred, cowardice is not, and every deal is sealed on horseback at least ceremonially.
Khalkha Mongol steppe culture is retained deliberately rather than by default. No part of the Mongolian homeland lay below the water, and a people who needed no fixed infrastructure to survive the Itinerant Period drew the obvious conclusion: the ger and the herd are not a phase you graduate from. Kazakh communities of the western mountains and the southern steppe brought eagle-hunting and now dominate the stormchaser scout trade. Kyrgyz families come from a country that survived nearly whole, Inner Mongolian herders from the surviving northern plateau, and Uyghur caravaneers from further west. Korean labor-migrant lineages were joined by Taebaek highlanders arriving overland, and small, proud Buryat, Tuvan and Russian mountain remnants out of the Altai and Sayan are all that is left of the north, whose cities lay low and lie deep.
Guest-law is absolute and enforceable. A door is never barred, food is never refused, and violating hospitality is the one crime the city will pursue across a continent. Naadam, the wrestling, archery, and horse racing, is the annual reallocation of status, and stormchaser crews recruit from its results. Ovoo cairns are circled three times before any departure, and Tengri sky-reverence coexists with Gelug Buddhism without either side finding this odd. Half the city empties onto the steppe each season, and the Basilica is built to be half-empty. Mongolians are among the very few people who lost nothing, and they know it, which makes them either the most generous or the most quietly superior people in the League, depending on whether you are a guest.

*Languages heard here: Mongolian, Kazakh, Kyrgyz, English, Korean, Buryat, Tuvan, Russian, Uyghur.*


## How The World Works Now

**TL;DR:** *Old World technologies no longer work reliably: reality is too unstable. Modern life runs on **Paratech** (complex, interlocking enchantments that mimic technology), anchored by city-core **Hearthstones** that hold back the storms and the monsters.*

### **Paratech (Paracausal Technology)**

Old World technology (“scitech”) is too fragile for the third era: It was built under the presumption of a reality with static rules and goes haywire the moment a wyrdstorm nears. **Paratech** is really the sorcerous art of enchanting, elevated into a standardized practice by *paratech engineers.* It draws on a settlement’s Heartstone energy field and uses carefully etched **leystone** crystals interlinked by pathways of silver and gold to route energy through layered enchantments in a dependable and autonomous fashion— The end result is reproducing the *effect* of lightbulbs, microwaves, and more, if not their old inner mechanisms.

### **Leystone**

Leystone is the Oil of the third era; the one resource no one ever has enough of, yet their entire civilization runs on it. Vital as both the foundational material that Paratech is built from and a fuel to power lesser Hearthstones, its acquisition is one of the key topics that drive political calculus of the era. It is extracted from two primary sources: It forms in large seams from natural rock twisted by a Wyrdstorm, and can be alchemically refined from the ichor of Aberrants. The higher its quality, the clearer it is; exceedingly high quality leystone is akin to looking through lightly glowing colored glass.

There are three known types of Leystone:

#### *Green Leystone*

Prized by paracausal engineers, it is the only known stable substance able to hold multiple active enchantments at once, making it the workhorse material for etching the complex glyph formulas that make paratech work.

#### *Blue/Purple Leystone*

Exceedingly aether dense, its innate unstable nature makes it valuable as a fuel (and a material to make bombs). Common fuelstock is blue, but rarely a purple variant shows up which contains twice the energy (and also goes for about twice the usual rate).

#### *Red Leystone*

Found only on ruby beach of the Wandering Isle, it is to locals as “Heartglass”. Red Leystone contains, pound-for-pound ten to twelve times the energy of even high quality Purple variety, making it incredibly sought after as the prized (and restricted) export of the Wandering Isle. However, unlike other types of Leystone, it is known to have a strange will of its own that seeks to merge with living things; attempts to make paratech with it have resulted in erratic creations seemingly with a mind of their own.

### **Hearthstones**

Hearthstones are the beating heart of every permanent settlement the world over. They moderate weather, power the paratech grid, and: most importantly: anchor the **defensive barriers** that deflect wyrdstorms and repel aberrant beasts. 

There are three tiers:

| Tier | Protects | Powered By |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Minor** | A village | Natural leyline flow |
| **Lesser** | A small city | Leystone-fueled generators |
| **Greater** | A metropolis (Basilica) | A **quintessence reactor**\* |

\*Greater Hearthstones require truly staggering amounts of aether: enough that they run on immense subterranean **quintessence reactors**. Everyone knows the reactors exist; almost no one outside a rarefied few knows exactly *how* they work.

## Living in the Shadows

**TL;DR:** *Nightfolk are legally allowed inside the Basilicas and their satellite settlements if they register and behave. Register as a **BRD** (Benign Reality Deviant), obey the **Pax Eldritus**, and you’re treated as a citizen. Prey on humans (or get caught acting too inhuman) and a **writ of execution** goes to the Hunters. Discretion is survival.*

**The Masquerade’s End.** The Second Masquerade lasted only \~30 years before the Shadow Directorate suddenly decided to reveal the existence of the Nightfolk living among humanity. The result was predictable: fear, riots, and lynchings: with *far more humans* dying in the “monster hunts”than actual Nightfolk: until the Basilicas cracked down.

Today, safety depends on *downplaying* the danger within the walls. So long as mankind can be convinced the real threat is *out there* in the trackless wilds: and that sorcerers and aberrant-hunters keep the walls safe: people don’t reach for torches and pitchforks over their neighbors.

**Pax Eldritus (Peace of the Eldritch).** The bargain that makes true coexistence possible:

* Nightfolk must register as **BRDs (Benign Reality Deviants).** So long as they aren’t *caught* acting hostile or predatory toward humans, they live with the full rights of any citizen.

* Accusations are investigated by the **Order of Vigiles.** A confirmed breach revokes BRD status and requires issuing a **writ of execution** to the **Aberrant Hunters**, who may then legally kill the offender.

* Protection runs both ways: **unsanctioned** hunts are also investigated by the Vigiles, and rogue hunters “tend to suddenly go missing”.

**The unspoken rule:** *Discretion is the better part of valor.* Hide obviously inhuman features and abilities in unknown company; reveal your nature only to a trusted few (and even then, spin it favorably). BRD status is fragile: even the *perception* of using your inhuman advantages over a normal human can cost it. Easiest, always, to simply seem human from the start.

**On the Imbued.** The Imbued are humans who have undergone the Harrowing — died to an aberrant and returned, bound to an unknown Edict that drives them to hunt and destroy the supernatural. Most have lost someone to one. Some curdle into militant zealots who see every Supernatural as a tragedy waiting to happen; others overcome the pain and become true guardians, working so that no one else suffers as they did.

## The Splats: An Overview

Full character creation information for each is in the [Character Creation Guide](/character-creation/).

**Vampire (Lilum).** When Caine walked into Oblivion, Lilith intervened and gave every vampire a choice. Those who accepted rebirth through her covenant became the Lilum — still vampiric in nature, but tied to the Dark Mother rather than the First Murderer. The old Camarilla, Sabbat, and Anarch exist only in historical accounts; in modern nights, Vampires organize through the Factions. The six common bloodlines are Ventrue, Toreador, Malkavian, Gangrel, Nosferatu, and Brujah.

**Werewolf (Garou).** When Gaia the Elder died, every Garou not sheltered in a spiritual realm was erased from existence. Eshtarra's ascension as Gaia the Younger remade them as the Gaian Shifters — servants bound to cleanse the world's spiritual contamination. They carry no silver bane; their bane is obsidian. The six surviving tribes are Black Furies, Fianna, Get of Fenris, Glass Walkers, Silent Striders, and Children of Eshtarra.

**Lost.** The Great Wyrdstorm cracked the gates to Arcadia and the Dreaming open. What remains are three distinct peoples: the Lost (playable), the Dreamborne (rare, playable), and the Wyrd-born (Storyteller-controlled). The six seemings are Beasts, Darklings, Elementals, Fairest, Ogres, and Wizened.

**Hungry Dead** *(Rare splat — Storyteller-share only).* Those who have died, endured the Thousand Hells, and returned changed. Not vampires, though both are undead. They walk a Dharma path and manage the balance between their Ego and their Shadow.

**Sorcerers.** Awakened magic died with the Mages. Sorcerers survived because their magic was always smaller in scale and more rigid — a "paint by numbers" practice that bureaucracy could legislate. The six schools are Artificers, Augurs, Druids, Ossians, Wayfarers, and Fury Shapers.

**Psychics.** Psychic ability can emerge in any human born inside the Basilica — and it is a liability, not a gift, since the general population mistakes it for Awakened magic. Every Psychic learns to mask their ability as Sorcerer-style practice.

**Humans.** The majority of Basilica Purgatory. They run the city, staff its institutions, and outnumber every supernatural type combined. Human characters choose a Profession.

**Imbued.** Humans who died to an aberrant and returned through the Harrowing, bound to an Edict none of them chose and none can explain. They don't hunt supernaturals purely to destroy them — each Calling (Hunt, Hold, Witness, Weigh, Mend, Warn) answers a different mandate, and together they exist to maintain balance and keep the Veil intact.
